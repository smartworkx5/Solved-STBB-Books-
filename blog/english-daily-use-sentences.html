Daily Use English Sentences for Students

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 1663 words · 8 min read

Fifty sentences can change a student's English life, because daily-use sentences for school, home, the market, friends and travel turn English from a textbook subject into a language you actually speak every day. This guide groups fifty useful sentences by place, explains when to use each one, and shows how to practise them in real situations without fear. Learn five sentences at a time, speak them aloud, use one in a real situation each day, and revise them through the [solved notes](/solved.html) and [books](/books.html) pages, because sentences practised in life are remembered far longer than sentences only read once.

Classroom Sentences

Classroom sentences are the first English you speak every day, so learn them until they come naturally, and practise them with classmates because real speaking builds confidence faster than silent reading, as shown in the speaking guides on blog. 'Good morning, teacher.' Say this when you enter the classroom in the morning with respect and a smile.. 'May I come in, please?' Use this polite sentence when you arrive after the class has already started.. 'I have completed my homework.' Say this when the teacher asks about yesterday's written work.. 'Please explain this again.' Use this when a lesson point is still unclear after the first explanation.. 'I did not understand this question.' Say this honestly so the teacher can help you before the test..

More classroom sentences help you ask for help, borrow books and take part in lessons, and the more you use them, the less you will need to translate from your mother tongue before speaking in class. 'May I go to the library?' Use this when you need a book during a free period.. 'Today we have a test.' Say this to remind a friend about an important class test.. 'Please write it on the board.' Use this when you cannot hear or read a word clearly.. 'I need help with this sum.' Say this to a teacher or friend when a mathematics problem is difficult.. 'Thank you for teaching us.' Say this at the end of a good lesson to show gratitude..

Home Sentences

Home sentences let you practise English with your family in a safe, friendly place, and because family members forgive mistakes, home is the best place to try new sentences without fear of being laughed at. 'I have finished my breakfast.' Say this when you are ready to leave for school in the morning.. 'Please pass me the water.' Use this polite request at the dining table during meals.. 'I will help mother in the kitchen.' Say this to offer help with cooking or cleaning work.. 'Let us clean the room together.' Use this to invite a brother or sister to tidy a shared room.. 'I am going to study now.' Say this to tell your family you need quiet time for lessons..

Evening sentences at home are equally useful, because they cover dinner, rest and preparation for the next school day, and using them daily turns English from a school subject into a living language. 'Please switch off the light.' Use this when leaving a room that no one is using.. 'Dinner is ready.' Say this to call your family to the table for the evening meal.. 'I will sleep early tonight.' Use this when you plan to rest well before a test day.. 'Please wake me at six.' Say this to a parent when you need help waking up early.. 'I have washed my clothes.' Use this to report a completed home task proudly..

Market and Shopping Sentences

Market sentences help you buy things, ask prices and speak politely to shopkeepers, and they are useful for every student because markets are part of weekly family life in every town and village. 'How much does this cost?' Use this to ask the price of anything before buying it.. 'Please show me another one.' Say this when the first item does not suit your need.. 'This is too expensive.' Use this politely when the price is higher than you expected.. 'Please give me one kilogram.' Say this when buying vegetables, fruit or other weighed goods.. 'I need a notebook and a pencil.' Use this clear sentence when buying school stationery..

Polite shopping sentences also teach bargaining manners, because a calm request and a thank you leave a better impression than a loud argument, and shopkeepers remember polite young customers with kindness. 'Do you have a smaller size?' Use this when clothes or shoes feel too large for you.. 'Please pack it carefully.' Say this for fragile items that can break on the way home.. 'I will pay in cash.' Use this to tell the shopkeeper your payment method clearly.. 'Thank you, keep the change.' Say this when you do not need small coins back.. 'Where is the nearest shop?' Use this when you are new to an area and need directions..

Friends and Travel Sentences

Friend and travel sentences help you walk to school, catch a bus and meet people kindly, and they build the social English that textbooks alone cannot teach, because real journeys need real spoken sentences. 'Let us walk to school together.' Say this to invite a friend to share the morning journey.. 'Please wait for me.' Use this when a friend is walking faster than you.. 'Which bus goes to the city?' Ask this at a bus stop when you are unsure of the route.. 'I missed my bus today.' Say this to explain why you arrived late at school.. 'Please tell me the way to the library.' Use this polite request when asking a stranger for directions..

Friendly closing sentences matter too, because they end meetings warmly and promise the next meeting, and students who use them sound polite and confident in every social situation they meet. 'Let us play after school.' Say this to plan games with friends after homework.. 'I will meet you tomorrow.' Use this to confirm a plan for the next day.. 'Please speak slowly.' Say this when someone speaks too fast for you to understand.. 'I am feeling happy today.' Use this to share a good feeling honestly with a friend.. 'See you tomorrow.' Say this as a warm goodbye at the end of the school day..

Polite Sentences for Every Day

Polite sentences are the most powerful sentences in English, because excuse me, sorry and thank you open doors in school, at home and in the market, and the solved notes speaking guides stress manners as part of good language. 'Excuse me, please.' Use this before asking a question or passing through a crowd.. 'I am sorry for the mistake.' Say this honestly when you have done something wrong.. 'Thank you very much.' Use this to show gratitude for help, gifts or teaching.. 'You are welcome.' Say this as a kind reply when someone thanks you.. 'Please forgive me.' Use this when you want someone to pardon a fault..

Finish by learning five new sentences each week, using one every day in a real situation, and writing them in a small notebook, because sentences used in real life stay in memory far longer than sentences only read from a page. 'May I help you?' Say this to offer help to a teacher, elder or friend.. 'That is very kind of you.' Use this to praise someone's generous and thoughtful action.. 'I agree with you.' Say this when you share someone's opinion in a discussion.. 'Please take care.' Use this as a caring goodbye to friends and family members.. 'Have a nice day.' Say this warmly to wish someone a pleasant and happy day..

5 key points you must know

  • Learn sentences by place: school, home, market, travel.
  • Five new sentences per week is a strong, steady pace.
  • Speak each sentence aloud before writing it down.
  • Use one sentence in a real situation every day.
  • Polite sentences matter as much as correct grammar.

Frequently asked questions

How many daily-use sentences should I learn each week?

Five new sentences per week is a strong target, because it gives you time to speak each sentence aloud, use it in a real situation, and revise it, while a larger list usually leads to fast forgetting and little real speaking practice. Keep this answer beside your lesson notes, and read it once more before the next test so the idea stays fresh and ready.

Should I memorise sentences or understand them?

Understand each sentence first, then practise it until it comes naturally, because understanding lets you change a sentence for new situations, while pure memorising breaks down as soon as the situation changes even slightly. Use the idea in one real sentence or task today, because using knowledge turns it from a fact into a skill you own.

What if I make a mistake while speaking?

Smile, correct yourself calmly, and continue speaking, because mistakes are a normal part of learning any language, and teachers and friends respect a student who tries bravely far more than one who stays silent from fear. Revise older answers together with new ones, so new learning connects with what you already know instead of replacing it.

How can I practise speaking if no one speaks English at home?

Speak aloud to yourself, read sentences to a mirror, record your voice and listen back, and use sentences with classmates at school, because regular solo practice still builds fluency, pronunciation and confidence over time. Stay patient with slow progress, because steady small steps in English build more lasting skill than rushed cramming before tests.

Are these sentences enough for daily conversation?

These fifty sentences cover the most common daily situations for students, and once you use them confidently, you can add new sentences for new situations, because a strong base of fifty makes every next sentence easier to learn. End each study session by recalling the main point aloud without looking, since recall practice strengthens memory far more than re-reading.
