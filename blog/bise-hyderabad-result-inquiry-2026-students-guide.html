BISE Hyderabad Result Inquiry 2026: What Students Need to Know

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-29 · 1755 words · 9 min read

On 23 October 2026, the Sindh government suspended the Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education Hyderabad's Deputy Controller of the Top Secret Branch and formed a three-member inquiry committee to examine alleged irregularities in the Class XII annual examination 2026 results. The story has since travelled fast on social media, and with it a great deal of speculation that goes far beyond what is actually known. This guide sets out the confirmed facts, explains what an inquiry of this kind can and cannot do, and tells students plainly what they should and should not do while it runs. The most important point comes first: the allegations are allegations, not proven findings, the announced results have not been cancelled, and no student has been named as a beneficiary of manipulation.

What Happened on 23 October 2026

The confirmed sequence is as follows. The Sindh government suspended BISE Hyderabad Deputy Controller (Top Secret Branch) Ijaz Ali Kaka — spelled Ejaz Ali Kaka in some reports — on the orders of Provincial Minister for Universities and Boards Muhammad Ismail Rahoo. At the same time, a three-member inquiry committee was formed through a notification issued by Universities Secretary Abbas Baloch. A suspension in these circumstances is an administrative step taken while an inquiry runs; it is not itself a finding of guilt, and students should not treat it as one. Officials are suspended pending inquiry precisely so that the examination of records can proceed without interference, and the outcome depends on what the committee finds.

The inquiry's scope, as reported, covers alleged irregularities in the Class XII (Intermediate Part-II) annual examination 2026 results, specifically in the pre-medical and pre-engineering groups. The committee is examining position holders, top-10 candidates, and students with A-1 and A grades, and it is looking at the checking, tabulation, and announcement processes behind the results. The allegations themselves circulated through social media and public complaints; that is how they reached the authorities, but circulation is not proof. Until the committee reports, every claim about what happened in the marking or tabulation process remains an allegation, and responsible coverage must keep saying so.

Who Is on the Committee and When It Reports

The committee is headed by Professor Dr Arbeela Bhutto, Vice Chancellor of the Sindh Agriculture and Business University (SIBA Jamshoro). Its members are Additional Secretary Amir Fazal Owaisi — spelled Ameer Fazal Owaisi in some reports — and Mehran University Registrar Dr Nasrullah Pirzada. The composition matters because it places the inquiry in the hands of senior academics and administrators from outside the board under inquiry, which is the normal way to give such a process credibility. Students do not need to follow the committee's internal workings, but knowing who is responsible helps distinguish genuine updates from rumours that invent new names or new findings.

The committee's report is due within 30 days of 23 October 2026, which this guide describes only as within 30 days rather than as a confirmed calendar date. That window means students should expect news during November, but they should not treat any particular day as fixed, and they should not trust social media posts that claim the report has already been delivered before any official announcement. If the committee needs longer, or if its findings lead to further steps, those developments will come through official channels, not through forwarded messages. Patience during this window is not passivity; it is the sensible course while a formal process runs. For general board guidance, see the blog and the [home page](/).

What the Inquiry Does Not Mean

Three misunderstandings need correcting clearly. First, the announced results have not been cancelled. Reports confirm that the results stand as announced while the inquiry proceeds, so a student holding a Class XII mark sheet still holds a valid result unless and until an official decision changes it. Second, the inquiry does not mean every position holder or A-1 grade student did something wrong; the committee is examining those categories because that is where irregularities, if any, would show up, not because every student in them is suspect. Third, no student has been named in the published reporting as a beneficiary of manipulation, and this guide names nobody; any social media post that names a student in that connection is unverified and potentially defamatory, and sharing it helps nobody.

It is also worth understanding what an inquiry can actually establish. A committee examining checking, tabulation, and announcement processes can find procedural errors, inconsistencies in records, or evidence of wrongdoing, and it can recommend action, but it does not itself rewrite results on the spot. Any change to an individual student's result, if one were ever recommended, would follow a formal decision by the competent authority, with its own process. Students should therefore ignore claims that results have already been changed, that particular grades have been withdrawn, or that a new merit list exists, unless such a claim comes from the board or the Universities and Boards Department itself.

What Students Should Do Now

The practical advice for Hyderabad board students is short and calm. Keep your official transcript and mark sheet safe, together with your admit card and any result documents your college issued, because these are the records any future process would refer to. If you are a Class XII student who believes there is a genuine discrepancy in your own mark sheet — for example, marks that do not match your answer sheet as you remember it — use the board's formal rechecking route rather than joining public speculation. Rechecking deals with your own paper through a defined process; it does not depend on the inquiry, and it does not require you to prove anything about other students.

Students applying for further study should proceed with their applications using the result they hold, since it has not been cancelled. Colleges and universities deal with board results as announced, and an inquiry in progress does not suspend admissions based on those results unless an authority formally says so. If an institution asks about the inquiry, the accurate answer is that a committee is examining allegations and is due to report within 30 days of 23 October 2026, and that no findings have been published yet. That answer is honest, it protects you from repeating rumours, and it leaves you free to focus on your studies rather than on a process you cannot control. How to Spot Rumours About the Inquiry

Inquiry stories attract rumours because they combine official action with unanswered questions, and students need a simple test for what they read. Ask three questions of any claim: does it name an official source, such as the Universities and Boards Department or the board itself; does it distinguish between an allegation and a finding; and does it name a student or a grade change without evidence? If the answer to the first is no, or if the claim presents allegations as proven facts or names a student, treat it as unverified and do not forward it. Forwarding an unverified claim about a named student can cause real harm to a young person who may have done nothing wrong, and it can also expose the forwarder to trouble.

The same test applies to screenshots of supposed official letters. A genuine notification will be traceable to the issuing authority and will usually be reported by established news outlets as well as shared on social media; a screenshot circulating alone, with no news report and no official posting behind it, should be treated with suspicion. During the inquiry window, the safest information habit is to check one or two established news sources and the board's own communications, and to ignore the rest. When the committee's report is genuinely published, it will be news everywhere at once; you will not miss it by ignoring the rumours, but you may be misled badly by believing them.

5 key points you must know

  • On 23 October 2026, BISE Hyderabad's Deputy Controller (Top Secret Branch) was suspended and a three-member inquiry committee was formed.
  • The inquiry covers alleged irregularities in Class XII 2026 pre-medical and pre-engineering results.
  • Allegations are not proven findings; the committee's report is due within 30 days of 23 October.

Frequently asked questions

Has the BISE Hyderabad Class XII result been cancelled?

No. The announced results have not been cancelled. The inquiry is examining allegations about the results, but until an official decision follows the committee's report, the results stand as announced.

Who is heading the inquiry committee?

Professor Dr Arbeela Bhutto, Vice Chancellor of the Sindh Agriculture and Business University (SIBA Jamshoro), heads the committee. The members are Additional Secretary Amir Fazal Owaisi and Mehran University Registrar Dr Nasrullah Pirzada.

When will the inquiry report be published?

The report is due within 30 days of 23 October 2026. This guide does not claim a confirmed calendar date; watch official announcements rather than social media claims about a delivery date.

Does the inquiry mean position holders cheated?

No. The committee is examining position holders, top-10 candidates and A-1/A grade students because that is where irregularities would show up, not because every student in those categories is suspect. The allegations remain unproven.

What should I do if I suspect an error in my own mark sheet?
