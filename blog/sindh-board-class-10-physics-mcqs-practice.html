Sindh Board Class 10 Physics MCQs — Practice with Answers

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 811 words · 4 min read

474Subject Guides

The objective section of the Sindh Board Class 10 Physics paper is decided by small facts: one formula, one unit, one definition. Students who practise MCQs unit by unit collect these marks easily, while students who only read theory lose them. This guide shows how to practise Class 10 Physics MCQs the right way, using the units of the Sindh Textbook Board book.

The units your MCQs come from

The Sindh Textbook Board Class 10 Physics book covers Simple Harmonic Motion and Waves, Sound, Geometrical Optics, Electrostatics, Current Electricity, Electromagnetism, Basic Electronics, and Atomic and Nuclear Physics. Every unit contributes MCQs, and the questions stay close to the textbook: definitions, SI units, formula relations, and the results of the book's worked examples.

Before practising a unit's MCQs, read that unit's solved exercises on the Class 10 Physics book page. The solved numericals teach you the formulas in use, and the short questions teach you the facts — the MCQs then test both together.

How to practise: unit by unit, never mixed at first

Practise one unit at a time on the MCQs practice page. Mixed practice is for the final week; unit-wise practice is how you find your weak unit. After each set, read the explanation given under every answer. The explanation is the real lesson — it tells you why the correct option is correct and why the tempting wrong option is wrong.

Keep a formula sheet for each unit as you practise: wave equation and frequency relations for waves, the lens and mirror relations for optics, Ohm's law and resistance relations for current electricity. When an MCQ needs a formula you cannot recall, that formula goes on the sheet in your own handwriting.

The four traps in Physics MCQs

Trap one: units. Options often differ only in unit — hertz vs seconds for frequency and period. Convert before you choose. Trap two: direction and sign in optics and electricity questions, where the correct fact is stated in reverse. Trap three: similar terms — transverse vs longitudinal waves, series vs parallel resistance, fission vs fusion. Trap four: formula rearrangements, where the MCQ gives the value of a different quantity than the one you practised.

The defence against all four traps is the same: read all four options before choosing, and say the reason for your choice in one silent sentence. If you cannot state a reason, mark the question and return to it after the set.

Pair MCQ practice with numerical practice

Many Physics MCQs are one-step numericals. If you can solve the unit's written numericals, these MCQs take seconds. Alternate your study sessions: one session of solved numericals from the book page, one session of MCQs from the same unit. The two formats strengthen each other.

In the last two weeks before the exam, switch to timed mixed sets across all units. Note which units still produce wrong answers and give those units one more round of solved exercises. That targeted round is worth more than re-reading the whole book.

5 key points you must know

  • Practise MCQs unit by unit first: waves, sound, optics, electrostatics, current electricity, electromagnetism, electronics, atomic and nuclear physics. Mixed sets come only in the final week.
  • Read the explanation under every answer, right or wrong. The explanation teaches the fact the next MCQ will test.
  • Maintain a handwritten formula sheet per unit. Any formula you fail to recall during practice goes onto the sheet the same day.
  • Watch the units in the options: frequency in hertz, period in seconds, resistance in ohms, power in watts. Half of Physics MCQ traps are unit traps.
  • Alternate MCQ sessions with solved-numeral sessions from the same unit — one-step numerical MCQs become easy once the written method is clear.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I practise Sindh Board Class 10 Physics MCQs with answers?

On the MCQs practice page of this website. Choose the Class 10 Physics book and practise unit by unit; every MCQ shows its correct answer with an explanation.

How many MCQs should I practise daily for Physics?

One unit's set per study session is a good pace. Quality matters more than quantity: read every explanation and write down each fact you missed. Two focused units a day cover the whole book in a week.

Do board MCQs come from the textbook?

Yes, board objective questions are set from the textbook's own lines — definitions, units, laws and worked examples. That is why practising after reading the unit's solved exercises on the book page works so well.

What is the best way to revise Physics formulas?

Keep one formula sheet per unit and test yourself from it: cover the formula, say it, check it. Formulas you can recall under that test are the ones you will recall in the exam hall.
