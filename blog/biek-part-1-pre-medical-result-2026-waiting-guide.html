BIEK Part-I Pre-Medical Result 2026: Still Waiting? What Students Should Do Now

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-25 · 1718 words · 9 min read

If you are a Karachi Pre-Medical student waiting for your BIEK Part-I result, here is the honest position as of mid-October 2026: the result has not yet been announced, and no official date has been published by the board. That plain statement matters, because waiting students are surrounded by rumoured dates, forwarded messages and websites that present guesses as announcements. BIEK releases its groups separately rather than on one single result day, so the Pre-Engineering announcement on 2 October 2026 tells you nothing about when Pre-Medical will follow. This guide explains what is actually known, why rumour dates should be ignored, how BIEK's separate group releases work, and what you can productively do while you wait, including starting 12th class preparation and getting your documents ready.

What Is Actually Known, and What Is Not

When BIEK's Part-I results were checked on 2 October 2026, the Pre-Engineering group had been announced, but Pre-Medical, Commerce and Humanities Part-I groups were not listed among the 2026 Part-I results. Some result websites in mid-October expected Pre-Medical Part-I around the end of October, but that expectation is unverified and is not a board date, so this article does not present it as one. The only reliable position is that the Pre-Medical Part-I result is not yet announced and no official date has been published, and students should check the board's official information rather than any aggregator's countdown. Treating an unverified expectation as a promise only creates a second disappointment when the guessed day passes without a result.

This uncertainty is frustrating, but it is not unusual for BIEK, which announces each group when its own checking and gazette work is complete. Pre-Medical is a large group with many candidates, and careful checking takes time. Students sometimes assume that a delay means something has gone wrong with their own paper, but group-level timing has nothing to do with individual results. Your paper is checked through the same process whether the announcement comes this week or later, and no amount of refreshing a website will move that process faster. The productive response is to separate what you can control, which is your preparation and your documents, from what you cannot, which is the board's announcement schedule.

How BIEK Releases Groups Separately

BIEK, the Board of Intermediate Education Karachi for Classes 11 and 12, does not publish one combined inter result. Each group, Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, Science General, Humanities, Commerce and Home Economics, is announced on its own date when ready, with its own gazette. The Part-II 2026 pattern shows this clearly: Pre-Medical Part-II came on 31 July, Humanities on 7 August, Pre-Engineering on 17 August, Science General on 27 August and Commerce on 14 September 2026. A student waiting for one group therefore cannot infer their date from another group's announcement, and a website that lists a single BIEK result day is oversimplifying how the board works.

Understanding this pattern also explains why your classmates in different groups may receive results weeks apart, and why that gap means nothing about relative performance. Each group's gazette is prepared separately, college-wise, and students should check their own group's announcement rather than assuming a friend's result day applies to them. When Pre-Medical Part-I is announced, the board's official channels and your college will be the reliable sources, and the gazette will let colleges verify details. Until then, any page claiming an exact Pre-Medical Part-I date without citing an official board notice should be treated as a guess. For background on how other Karachi results were announced, see our guide to BIEK Part-II results by group and the Pre-Engineering ungraded rule.

How to Spot and Ignore Rumour Dates

Rumour dates spread because waiting is uncomfortable and a specific date feels more reassuring than uncertainty, even when the date is invented. Warning signs are easy to recognise: a page that states an exact date but cites no board notice, a video thumbnail shouting a date in capital letters, a forwarded message with no source, or a website that changes its claimed date every few days. Genuine board announcements come through official channels and are then reported consistently by credible outlets, not by a single aggregator page. If you cannot trace a date back to an official board statement, treat it as unverified, however confidently it is written.

Rumours also cost real study time, because students who believe a result is coming tomorrow stop studying, then feel cheated when tomorrow passes. A healthier habit is to check official sources once a day at a fixed time, and otherwise to treat the result as something that will arrive in its own time. Tell your family the same plain facts, so relatives do not forward you every new rumour they encounter. If a friend shares a date, ask politely where the board published it; if there is no answer, you have your answer. Protecting your attention during the waiting period is part of preparing well, because the students who start 12th class work now will be ahead regardless of when the result appears.

A Productive Waiting Plan: Start 12th Class Now

The best use of the waiting weeks is to begin 12th class preparation, since Part-II builds directly on Part-I foundations and early starters gain a lasting advantage. Read ahead in biology, chemistry and physics for a focused hour each day, working through the first chapters of your 12th class textbooks and noting where Part-I concepts reappear. Solve a small number of Part-I style questions each week to keep your foundations sharp, and build short notes on topics you know were weak in your Part-I preparation. This is not wasted effort if your result is good, and it is essential preparation if any paper needs reappearing in, because the same foundations will be tested again.

Structure your waiting days like school days, even if your college has not formally started Part-II teaching. Morning hours suit biology reading and chemistry revision, afternoons suit physics numericals, and evenings suit short notes and next-day planning. Keep a simple diary of what you covered, so you can show your teachers a genuine head start when classes begin. Avoid the trap of doing nothing until the result arrives, because that habit can cost you a month of Part-II progress. Students who arrive at Part-II with notes already made and foundations revised settle faster, ask better questions, and handle the faster Part-II pace with confidence. The solved notes and books page can help you find the right material for this head start.

  • One focused hour of 12th class reading every day: biology, chemistry and physics
  • Weekly Part-I revision so weak foundations are repaired before Part-II pressure builds
  • Short notes on every chapter you read, not just passive reading
  • A daily diary of topics covered, to show teachers and track your own progress

Your Document Checklist Before Result Day

Result day goes smoothly when your documents are already in order, so prepare a single folder now. Keep your Part-I admit card or roll number slip, your college admission record, fee receipts, and any board correspondence together, and note your roll number in a place you can find quickly. When the result is announced, you will need your roll number to check it, and your college may ask for documents during verification or admission to Part-II. If any document is missing, ask your college office about it now, while offices are not crowded with result-day enquiries. Students who scramble for a lost roll number on result day lose time and add stress to an already tense moment.

Also prepare yourself for every possible outcome, not just the one you hope for. If you pass all papers, celebrate briefly and then commit fully to Part-II. If one or two papers need attention, remember the Pre-Engineering experience: thousands of students face papers to clear, and the route forward is reappearance plus steady preparation, not panic. If any detail on your result looks wrong, your college can help you verify it against the gazette and advise on the proper route, which may include scrutiny. Whatever the result, keep your documents, stay in touch with your teachers, and move into Part-II work the same week. The result is a checkpoint, not a destination, and your preparation during this waiting period already counts toward what comes next.

Frequently asked questions

Has the BIEK Part-I Pre-Medical result 2026 been announced?

No. As of mid-October 2026 checks, Pre-Medical Part-I was not listed among BIEK's announced 2026 Part-I results, and no official date has been published. Check the board's official information and your college for the announcement.

Is there an official date for the BIEK Pre-Medical Part-I result?

No official date has been published. Some result sites expected it around the end of October, but that is an unverified expectation, not a board date, and it should not be treated as fact.

Why did Pre-Engineering students get their result earlier?

BIEK announces each group separately when its own checking and gazette work is complete. Pre-Engineering Part-I was announced on 2 October 2026, but each group's timing is independent.

What should I study while waiting for the Pre-Medical result?

Begin 12th class biology, chemistry and physics reading for a focused hour daily, revise weak Part-I foundations weekly, and make short notes. This head start helps whether you pass all papers or need to reappear in any.
