Sindh Board Result Websites: The Real Portals and the Fake Ones to Avoid

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-25 · 1705 words · 9 min read

On result day, the most dangerous website is not the one that fails to load; it is the one that loads confidently with wrong information. Sindh students check results for several different boards, and each board has exactly one official portal, yet guessed domains, lookalike sites and aggregator pages compete for the same anxious clicks. This guide lists the verified portals for Sindh's boards, explains the traps that catch students every year, and gives you a safety checklist to use before you trust any result you see online. The verified portals are BSEK at bsek.edu.pk, BIEK at biek.edu.pk, BISE Sukkur at bisesuksindh.edu.pk, and BISE Larkana at biselrk.edu.pk. Bookmark the real ones now, before result pressure makes a wrong click more likely.

The Verified Portals, Board by Board

Start with the two Karachi boards, because confusing them is the most common Sindh mistake. BSEK, the Board of Secondary Education Karachi, handles matric, Classes 9 and 10, and its verified portal is bsek.edu.pk. BIEK, the Board of Intermediate Education Karachi, handles inter, Classes 11 and 12, and its verified portal is biek.edu.pk. A Class 9 student who searches the inter board, or an inter student who searches the matric board, will not find their result, and may instead land on an aggregator that presents old or guessed data as current. Knowing which board owns your class is the first filter, and your admit card and school will always confirm it if you are unsure.

Beyond Karachi, BISE Sukkur's verified portal is bisesuksindh.edu.pk, and students should note carefully that bisesukkur.edu.pk does not exist as the board's address, despite looking like the obvious guess. BISE Larkana's portal is biselrk.edu.pk, a shorter address than many students expect, which is precisely why guessing fails. These addresses were verified against a tested list of board portals, and they are the addresses this guide recommends. If a website's address differs from these verified portals, even slightly, treat its result information as unverified until you cross-check it officially. Your school can confirm your board's portal, and that confirmation is worth more than any search ranking.

Why Guessed Domains and Aggregators Mislead

Guessed domains mislead because board addresses do not always follow the pattern a student would invent. Sukkur is the clearest example: the natural guess bisesukkur.edu.pk does not exist, while the real address bisesuksindh.edu.pk follows a different pattern. A student who types the guessed address may reach nothing, an unrelated page, or a parked domain, and in the stress of result day that confusion can turn into panic or a click on a further wrong site. Larkana's short address biselrk.edu.pk similarly defeats guessing, and Karachi's two boards defeat anyone who does not distinguish matric from inter. The lesson is simple: never guess a board address, and never trust an address from memory when the verified list is available.

Aggregator sites cause a different problem: they often show real-looking result pages built from old data, press reports or user submissions, without access to the board's official gazette. An aggregator may display a plausible result, a wrong date, or a page that asks for personal details before showing anything, and students have no way to tell which parts are accurate. Some aggregators copy board notices with small changes, some keep last year's pages live with new dates, and some exist mainly to collect clicks and advertising. Even a well-meaning aggregator can be out of date by hours or days, which matters when rechecking windows and admission deadlines are calculated from official dates. The official portal and your school are the only sources that count.

Red Flags: How to Recognise a Fake or Unreliable Result Site

Learn to recognise red flags before result day, so you can spot them under pressure. A site that demands payment to show your result, asks for unnecessary personal details beyond a roll number, or promises to increase your marks for a fee is not a board portal and should be closed immediately. A site that shows a result without asking for your correct roll number, or that shows the same result for different roll numbers, is fabricating data. Pop-up heavy pages, aggressive advertisements, countdown timers claiming a result that the board has not announced, and pages that copy a board's name but use a different domain are all warnings. Genuine board portals are plain, functional and free for result checking, and they do not sell result improvements.

Also beware of social media pages and channels that impersonate boards. A page named after a board is not the board unless it links to the verified portal and is recognised by your school or the board itself. Forwarded screenshots are especially unreliable, because a screenshot can be edited, cropped or taken from a previous year, and it carries no way to verify its source. If a screenshot claims a result or a date, verify it yourself on the official portal before telling anyone else, because forwarding an unverified screenshot spreads confusion to other students. When in doubt, the rule is always the same: check the verified portal, check with your school, and ignore everything else until those two agree.

  • Payment or a fee to view a result is a red flag; genuine checking is free
  • Requests for unnecessary personal details beyond a roll number are suspicious
  • Promises to increase marks for money are always fake
  • Countdowns for results the board has not announced are guesses, not news
  • A board name on a different domain does not make a site official

Your Result-Day Safety Checklist

Use this checklist on every result day, for every board. First, open only the verified portal for your board, from a bookmark you saved in advance or from your school's guidance, not from a search advertisement or a forwarded link. Second, enter your roll number exactly as printed on your admit card, and check the name and subjects on the result before accepting it as yours. Third, compare the online result with your school's marksheet or gazette information when it becomes available, and keep copies of both. Fourth, note announcement dates only from the official portal or your school, especially when calculating any rechecking window or admission deadline. Fifth, never pay anyone or share sensitive personal details to obtain or change a result.

If the official portal is slow, wait and retry, and ask your school whether it has gazette information, rather than switching to an aggregator out of impatience. If you see conflicting information between two websites, trust the official portal and your school, and treat the other site as wrong until proven otherwise. Teach this checklist to your family as well, because relatives often forward the first result link they find, and a calm family that checks officially protects the student from rumour stress. Result day should be about your achievement and your next steps, not about fighting misinformation, and a few minutes of careful checking at the start saves hours of confusion later.

After Checking: Keeping Your Result Safe

Once you have checked your result officially, protect it like any important document. Save or print the result page, keep it with your admit card and school marksheet, and photograph the set as a backup. Note your roll number, result date and any reference details in a safe place, because future applications, verifications and admissions may ask for them. Do not post your full result card publicly on social media, since it contains personal details that can be misused, and celebrate by sharing your achievement in words rather than by publishing documents. If you need to share a result for admission, share it only with the institution or office that asks for it, through their official channel.

Finally, remember that a result checked on the wrong site is not a result at all, however convincing it looked. Students who discover a discrepancy later, during admission or verification, face delays that a single official check would have prevented. Make the verified portal your habit now, bookmark it, and use it for every board announcement in your academic life, from matric through inter and beyond. The blog guides, solved notes and books page on this site can help with study planning, but for results themselves, only your board's verified portal and your school carry authority. That habit, practised once, will protect every result you ever check.

Frequently asked questions

What is the official website for BSEK Karachi results?

BSEK, the Board of Secondary Education Karachi for Classes 9 and 10, uses bsek.edu.pk. Do not confuse it with BIEK, the inter board.

What is the official website for BISE Sukkur results?

BISE Sukkur uses bisesuksindh.edu.pk. The guessed domain bisesukkur.edu.pk does not exist, so students should not use it or trust sites at similar guessed addresses.

Can I trust a result shown on an aggregator website?

No. Aggregators may show old, incomplete or guessed data without access to the board's official gazette. Always verify your result on your board's verified portal and through your school.

What should I do if a site asks me to pay to see my result?

Close the site immediately. Genuine board result checking is free, and any payment demand or promise to increase marks is a sign of a fake or dishonest site.
