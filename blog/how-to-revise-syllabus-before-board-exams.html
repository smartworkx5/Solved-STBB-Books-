How to Revise Syllabus Before Board Exams

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 570 words · 3 min read

553Study Tips

Most students finish the syllabus but never revise it properly — they read the book again from the start and run out of days. Revision is not re-reading; it is testing what you remember and fixing what you forgot. This guide gives a day-by-day revision plan that covers the whole syllabus before the paper.

Make a revision timetable, not a reading list

Write down the days left and divide the syllabus across them — one subject a day, hardest chapters first. A timetable with dates is a plan; a list of chapters is only a wish.

Give the last three days to the chapters you find hardest, not the easiest. The easy chapters need one hour; the hard ones need a full day.

Revise from short notes, never from the full book

Revise from the short notes you made during the year — definitions, formulas, diagrams and solved questions. If you have no short notes, make them now: one page per chapter, and revise from those pages only.

Start each revision session from the solved exercises and notes on this site — for example, the Class 10 Maths solved exercises and the Class 9 Physics solved exercises — and test yourself on the questions before reading the answers.

Test yourself every day, with past papers weekly

End every revision day by writing answers from memory — ten short questions, no book open. What you cannot write, you do not know yet, and that is exactly what to revise next morning.

Do one past paper every week under exam timing. A past paper shows the real paper pattern and the questions that repeat — and it teaches time management better than any advice.

The last week: revise, sleep, repeat

In the last week, revise only your short notes and your wrong-answer notebook. Do not start a new chapter — a new chapter in the last week costs the marks of three revised ones.

Sleep properly every night in the last week. A rested mind remembers; a tired mind forgets, however much it read. See all the guides on the blog index for subject-wise revision plans.

5 key points you must know

  • Divide the days left across the syllabus — one subject a day, hardest chapters first.
  • Revise from short notes only: one page per chapter with definitions, formulas and diagrams.
  • End every revision day by writing ten short answers from memory, no book open.
  • Do one past paper every week under exam timing.
  • In the last week, revise only short notes and the wrong-answer notebook — never start a new chapter.

Frequently asked questions

How do I revise the full syllabus before board exams?

Divide the days left across the syllabus, revise from short notes one page per chapter, and test yourself every day by writing answers from memory.

Should I re-read the whole book for revision?

No. Revise from short notes — definitions, formulas, diagrams and solved questions — and test yourself on the questions before reading the answers.

How often should I do past papers?

One past paper every week under exam timing. Past papers show the real paper pattern and teach time management.

What should I do in the last week before the exam?

Revise only your short notes and wrong-answer notebook, sleep properly every night, and do not start any new chapter.
