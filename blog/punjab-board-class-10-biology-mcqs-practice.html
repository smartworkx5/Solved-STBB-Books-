Punjab Board Class 10 Biology MCQs Practice Guide

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 731 words · 4 min read

484Subject Guides

Class 10 Biology is a fact-heavy paper, and its objective section is where careful students collect easy marks: every MCQ has one exact answer drawn from the book. Punjab Board students who practise MCQs chapter by chapter — after studying the solved exercises — find the objective section the most predictable part of the paper. This guide shows how to practise Class 10 Biology MCQs properly and turn them into certain marks.

The chapters your MCQs come from

The Punjab Board Class 10 Biology book covers inheritance and variation, the nervous and endocrine systems, reproduction, evolution, ecosystems and environment, and biotechnology. Each chapter produces MCQs of a recognisable kind: inheritance tests terms and ratios, physiology tests structures and functions, and ecosystems test definitions and relationships.

Practise a chapter's MCQs only after studying its solved exercises on the Punjab Board Class 10 Biology book page. The written exercises teach the concepts; the MCQs then measure whether the facts are exact — a name, a number, a function.

How to practise on the MCQs page

Work chapter by chapter on the MCQs practice page. For every wrong answer, read the explanation and write the correct fact in an error notebook — one line, in your own words. Biology MCQs repeat their facts across years and sets, so a fact fixed once is a mark earned many times.

For diagrams and cycles — the human brain, the nephron, the menstrual cycle, food chains — keep labelled sketches beside you while practising. Most diagram-based MCQs ask you to name a part or state its function, and a sketch you drew yourself answers both at once.

Terminology: the marks most students lose

Biology MCQs punish vague words. Homozygous is not heterozygous, a gene is not an allele, and a habitat is not a niche. Make a glossary of the pairs students confuse — one page per chapter — and revise it before every practice set. Precise terms are half the marks in this subject.

Inheritance questions need working, not memory: draw the cross, write the gametes, and read the ratio from your own diagram. A Punnett square drawn in the margin turns a ratio question from a guess into a calculation.

Final weeks: mixed timed practice

In the last two weeks, switch from chapter sets to mixed sets across the whole book, timed. Note which chapters still produce errors and give those chapters one more pass through their solved exercises and notes on the Notes page.

On the final day, revise only your error notebook and your glossary of confused terms. Those two documents contain exactly what you personally were getting wrong — the highest-value revision material you own.

5 key points you must know

  • Practise MCQs chapter by chapter — inheritance, nervous and endocrine systems, reproduction, evolution, ecosystems, biotechnology — and only mix chapters in the final two weeks.
  • Read the explanation under every answer and write each missed fact as one line in an error notebook. Biology facts repeat across sets; a fact fixed once pays many times.
  • Keep labelled sketches of key diagrams beside you: brain, nephron, food chains. Diagram MCQs ask for a name or a function, and a sketch answers both.
  • Learn confused term pairs exactly — gene versus allele, homozygous versus heterozygous, habitat versus niche. Precise terms are half the marks.
  • For inheritance questions, draw the cross and read the ratio from your own Punnett square. A drawn cross turns a ratio question into a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I practise Punjab Board Class 10 Biology MCQs?

On the MCQs practice page. Choose the Class 10 Biology book and practise chapter by chapter; every MCQ shows the correct answer with an explanation.

Should I practise MCQs before or after the written exercises?

After. Study the chapter's solved exercises on the book page first so the concepts are clear, then use MCQs to test exactness.

How do I handle inheritance and ratio MCQs?

Draw the cross: write the gametes, fill the Punnett square, and read the ratio from your own diagram. Never guess a ratio from memory when a square gives it to you.

What should I revise on the last day for Biology?

Your error notebook and your glossary of confused term pairs. They hold precisely the facts you personally missed, which is the most efficient final revision.
