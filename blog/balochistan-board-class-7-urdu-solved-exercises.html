Balochistan Board Class 7 Urdu Solved Exercises

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 770 words · 4 min read

491Solved Notes

Class 7 Urdu is the year the subject becomes serious: longer sabaq, real poetry, and grammar that returns in every later class. Balochistan Board students who work through the solved exercises lesson by lesson — answering in writing and checking against the solved answers — build the habit that carries them through Classes 8, 9 and 10. This guide shows how to use the Class 7 Urdu solved exercises properly.

What the Class 7 Urdu paper asks for

The Balochistan Board Class 7 Urdu paper has three clear parts: nasr and nazm comprehension, grammar, and composition — mazmoon, khat and darkhwast. Comprehension questions reward answers taken straight from the sabaq, grammar questions reward rules applied correctly, and composition rewards a clean, planned structure. No part of the paper rewards guessing.

Start with the solved exercises on the Balochistan Board Class 7 Urdu book page. Work lesson by lesson: read the sabaq, answer its questions in writing, then compare with the solved answers. The comparison is where the learning happens.

Nasr and nazm: answer from the text

For nasr lessons, the short questions are almost always answered by one or two sentences in the sabaq itself. Read the sabaq once for meaning, then read each question and find the exact lines that answer it. Write the answer in your own words but keep the lesson's facts exact — names, places and reasons must not change.

Nazm questions ask for the theme, the message, and the meaning of selected ash'ar. Learn the central idea of each nazm in two lines of your own words, and practise explaining one sher at a time. A sher explained clearly, with the poet's message stated plainly, earns full marks without memorising long notes.

Grammar: the marks you can make certain

Grammar is the most certain section of the paper because its rules never change: ism and its kinds, fail, jumlay, and the correct use of harf. Practise each rule with the solved exercises until the identification becomes automatic — classify ten words or sentences a day and check them against the answers.

Keep a small grammar notebook: one page per rule, with three examples of your own. Before the exam, this notebook replaces a whole grammar book — and because every example in it is one you wrote and checked yourself, it is the revision material you trust most.

Mazmoon, khat and the final weeks

Composition needs planning, not length. A mazmoon with an introduction, three clear paragraphs and a conclusion beats a long, wandering one. Learn the formats for mazmoon, khat and darkhwast by heart, and practise writing one composition a week in the exam time limit.

In the last two weeks, test yourself on the MCQs practice page for the objective section, and re-read your grammar notebook and composition formats. On the final day, revise only the formats and the rules you personally get wrong — that is the highest-value revision you own.

5 key points you must know

  • Work lesson by lesson through the solved exercises: read the sabaq, answer in writing, then compare with the solved answer and note what yours missed.
  • Answer short questions from the lesson's own facts — names, places and reasons exact — written in your own clear words.
  • Learn each nazm's central idea in two lines and practise explaining one sher at a time with the poet's message stated plainly.
  • Make grammar certain: ism, fail, jumlay and their kinds, practised with the solved exercises, plus one notebook page per rule.
  • Learn the formats for mazmoon, khat and darkhwast and write one composition a week inside the exam time limit.
