How to Present Answers Neatly in Board Exams

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 581 words · 3 min read

563Study Tips

A neat answer sheet is not about beautiful handwriting — it is about being read. Examiners mark hundreds of papers, and a paper where every answer is easy to find gets every mark it earned. This guide shows how to present your answers neatly in board exams: headings, spacing, underlining and margins that take no extra time.

Number every answer and give it a heading

Write the question number clearly in the margin, then a short heading for the answer — for example, the topic name or the first key term. An examiner who can find each answer in seconds marks it fairly; one who has to hunt for it marks it tired.

Keep the heading in slightly larger writing and underline it. The heading tells the examiner what the answer is about before they read the first line.

Leave one blank line between answers

Leave one blank line between the end of one answer and the heading of the next. Crowded answers run into each other and the examiner cannot tell where one answer ends and the next begins.

If an answer is long, leave a blank line between its paragraphs too. Short paragraphs with space between them read far better than one solid block of writing.

Underline the key term in each answer

Underline the definition, the formula or the key term in each answer. The examiner's eye goes to the underlined term first — that is where the marks are, so make it easy to find.

Underline only the key terms, not whole sentences. A page where everything is underlined is the same as a page where nothing is.

Keep margins straight and diagrams labelled

Keep a straight left margin and do not crowd the right edge. A clean margin makes the whole page look organised, even when you are writing fast under pressure.

Draw diagrams in the middle of the page with labels around them, not squeezed into a corner. The guide to writing answers for board exams shows how headings, spacing and step marks work together — neat presentation is one part of a neat answer sheet.

5 key points you must know

  • Number every answer in the margin and give it a short underlined heading.
  • Leave one blank line between answers and between long paragraphs.
  • Underline only the key term in each answer — definition, formula or main point.
  • Keep a straight left margin and do not crowd the right edge of the page.
  • Draw diagrams in the middle of the page with labels around them.

Frequently asked questions

How should I start each answer in a board exam?

Write the question number clearly in the margin, then a short heading — the topic name or the first key term — underlined.

How much space should I leave between answers?

One blank line between answers, and a blank line between paragraphs of a long answer, so the examiner can tell where each answer begins and ends.

Should I underline everything important in my answers?

No — underline only the key term in each answer. A page where everything is underlined is the same as a page where nothing is.

Where should I draw diagrams in the answer sheet?

In the middle of the page with labels around them, not squeezed into a corner — a labelled diagram in the centre is easy to find and easy to mark.
