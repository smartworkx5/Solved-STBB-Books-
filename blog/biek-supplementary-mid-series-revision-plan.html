BIEK Supplementary Exams: Mid-Series Revision Between Papers

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-03 · 1447 words · 7 min read

The BIEK supplementary examinations are now past their opening week and into the middle of the series, running from 28 October to 21 November 2026. For students still appearing, the hardest part of this stage is not any single paper but the stretch between papers: revising the next subject while recovering from the last one, without burning out before the series ends. This article offers a mid-series revision plan built for that exact situation, using the verified timing and structure of the BIEK supplementary series, so that the days between papers work for you instead of wearing you down.

The Series You Are In the Middle Of

The Board of Intermediate Education Karachi is holding its HSC Part-I and Part-II supplementary examinations from 28 October to 21 November 2026, in a single afternoon shift from 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM daily, with a reporting time of 1:30 PM. The date sheet was released on 23 October 2026 under press releases No. BIE/PR/2026/150 and 151, dated 22 October 2026. More than 21,000 students are appearing across 49 examination centres in Karachi, covering the Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, Science General, Commerce, Arts, Home Economics and special-category groups, under Chairman Faqir Muhammad Lakho.

The afternoon shift shapes everything about mid-series revision. Your mornings are free, your papers occupy the afternoon, and your evenings are for recovery and light preparation. Students who treat the morning as a second full school day and the evening as another study session quickly exhaust themselves, and exhaustion is the main enemy in the middle of a long series. The plan below treats the morning as the main revision block, the paper as the main event, and the evening as recovery plus only the lightest next-day preparation.

The Evening After a Paper: Recover First

When you return home after a 2:00 to 5:00 PM paper, resist the urge to open the next subject immediately. Eat a proper meal, rest for at least an hour, and let the paper go. Long discussions about answers you cannot change only drain energy and raise anxiety, and they steal time from the sleep that consolidates what you already know. A short walk, a conversation with family, or simple rest does more for tomorrow's paper than an evening of tired, unfocused reading.

In the later part of the evening, spend no more than an hour on the next paper, and keep it light: glance at the chapter list, pick the three topics you will revise in the morning, and lay out your notes and stationery. This is planning, not studying, and it means the next morning starts with a decision already made. Then sleep at a regular time. A supplementary series is a marathon, and students who protect their sleep in the middle weeks are the ones still performing well in the final week.

The Morning Before a Paper: Short, Targeted Revision

The morning before a paper is your main revision block, and it should be short and targeted rather than broad. Work through the three topics you chose the night before, practise the question types that appear most often in that subject, and write out anything you keep forgetting, such as formulas, definitions, diagrams or dates. For science papers, redraw key diagrams from memory and practise numerical steps. For commerce and arts papers, practise structuring answers and recalling definitions and explanations in your own words.

Stop revising at least an hour before you need to leave for the centre. Use that hour to check your admit card, which is available at admitcard.biek.edu.pk using your 16-digit challan number and matric roll number, confirm your centre, and travel so that you arrive by the 1:30 PM reporting time. Arriving calm and on time matters as much as the last hour of revision, because a rushed, anxious entry into the hall affects the first thirty minutes of the paper, which is when many students settle their rhythm.

Between Papers: The One-Day Gap Routine

When you have a full day between two papers, split it into three blocks with real breaks. The morning block covers the weakest topics of the next paper, the afternoon block is for practice questions and writing answers under time, and the evening block is for light review and preparation of materials. Between blocks, take genuine breaks: eat, rest, step outside, and avoid screens that tire your eyes. Three focused blocks beat six unfocused hours, and the breaks are part of the plan, not a failure of it.

If the gap is only a single evening and morning, shrink the plan rather than skipping rest. One evening of planning plus one morning of targeted revision is enough to enter the paper prepared, provided you studied the subject properly during the year. Mid-series revision is about recall and confidence, not about learning a subject from scratch, and students who accept that do better than students who try to cover everything the night before and arrive exhausted. Keep your notes organised by paper so that switching subjects takes minutes, not an hour of searching.

Staying Steady Until 21 November, and What Comes After

The series ends on 21 November 2026, and the final papers deserve the same steady routine as the first ones. Do not change your method in the last week, do not add new material, and do not compare your performance with other students' accounts of their papers, since those accounts are unreliable and unhelpful. Keep the same pattern: recover in the evening, revise in a targeted way in the morning, arrive by 1:30 PM, and attempt the paper calmly. Consistency is what carries students through a long series.

After the last paper, there is one fact to hold clearly: no supplementary result date has been announced, and a result can only come after the series ends on 21 November 2026. Do not trust any date circulating without an official BIEK notice, and check the official BIEK website for genuine announcements. In the meantime, rest properly, keep your admit card and any result-related documents safe, and use the waiting period to plan your next academic step in general terms rather than worrying about a date nobody has announced. 5 key points you must know

  • BIEK supplementary exams run 28 October to 21 November 2026, afternoon shift 2:00 to 5:00 PM
  • Reporting time is 1:30 PM; admit cards are at admitcard.biek.edu.pk
  • Evenings after a paper are for recovery and light planning, not heavy study
  • Mornings before a paper should be short, targeted revision of weak topics
  • No supplementary result date has been announced; results can only follow 21 November 2026

Frequently asked questions

When do BIEK supplementary exams run, and what is the timing?

The HSC Part-I and Part-II supplementary exams run from 28 October to 21 November 2026 in one afternoon shift, 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM daily, with a reporting time of 1:30 PM.

How should I use the evening after a paper?

Eat, rest for at least an hour, and avoid long answer discussions. Later, spend at most an hour picking the next paper's three revision topics and laying out your notes, then sleep at a regular time.

What is the best morning routine before a paper?

Revise three targeted topics, practise the most common question types, and write out anything you keep forgetting. Stop at least an hour before leaving, check your admit card and centre, and arrive by 1:30 PM.

How do I handle a single day between two papers?

Split it into three blocks: morning weak topics, afternoon practice questions, and evening light review, with real breaks between them. If the gap is shorter, shrink the plan rather than skipping rest.

Has a BIEK supplementary result date been announced?

No. No supplementary result date has been announced, and a result can only come after the series ends on 21 November 2026. Trust only official BIEK announcements, not circulating dates.
