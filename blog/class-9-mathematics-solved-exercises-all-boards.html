Class 9 Mathematics Solved Exercises — All 4 Boards Guide

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 784 words · 4 min read

477Solved Notes

Class 9 is where Mathematics becomes a board subject in all four boards — Sindh, Punjab, KPK and Balochistan. The books differ in chapter order, but the heart of the subject is the same everywhere: number systems, algebra, factorisation, linear equations, and geometry with theorems. This guide shows how to use the solved exercises of your own board's book, step by step.

One class, four boards — find your book

Each board publishes its own Class 9 Mathematics book, and you must practise from yours, because exercise numbering and chapter order differ. On this website every board's book is solved separately: the Sindh Board Class 9 Math book, the Punjab Board Class 9 Mathematics book, the Balochistan Board Class 9 Mathematics book, and the KPK Board Class 9 Mathematics book.

Open your board's page and work exercise by exercise. The solved exercises show every step of every question, so you can compare your method line by line instead of only checking the final answer.

The step method that works in every board

Board examiners in all four boards award method marks. Begin every solution by writing what is given and what is to be found. Then write one step per line, with a short reason beside any step that uses a rule — 'dividing both sides by 2', 'using the factor theorem', 'corresponding angles are equal'. A solution with reasons shows the examiner the method even if an arithmetic slip happens later.

For geometry, the rule is absolute: no diagram, no full marks. Draw the figure, mark the given lengths and angles on it, then write the proof in two columns or in clear steps — statement and reason together. The solved exercises model this format for every theorem in your book.

Algebra: the practice chapters

Algebra carries the largest share of Class 9 practice: algebraic expressions and identities, factorisation, linear equations and inequalities, and coordinate geometry basics. These chapters improve only with written practice. Solve five questions daily with the book closed, then open the solved exercise and compare methods — a different method that reaches the same answer is also correct, and knowing two methods makes you faster.

Keep a formula and identity sheet: the square and cube identities, the factorisation patterns, and the distance and midpoint formulas. Revise the sheet every Sunday. In the exam, identities should be reflexes, not puzzles.

Test and revise with notes and MCQs

After finishing a chapter's exercises, revise it from the chapter notes on the Notes page, then take the chapter's set on the MCQs practice page. Math MCQs test whether you can choose the right method quickly — a different skill from solving slowly, and the objective section rewards it.

Keep an error notebook for questions you solved wrongly. Write the correct first step only — most Math errors are wrong starts, not wrong middles. Reviewing first steps before the exam fixes the most expensive mistakes.

5 key points you must know

  • Start every solution with 'Given' and 'To find', then one step per line with a short reason. Method marks exist in all four boards — claim them.
  • Practise from your own board's book: chapter order and exercise numbers differ between Sindh, Punjab, KPK and Balochistan books.
  • Geometry answers need a drawn, labelled diagram plus statement-and-reason steps. A proof without a diagram is an incomplete answer.
  • Solve five algebra questions daily with the book closed, then compare with the solved exercise. Closed-book solving is the only practice that resembles the exam.
  • Finish each chapter with its notes and MCQ set. The notes compress the method; the MCQs test whether you can choose the method quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Are Class 9 Math solved exercises available for all four boards?

Yes. Sindh, Punjab, KPK and Balochistan Class 9 Mathematics books are all solved on this website, each from its own board's textbook. You can browse them from the books catalogue.

Do the four boards have the same Class 9 Math syllabus?

The core topics are the same — number systems, algebra, factorisation, linear equations and geometry — but chapter order and exercise numbering differ by board. Always practise from your own board's book.

How do I get full marks in geometry theorems?

Draw and label the diagram, write what is given and what is to be proved, then give each step with its reason. The solved exercises show this format for every theorem.

How many Math questions should I practise daily?

Five questions solved properly — written, stepped and checked — beat twenty questions copied. Add one past exercise question from a previous chapter each day to keep old chapters alive.
