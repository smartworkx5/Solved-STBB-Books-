BIEK Part-I Results One Week On: Next Steps If You Did Not Pass All Papers

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-03 · 1604 words · 8 min read

A week has passed since the Board of Intermediate Education Karachi announced its HSC Part-I Annual Examination 2026 results on 26 October 2026. For students who passed all their papers, the path is clear. For students who did not pass every paper, this first week after the result is the right time to stop worrying in general and start checking specific things: what exactly the gazette and marksheet say, which papers need attention, what your college advises, and what the supplementary route looks like in general terms. This article walks through those next steps one by one.

One Week On: What the 26 October Announcement Covered

BIEK announced the HSC Part-I Annual Examination 2026 results on 26 October 2026 for the Pre-Medical, Science General, Commerce (Regular and Private), Arts (Regular and Private), Special Candidates (Arts Regular) and Home Economics groups. Pre-Engineering results had been announced earlier, on 2 October 2026. Results are available on the official BIEK website, biek.edu.pk, and through the official BIEK Android app. A week after the announcement is a good moment to check your result calmly, because the first-day rush, slow pages and shared confusion have settled, and you can read your own record carefully.

The gazettes behind the announcement are large documents: the Pre-Medical gazette runs to 178 pages and the Commerce gazette to 172 pages, according to result.pk. That size is worth knowing, because it explains why finding your entry takes patience, and why you should search by your roll number rather than scrolling. Your marksheet, when you receive it, is the document that matters most for the next steps, so keep it safe and make a copy or a clear photograph of it for your records.

The 40 Percent Rule: Understanding What Ungraded Means

Under the rule applied by IBCC and the Sindh Universities and Boards Department, a paper in which a student scores under 40 percent is marked Ungraded, which means the paper is not passed. This is the single most important rule for students who did not pass all papers, because it tells you exactly which papers need action and which do not. A student who passed five papers and fell below 40 percent in one paper has one paper to address, not a whole year to repeat, and that distinction changes the whole plan.

Read your marksheet paper by paper and mark each result against the 40 percent line. Note the papers you passed, the papers marked Ungraded, and any paper where the marks look inconsistent with your expectations. If a paper's marks surprise you badly, note it separately and ask your college about the proper route for queries, since colleges handle student questions about results through official channels. Do not rely on rumours about rechecking or retotalling procedures that have not been announced for your case; ask the college office what applies to you.

Checklist: Gazette, Marksheet and College Questions

Start with the gazette entry for your roll number and compare it with your marksheet when it arrives, checking that your name, roll number and subject marks match in both places. Any mismatch should be raised with your college promptly, with a copy of both documents in hand, because corrections are easiest when they are reported early and with evidence. Keep your admit card, marksheet and any result printouts together in one folder, since you will need them for every later step, from college queries to future examination forms.

Then visit your college office with three specific questions. First, which of my papers are recorded as Ungraded, and what does that mean for my next examination appearance? Second, what is the college's process and timetable for students in my situation? Third, what study support does the college offer for the papers I need to improve? Written answers to these questions are worth more than general reassurance, so note down what you are told and the name of the person who told you. The Supplementary Route in General Terms

For students who did not pass all papers, the supplementary examination route is the usual next opportunity to clear the remaining papers, and it is worth understanding in general terms while waiting for specific announcements. Supplementary examinations are held for students who need to reappear in particular papers, and the current supplementary series running from 28 October to 21 November 2026 shows the shape of such a series: an afternoon shift, a published date sheet, and admit cards issued through the official BIEK channels. Your own appearance, however, depends on the official announcement that applies to your result, and that announcement has not been made for the 26 October Part-I cohort in the sources checked for this article.

That means two things. First, do not assume a date, a form deadline or a fee from another group's schedule; wait for the notice that names your examination. Second, use the waiting time productively by revising the Ungraded papers now, while the material is still fresh, rather than waiting for a form to appear before opening a book. Students who start revision early enter the supplementary series with confidence, while students who wait for paperwork often find the preparation time has shrunk. No recheck or supplementary date is invented in this article, because none has been announced for this cohort in the sources checked.

Planning the Next Few Weeks Without Panic

A result that is not a full pass is disappointing, but it is not the end of an academic year, and it is far more common than students realise. Many students clear their remaining papers in the next appearance and continue without losing a year, especially when they treat the weak papers as a focused project rather than a general failure. Build a simple weekly plan: two or three study blocks per week for each Ungraded paper, regular practice questions, and one weekly review of what improved. Keep the plan small enough to follow every week, because a plan followed for six weeks beats an ambitious plan abandoned after one.

Talk to your teachers about the specific topics where you lost marks, and ask for short practice tests on those topics. Teachers can usually identify quickly whether the problem was understanding, presentation, time management or exam technique, and each problem has a different fix. If the problem was time management, practise timed papers. If it was understanding, revisit the chapters with a teacher's help. If it was presentation, practise writing structured answers. One week after the result, you have time to fix the real problem, and that is the most useful thing this week can give you.

5 key points you must know

Frequently asked questions

When were the BIEK Part-I results announced?

The HSC Part-I Annual Examination 2026 results were announced on 26 October 2026 for Pre-Medical, Science General, Commerce, Arts, Special Candidates and Home Economics groups. Pre-Engineering results were announced earlier, on 2 October 2026.

What does Ungraded mean?

Under the IBCC and Sindh Universities and Boards Department rule, a paper scored under 40 percent is marked Ungraded, which means the paper is not passed. That paper is the one that needs attention in your next appearance.

What should I check first after the result?

Compare your gazette entry with your marksheet, checking name, roll number and subject marks. List each paper as passed or Ungraded, keep all documents together, and report any mismatch to your college promptly with copies in hand.

What should I ask my college?

Ask which papers are Ungraded and what that means for your next appearance, what the college's process and timetable are for students in your situation, and what study support is available for the papers you need to improve.

Is there a supplementary date for Part-I students who did not pass all papers?

No date for this cohort has been announced in the sources checked for this article. Do not assume a date from another group's schedule. Use the waiting time to revise your Ungraded papers, and watch for an official BIEK notice.
