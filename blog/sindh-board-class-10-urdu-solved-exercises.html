Sindh Class 10 Urdu Solved Exercises

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 561 words · 3 min read

562Solved Exercises

Class 10 Urdu is the Matric paper where the sabaq, the ghazal, the grammar and the writing section each carry enough marks to change the grade. Students who prepare all four parts steadily pass comfortably; students who only memorise khulase struggle. This guide shows how to work through the solved exercises so every part of the paper is covered.

Khulasa from the sabaq, not from the guide

Open the Class 10 Urdu book page and read the full sabaq before looking at its khulasa. Then close the book and write the khulasa in your own words in five or six lines, and compare it with the solved exercise.

A khulasa written from the lesson you read sounds natural and complete. One copied from a guide sounds borrowed, and examiners mark the difference.

Tashreeh in three steps

For every she'r, follow the same three steps: the literal meaning of the difficult words, the meaning of the whole she'r in simple Urdu, and one line about what the poet wants to teach. Students who skip the first step write tashreeh that misses the poet's point.

The Class 9 Urdu solved exercises practise the same three steps on the shorter ghazals of Class 9 — the method is identical, only the poetry is harder.

Grammar by daily practice

Grammar — mutradif, mutazad, wahid-jama, islah-e-jumla, and the use of idioms — is learnt by doing, not by reading. Solve five grammar questions every day from the solved exercises and mark them yourself the same day.

A grammar mistake repeated five times becomes a habit. Correct each mistake in writing the day you make it, and it will not appear in the paper.

One mazmoon a week, in exam format

Write one mazmoon every week in the full exam format: introduction, three body paragraphs, conclusion. A practised structure is worth more than a memorised essay, because the paper's topic is never the one you memorised.

Keep a list of mazmoon outlines — five lines per topic — instead of memorising whole essays. An outline lets you write on any topic the paper gives.

5 key points you must know

  • Read the full sabaq, then write the khulasa in your own words before comparing with the solved exercise.
  • Do tashreeh in three steps: difficult words, whole-she'r meaning, the poet's message.
  • Solve five grammar questions daily and correct mistakes the same day.
  • Write one mazmoon a week in the full exam format.
  • Keep mazmoon outlines — five lines per topic — instead of memorising whole essays.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a good khulasa in Class 10 Urdu?

Read the sabaq first, close the book, and write the main points in your own words in five or six lines — then compare with the solved exercise.

What is the best way to learn ash'aar ki tashreeh?

Three steps: difficult words first, then the whole she'r in simple Urdu, then one line about the poet's message.

How can I improve Urdu grammar for the Matric exam?

Solve five grammar questions every day and correct each mistake in writing the same day — grammar is learnt by doing.

How often should I practise mazmoon writing?

One mazmoon every week in exam format, with outlines of five lines per topic instead of memorised essays.
