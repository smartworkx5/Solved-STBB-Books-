How BISE Sukkur Announces Position Holders

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-19 · 1167 words · 6 min read

The BISE Sukkur position holders announced question is best answered by looking at how the board actually makes the announcement, because Sukkur follows a careful, formal process. For the SSC Part-II (Class 10) 1st Annual Examination 2026, Science Group, BISE Sukkur's official notification declares positions provisionally. The board publishes separate boys, girls and overall merit lists, so a pupil can appear in more than one list. This article explains the announcement process step by step, uses the 2026 Science Group example with its verified marks, and shows what pupils can learn from how positions are declared. Every position in this article is presented as provisionally declared, exactly as the board's notice states.

What provisionally declared means

The word provisional is the most important word in the Sukkur announcement. When the board says positions are declared provisionally, it means the declaration stands subject to verification. Marks, eligibility and records are still open to checking, and a position could change if verification finds an error. Pupils, schools and newspapers should therefore describe the positions as provisional, not final, until the board confirms them.

Provisional declaration is a sensible way to run a public announcement. It lets the board honour high achievers promptly while keeping the record honest. It also protects the pupils named: a provisional position is a real achievement, but nobody should treat it as a settled legal fact on the day of the announcement.

The 2026 Science Group example: overall list

In the 2026 SSC Part-II Science Group announcement, the overall first position was shared at 1009 marks. Two pupils held it: Umar Riyan, seat number 233378, and Emaan Hafeez, seat number 243820, both with A-1 grades. The overall second position stood at 1006 marks, and the overall third position was 1004 marks in one list and 1001 in another of the separate lists.

These figures show how close the top of the merit list can be. A gap of three marks separates first from second, and a handful of marks covers the whole top tier. That closeness is a reminder that position-level performance is built on care with every mark: presentation, accuracy and completeness across every paper, not brilliance in one subject alone.

Why there are three lists: boys, girls and overall

BISE Sukkur publishes separate boys, girls and overall merit lists. The boys list ranks boys, the girls list ranks girls, and the overall list ranks all candidates together. A pupil who tops the girls list may also appear on the overall list, and the overall first position in 2026 was shared by a boy and a girl, which is why the same 1009 marks appear for both.

Three lists serve three purposes. The overall list shows the highest achievement across the whole group. The separate lists recognise the highest achievers among boys and among girls. Together, they give schools and families a fuller picture than a single list could, and they make sure that achievement is recognised fairly across the group.

How a position announcement happens

The announcement follows a sequence that pupils rarely see. First, the examination is held and the papers are marked. Then the board compiles the results and prepares the merit lists. Then the board issues an official notification declaring the positions provisionally. Only then do the names and marks reach the public. Each step matters, because an error caught early never reaches the announcement.

Pupils should always look for the official notification itself rather than a forwarded list. The notification is the board's own document, and it carries the provisional wording, the lists and the marks. A list that cannot be traced to the notification should not be shared as if it were official.

What pupils can learn from the announcement

The first lesson is about margins. When first and second places are separated by three marks, the difference is not genius but consistency: attempting every question, writing organised answers, and checking work before handing it in. The second lesson is about the whole syllabus. A top position requires strength across all papers, because a single weak paper costs more marks than any single strong paper can recover.

The third lesson is about conduct. Pupils who reach the top of a merit list do so under the same papers, the same marking and the same rules as everyone else. That is what makes a board position worth respecting. Pupils aiming high should focus on steady, honest work: full syllabus coverage, timed practice, and teacher feedback on weak topics.
