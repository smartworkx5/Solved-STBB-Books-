Board Gazette vs Online Result: What Is the Difference?

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-19 · 1293 words · 6 min read

The board result gazette vs online result question comes up every result season, because pupils meet both documents and are not always sure which one to trust. A gazette is the board's consolidated official document: it lists roll numbers and marks together, usually as a downloadable file that schools use. An online result is the individual slip a pupil sees by entering a roll number on the board's portal. Both are official, but they serve different purposes, and each has strengths the other lacks. This article explains what a gazette is, what an online result shows, when to use each, and how to avoid the unofficial lists that circulate on result day. The BIEK Pre-Engineering gazette, which lists roll numbers and marks only and names no toppers, is a good example of how gazettes work.

Two official ways to see one result

Every board result exists in more than one form. The board keeps a master record, then publishes it for pupils and schools in two main ways: the gazette and the online roll-number result. A pupil who understands both never has to depend on a forwarded screenshot or a third-party page, because the two official forms cover every situation: the online slip for a quick personal check, and the gazette for verification, school records and whole-class checking.

The habit that saves pupils from confusion is simple. Check your own result in one official form, then confirm it in the other when it matters, for example before admission or before applying for rechecking. If the two agree, the result is settled. If they disagree, the school should sort it out with the board.

What a gazette is

A gazette is a consolidated document that lists the results of many candidates together, organised by roll number, with the marks or grades each candidate obtained. Schools download it to see all their pupils' results at once, to verify results for admissions, and to advise pupils about their next steps. It is the board's own publication, and it is the document schools treat as the reference copy.

The BIEK Pre-Engineering gazette shows exactly what a gazette contains and what it does not. It lists roll numbers and marks only. It does not name toppers or position holders. Any topper list circulating for that result is therefore unofficial, because the official gazette contains no such list. Pupils should remember this pattern: a gazette reports results, it does not rank pupils for publicity.

What the online result shows

The online result is the personal version. A pupil enters a roll number on the board's official portal and sees their own result: name, roll number, marks or grades, and the overall outcome. It is fast, it is private, and it is the form most pupils see first on result day. It is also the form most affected by result-day traffic, because thousands of pupils check at the same time.

The online slip is convenient but fragile as a record. A screenshot can be edited, a page can be misread, and a mistyped roll number can show someone else's result. That is why the online result is best treated as a first check, with the gazette or the school's confirmation as the second check whenever the result will be used for admission or any official purpose.

When to use each one

Use the online result when you want a quick personal check on result day, or when you need to see your own marks without visiting the school. Use the gazette when the portal is slow, when a roll-number slip is lost, when the school needs to verify a whole class, or when you need a second official source before admission or rechecking.

Schools live in the gazette, and pupils live in the online slip. The two meet when a pupil takes a doubtful online result to the school, and the school confirms it against the gazette. That confirmation step is the quiet workhorse of result season, and pupils should use it without hesitation whenever anything looks wrong. If the confirmed result still looks wrong, the next step is scrutiny, covered in our guide to scrutiny and rechecking in Sindh.

Avoiding fake and unofficial lists

Result day brings a flood of unofficial lists: topper lists, district merit lists, and screenshots that claim to show results before the board publishes them. The defence against all of them is one question: did the board publish this? If the answer is no, the list is unofficial, whatever it looks like. The BIEK Pre-Engineering example is the model case, since the official gazette names no toppers at all.

Pupils should also avoid third-party 'result service' pages that ask for roll numbers or money. Boards publish results on their own official websites, and schools can check the gazette for free. If a page asks for payment or personal details beyond a roll number, close it and use the official portal or your school instead. 5 key points you must know

  • A gazette is the board's consolidated official document listing roll numbers and marks together.
  • An online result is the individual slip a pupil sees by entering a roll number on the board's portal.
  • The BIEK Pre-Engineering gazette lists roll numbers and marks only and names no toppers.
  • Use the online result for a quick personal check and the gazette for verification and school records.
  • Unofficial topper lists and third-party 'result service' pages should never be trusted or paid.

Frequently asked questions

Is the gazette more official than the online result?

Both are official publications of the board. The gazette is the consolidated reference document schools use, while the online result is the individual slip for a quick personal check. When the two agree, the result is settled. When they disagree, the school should confirm with the board.

Does the gazette name toppers?

Not necessarily. The BIEK Pre-Engineering gazette lists roll numbers and marks only and does not name toppers or position holders. Any topper list circulating for that result is unofficial.

What should I do if the online portal does not show my result?

Recheck the roll number, retry later because result-day traffic overloads board sites, and then ask your school to check the institutional gazette. Do not hand your roll number to a third-party 'result service'.

Can I use a screenshot of my online result for admission?

A screenshot is a weak record because it can be edited or misread. Schools usually verify against the gazette, so confirm your result with your school before admission or any official step.

How do I learn to read a gazette entry?

Find your roll number, read across to the marks or grades for each paper, and confirm the name and school details match. Our guide to how to read the BIEK Pre-Engineering gazette walks through a real example.
