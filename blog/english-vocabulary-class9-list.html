Class 9 English Vocabulary: 100 Useful Words

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-04 · 1723 words · 9 min read

Class 9 is the first board-exam year for many students, so English vocabulary must cover the exact terms the paper uses: comprehension and precis, tense and voice, theme and moral, plus the study skills that turn knowledge into marks. This general list gives board-exam words with simple meanings and example sentences for the Class 9 level, and it is not an official answer key for any single lesson. Learn one theme at a time, practise one past-style question for each group, and revise through the [solved notes](/solved.html) and [books](/books.html) pages so the paper feels familiar when it arrives.

Board Exam Terms You Must Know

Board examinations use fixed terms in their papers, so Class 9 students should know exactly what comprehension, precis, essay and letter questions ask for, and the solved notes pages show how these terms appear in real papers. Comprehension means understanding a passage and answering about it. For example, Read the comprehension passage twice before answering its questions.. Precis means a short, clear version of a longer passage. For example, A good precis keeps only the main ideas of the passage.. Summary means a brief account of the main points. For example, Write the summary in your own words, not by copying lines.. Essay means a piece of writing on one topic. For example, The essay question carries good marks, so plan it carefully..

Letter, application, dialogue and narration questions also follow fixed patterns, so learn their names and formats now, and practise one of each every month so the board paper holds no surprises for you. Letter means a written message to someone. For example, Write a letter to your friend about your school library.. Application means a formal written request. For example, Write an application to the principal for leave of two days.. Dialogue means a conversation between two people. For example, Write a dialogue between a student and a bookseller.. Narration means telling a story or events in order. For example, Narration questions test how clearly you can retell events..

Grammar Terms for the Board Paper

Grammar terms decide marks in the board paper, because questions name them directly, and a student who confuses tense with voice, or speech with punctuation, loses marks on questions that are actually easy with clear terms. Tense means the verb form showing past, present or future. For example, Change this sentence from present tense to past tense.. Voice means active or passive form of a verb. For example, Change the sentence from active voice to passive voice.. Speech means direct or indirect reporting of words. For example, Change this direct speech into indirect speech carefully.. Punctuation means marks that organise written sentences. For example, Correct punctuation makes your writing clear and easy to mark..

Idiom, proverb, synonym and antonym questions reward students who learn them as pairs with sentences, because a meaning remembered without a sentence is often confused in the paper, while a sentence fixes the meaning firmly. Idiom means a phrase with a special meaning. For example, The idiom break the ice means to start friendly talk.. Proverb means a short saying with a wise meaning. For example, Slow and steady wins the race is a well-known proverb.. Synonym means a word with a similar meaning. For example, Happy is a synonym of glad in this sentence.. Antonym means a word with the opposite meaning. For example, Hot is an antonym of cold in this pair..

Reading and Literature Words

Literature questions use words like theme, moral, character and setting, so learn them before the paper, because they tell you exactly what the examiner wants: the message, the people, the place or the events of a text. Theme means the main idea or message of a text. For example, The theme of the story is the value of hard work.. Moral means the lesson taught by a story. For example, The moral of the story is that honesty always wins.. Character means a person in a story or play. For example, The main character of the story is a brave young student.. Setting means the time and place of a story. For example, The setting of the story is a small village school..

Plot, stanza, poet and narrator complete the literature set, and they appear in poetry and story questions alike, so learn each one with a short example from a lesson you have already studied in class. Plot means the sequence of events in a story. For example, The plot moves quickly from a problem to its solution.. Stanza means a group of lines in a poem. For example, Each stanza of the poem describes one part of nature.. Poet means a person who writes poems. For example, The poet describes the morning with beautiful images.. Narrator means the person who tells a story. For example, The narrator tells the story from the view of a student..

Study Skills for Board Success

Board success depends on study skills as much as on knowledge, because a good timetable, honest revision and calm concentration turn what you know into marks, while last-day panic wastes even well-prepared lessons. Revision means studying material again to remember it. For example, Daily revision keeps old lessons fresh before examinations.. Timetable means a plan showing when to study each subject. For example, Follow a simple timetable and give English time every day.. Practice means doing exercises repeatedly to improve. For example, Practice past paper questions to understand the paper pattern.. Preparation means getting ready for a test or task. For example, Good preparation starts weeks before the examination, not nights..

Concentration, confidence, accuracy and presentation are the quiet skills behind high marks, because they decide whether your knowledge reaches the paper neatly, completely and in the time allowed by the examiner. Concentration means full attention on one task. For example, Concentration improves when you study away from noise.. Confidence means belief in your own ability. For example, Confidence grows when you practise answers regularly.. Accuracy means being correct and exact. For example, Accuracy in spelling and grammar earns extra respect from checkers.. Presentation means how neatly work is shown. For example, Neat presentation with clear paragraphs impresses every examiner..

Writing Verbs and Useful Adjectives

Question verbs tell you what to do: analyse, describe, discuss and explain are not the same task, and students who learn the difference write focused answers, with model answers on blog showing each verb in action. Analyse means to examine something in detail. For example, Analyse the character of the hero in five sentences.. Describe means to say what something is like. For example, Describe the scene in the poem with clear details.. Discuss means to talk about ideas for and against. For example, Discuss the advantages of reading books daily.. Explain means to make an idea clear. For example, Explain the proverb with one real-life example..

Finish your board vocabulary with adjectives that add quality to answers, because a significant point, an effective method or an appropriate example sounds precise and mature, which is exactly the tone board examiners reward. Illustrate means to explain with an example. For example, Illustrate your answer with one example from daily life.. Significant means important and meaningful. For example, Reading daily has a significant effect on vocabulary.. Effective means producing the wanted result. For example, Regular revision is an effective way to prepare.. Appropriate means suitable for the situation. For example, Use appropriate words for a formal letter to the principal..

Frequently asked questions

What vocabulary matters most in the Class 9 board paper?

Board exam terms, grammar terms and literature words matter most, because questions name them directly, so students who know comprehension, precis, tense, voice, theme and moral can understand every question without guessing its meaning. Keep this answer beside your lesson notes, and read it once more before the next test so the idea stays fresh and ready.

Is this list from a specific Class 9 lesson?

No, this is a general word list for the Class 9 level, covering exam terms, grammar, literature and study skills, and it is not presented as an official answer key or as the vocabulary of any single lesson. Use the idea in one real sentence or task today, because using knowledge turns it from a fact into a skill you own.

How should I practise precis writing?

Read the passage twice, underline only the main ideas, write them in your own words in about one third of the length, and give it a short title, because a precis rewards selection and clarity, not copying or extra detail. Revise older answers together with new ones, so new learning connects with what you already know instead of replacing it.

What is the difference between describe and explain?

Describe asks what something is like, so you give details and features, while explain asks why or how something works, so you give reasons and clear steps, and mixing the two is a common cause of lost marks. Stay patient with slow progress, because steady small steps in English build more lasting skill than rushed cramming before tests.

How can I improve presentation in the board paper?

Leave margins, write clear paragraphs, underline headings where allowed, check spelling at the end, and keep handwriting readable, because neat presentation helps the examiner follow your answer and rewards your hard work fairly. End each study session by recalling the main point aloud without looking, since recall practice strengthens memory far more than re-reading.
