English Essay: My School (Class 9-10 Model)

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 1465 words · 7 min read

Learning to write an english essay my school class 9 10 students can be proud of starts with one simple idea: describe what you actually see, feel and learn every day. A school essay is not a list of buildings; it is a picture of a place where teachers guide you, friends support you and lessons shape your character. In this article you will find a clear outline, useful vocabulary and a full model essay that you can adapt in your own words. You can also explore other study material on the [Solved Notes](/solved.html) page and the [books](/books.html) collection before you begin writing.

Why a School Essay Matters

Every student can write about school because every student lives that experience, and that is why examiners set this topic so often. A good school essay shows that you can organise ideas, use correct grammar and express feelings in simple English. It also tests observation: the student who notices the library, the morning assembly and the kindness of a teacher writes a richer essay than the student who only writes that the school is big and beautiful.

Think of your essay as a short tour for a visitor who has never seen your school. Start at the gate, move through the classrooms and playground, and end with what the school has taught you about life. When you write in this order, your essay feels natural and easy to follow. Keep your sentences short and honest, and avoid praising your school with empty words such as very, very good repeated again and again. Specific details, like the morning assembly or the science laboratory, make your writing believable and interesting.

  • The name and location of your school
  • The classrooms, library and laboratory
  • The teachers and the head teacher
  • Games, assembly and co-curricular activities
  • What the school teaches you about character and discipline

Essay Outline: My School

An outline is your essay plan, and writing it first saves you from forgetting important points halfway through. For this topic, use five paragraphs. The introduction names the school and gives a first impression. The second paragraph describes the building and facilities. The third paragraph describes the teachers and their role. The fourth paragraph covers games, activities and friendships. The conclusion explains what the school means to you and what you hope to become because of it.

Notice how each paragraph has one job only. If you mix games into the paragraph about teachers, the essay becomes untidy. One idea per paragraph is the golden rule of essay writing, and it works for every topic in this series. Write your outline in the margin or on rough paper, then follow it paragraph by paragraph without jumping back and forth.

  • Introduction: name of the school and first impression
  • Body 1: building, classrooms, library and laboratory
  • Body 2: teachers, head teacher and discipline
  • Body 3: games, assembly and friends
  • Conclusion: gratitude and future hopes

Model Essay: My School

My school is the place where I spend the happiest hours of my day, and I love it more than any other place in my town. It is situated near my home, so I walk to school every morning with my friends, and we reach the gate before the bell rings. The school building is neat and clean, with bright classrooms, a small library and a playground where we play in the break. In the morning assembly we pray, sing the national anthem and listen to the announcements of the day, and this peaceful start fills our hearts with energy and discipline.

The teachers of my school are kind, hardworking and sincere, and they treat every student like their own child. They explain difficult lessons again and again until every student understands, and they encourage us to ask questions without fear. Our head teacher is strict about discipline but fair to everyone, and under his guidance the school runs smoothly like a well-organised family. I have learned from my teachers not only English, mathematics and science, but also honesty, respect and punctuality, and these lessons will stay with me for my whole life.

Model Essay: Conclusion and Useful Vocabulary

My school also gives us time for games and healthy competition, and this is why I never feel bored there. We play cricket and football in the games period, take part in debates and quiz competitions, and celebrate national days with speeches and songs. My friends make school life joyful, because we study together, help one another in difficult subjects and share our lunch in the break. In conclusion, my school is like a second home to me, and I am grateful to my teachers for shaping my character. I hope that one day I will make my school proud by becoming a useful and honest citizen of my country.

Useful vocabulary makes a simple essay sound polished. Learn these words and use them correctly: campus for the school grounds, assembly for the morning gathering, laboratory for the science room, discipline for orderly behaviour, and gratitude for thankfulness. You can also use phrases such as second home, shape my character and make my school proud. Do not use a difficult word unless you are sure of its meaning; a simple correct word is always better than a grand wrong one.

  • campus — the grounds and buildings of a school
  • assembly — the morning gathering of students
  • laboratory — a room for science experiments
  • discipline — obeying rules and behaving well
  • gratitude — a feeling of thankfulness

Exam Technique for This Essay

Good essays are planned before they are written, and this topic is no exception. Spend the first five minutes making a short outline on the rough-work page: introduction, three or four body points in a sensible order, and a conclusion. This small habit prevents repetition, keeps your ideas in order, and helps you finish on time. In the examination hall, write neatly, leave a clear space between paragraphs, and check your spelling at the end if time allows. A clean, well-organised answer always scores better than a longer, confused one, even when both students know the same facts.

Practise by writing this essay once at home without looking at your notes, then compare your draft with the outline in this article and improve the weak paragraphs. Learn five or six useful words for this topic and use them naturally, not forced into every sentence. Avoid copying long sentences you do not understand, because examiners reward clear, original English. Read your essay aloud once before submitting it in practice: if a sentence sounds strange when spoken, rewrite it in simpler words. With one careful practice essay a week, your speed, confidence and marks will all improve steadily.

  • Write the outline first, then the essay
  • One main idea in every paragraph
  • Use specific details, not empty praise
  • Keep sentences short and clear
  • Revise spelling and full stops at the end

5 key points you must know

  • A strong opening, a clear outline and one idea per paragraph.
  • Specific details about teachers, library and games make the essay believable.
  • The model essay above can be rewritten in your own words for practice.
  • Good vocabulary should be used naturally, not forced.
  • Regular practice, not memorisation, builds essay-writing skill.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start an essay on My School?

Begin with a simple personal sentence, such as naming your school and saying why you like it, then add one detail about the morning or the building. Avoid starting with a dictionary definition or a very general sentence about education. A personal, honest opening immediately makes your essay feel real.

What if my school is small and has few facilities?

Write honestly about what your school does have: good teachers, kind friends, a clean classroom or a small library. Examiners do not mark the size of your school; they mark your English, your organisation and your ideas. A sincere essay about a small school can score higher than an exaggerated essay about a large one.

How long should this essay be in the examination?

For Classes 9 and 10, about 250 to 300 words is a safe length. That is usually five to seven paragraphs: one introduction, three or four body paragraphs and one conclusion. Quality matters more than length, so do not pad your essay with repeated sentences just to make it longer.

Can I change the model essay in my own words?

Yes, and you should. Use the model essay as a guide for structure and ideas, then rewrite it in your own simple English. Examiners reward original writing, so keep the outline, change the examples where you can, and add one personal observation from your own life. You can find more study guides on the blog page.
