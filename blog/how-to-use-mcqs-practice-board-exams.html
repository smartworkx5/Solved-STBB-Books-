How to Use MCQs Practice for Board Exams

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 564 words · 3 min read

543Study Tips

MCQs look easy and lose students the most marks — one wrong option, one careless reading, and the mark is gone. The students who score full marks in the MCQ section do not guess better; they practise differently. This guide shows how to use MCQ practice so every question you have solved once stays solved in the paper.

Practise MCQs with a pencil, never by looking

Work every MCQ with a pencil: write the formula, the working or the reason before choosing an option. An MCQ solved on paper is learnt; one answered by looking at the options is guessed — and a guessed answer is forgotten by the paper.

Cover the options while you solve, then uncover them and pick the one that matches your answer. If none matches, your working is wrong — and finding that out in practice is worth more than finding it in the paper.

Keep a wrong-answer notebook

For every MCQ you get wrong, write the question, the correct answer and the reason in a notebook — one line each. A wrong-answer notebook is the most honest revision material you will ever own.

Revise the notebook every two days and redo each question. A question you solve twice is a question you own; a question you only read once is a question you will miss again.

Learn the chapter, then test it

MCQs test the chapter, not the question bank. Read the chapter first, then do its MCQs — a question you answer from understanding stays, a question you answer from the answer key does not.

Find chapter-wise MCQ practice on your subject's book page — for example the Class 10 Chemistry MCQs — and work one chapter a day until the whole book is covered.

Do a timed MCQ round every week

Once a week, answer twenty MCQs in twenty minutes without the notes. The clock teaches you to move past a hard question and return to it — the habit that saves marks in the real paper.

Check your answers against the MCQ practice guides and note which chapter your mistakes come from — that chapter is your next revision.

5 key points you must know

  • Work every MCQ with a pencil — write the working or the reason before choosing an option.
  • Cover the options while you solve, then pick the one that matches your own answer.
  • Keep a wrong-answer notebook: question, correct answer and reason, one line each.
  • Read the chapter first, then do its MCQs — one chapter a day until the book is covered.
  • Do a timed round of twenty MCQs in twenty minutes every week.

Frequently asked questions

How do I practise MCQs for board exams?

Work each MCQ with a pencil before looking at the options, keep a wrong-answer notebook, and do a timed round every week.

Should I memorise MCQ answers?

No. Learn the chapter first, then test it with MCQs — a question answered from understanding stays, one answered from the key does not.

What is a wrong-answer notebook?

A notebook with every MCQ you got wrong: the question, the correct answer and the reason, revised every two days.

How many MCQs should I practise daily?

One chapter a day, plus a timed round of twenty MCQs in twenty minutes once a week.
