Hyderabad Board Inquiry November 2026: What Students Should Know and Do

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-04 · 1691 words · 8 min read

The Sindh government has ordered an inquiry into alleged irregularities in the Intermediate Part-II results of the Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education Hyderabad, after complaints and social-media reports, and many students and parents are now unsure what this means for them. This guide explains only what has been verified so far, what has not been announced, and how students can stay informed without falling for rumours. The key message is calm and simple: an inquiry is a process for checking facts, and students should follow official announcements, keep their own documents safe, and avoid sharing or believing unverified claims.

What Happened: The Inquiry Ordered on 23 October 2026

On 23 October 2026, the Sindh government ordered an inquiry into alleged irregularities in BISE Hyderabad's Intermediate Part-II results, following complaints and reports circulating on social media. The notification was issued by Secretary Universities and Boards Abbas Baloch, after approval from Minister Muhammad Ismail Rahoo, which shows that the matter is being handled through official channels rather than through informal claims. As part of the same action, Deputy Controller of the Top Secret Branch Ejaz Ali Kaka was suspended, and a three-member committee was formed to examine the matter and report back.

The committee is headed by Professor Dr. Arbeela Bhutto, with Additional Secretary Ameer Fazal Owaisi and Dr. Nasrullah Pirzada as members, and its report and recommendations are due to the minister within 30 days of the 23 October order, which points to around 22 November 2026. Students should note that this timeline is for the committee's report to the minister, and it does not by itself mean that any new result, decision, or date has already been announced. For study continuity, keep using STBB books and solved notes while you wait for official news.

What Has NOT Been Announced

It is equally important to be clear about what has not been announced in the sources seen so far, because rumours usually grow in exactly this gap. No cancellation of results has been announced, so students should not claim, repeat, or act as if results have been cancelled, and should not make admission or study decisions based on a cancellation that has not happened. No individual student is named in connection with the inquiry in this guide, and students should not name, blame, or share personal details about any classmate online, since unverified naming can harm innocent people and spread false information.

Students should also be careful about messages that claim to know the inquiry outcome in advance, promise a changed result for a fee, or ask for personal documents through unofficial channels. No genuine board process works through random social media accounts or paid middlemen, and any such message should be treated as suspicious. If a message makes a strong claim, check whether it names an official notification, an official website, or a named official source, and if it does not, do not forward it. How to Verify News About the Inquiry

The safest way to verify news is to look for the primary source behind the story, which in this case means an official notification, a statement from the Universities and Boards Department, or a report from the board itself. A news story that quotes a notification date, a named official, and a clear committee should carry more weight than a screenshot with no source, a voice note with no name, or a post that says only that someone heard something. When two sources disagree, wait for the official version rather than choosing the more dramatic one, because drama travels faster than accuracy.

Students can also ask a teacher, a school principal, or a college office to help check a claim, since schools often receive official circulars and can explain what a notification actually says. Keep a small note of verified facts with their dates, such as the 23 October order and the 30-day report timeline, and compare every new claim against that note before reacting. If a claim cannot be matched to any official source, treat it as unconfirmed and keep studying normally. For ongoing study, STBB books and solved notes remain your reliable base.

What Students Should Do Right Now

Right now, the most useful action for students is to keep their own academic record in order and continue their studies without interruption. Keep your result card, admit card, and any official slips in one safe folder, and keep a copy or photograph of each document in a safe place at home, so that you can produce them quickly if any official process ever asks for them. If you are preparing for the next class, a supplementary paper, or an admission test, stay on your timetable, because pausing preparation on the basis of rumours only creates a real disadvantage later.

If you have a specific question about your own result, raise it through your school or college office, which can guide you to the correct board procedure, rather than through social media groups where answers are often guesses. Parents can help by checking news together with their children and by discouraging the forwarding of unverified posts in family groups. Students who feel anxious about the inquiry should talk to a teacher or a trusted adult, because worry shared early is easier to manage than worry carried alone. Avoiding Rumours and Protecting Classmates

Rumours about board results spread quickly because they touch real hopes and fears, but every forwarded rumour makes the situation harder for students, teachers, and officials alike. Before forwarding any post about the inquiry, ask yourself three questions: does it name an official source, does it match the verified timeline, and would sharing it help any student make a better decision. If the answer to any of these is no, the kindest and most responsible action is not to share it, and to tell the sender politely that the claim is still unverified.

Protecting classmates is part of the same responsibility, because naming or suspecting a student without evidence can damage a reputation that took years to build. No student should be discussed, photographed, or accused online in connection with an inquiry unless an official source has named them, and this guide names no student at all. Focus instead on what you can control: your own documents, your own preparation, and your own calm response to news. That steady approach serves students far better than speculation, and it keeps the whole student community safer.
