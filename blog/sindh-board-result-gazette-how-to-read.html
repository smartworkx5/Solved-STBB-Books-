How to Read a Sindh Board Result Gazette: Roll Numbers, Marks and Ungraded

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 1748 words · 9 min read

A result gazette can look intimidating at first, with page after page of roll numbers and marks, but it is simply the board's official printed record of who passed which papers, and any student can learn to read it in a few minutes. This guide uses verified facts to explain gazettes clearly, including the Board of Intermediate Education Karachi 178-page Part I gazette, how roll numbers and marks are listed, and what the under-40 percent Ungraded rule means. Whether you are checking your own entry or helping a classmate understand theirs, this guide will help you read a gazette accurately and calmly.

What a Result Gazette Actually Is

A result gazette is the official document published by a board alongside or after a result announcement, listing candidates by roll number together with the marks or result status for their papers. Unlike a single online result page, which shows only one student's marks, a gazette covers many candidates in a structured list, so schools, colleges, and students can verify results in a single authoritative document. The BIEK Part I gazette example shows the scale involved: a 178-page gazette was published for the Pre-Medical group, giving roll numbers and marks of candidates who passed one or more papers, which is why reading it systematically matters.

Gazettes are published by the board itself, and they should be treated as more reliable than screenshots, forwarded lists, or social media summaries, which can be edited, incomplete, or simply wrong. When you read a gazette, you are reading the board's own record, so the entry you find for your roll number carries official weight, even if it differs from a rumour you heard earlier. Keep in mind that a gazette may list only candidates who passed one or more papers, depending on the board's format, so an absent entry needs careful interpretation rather than panic. You can find general board guidance on the blog and about us pages.

Finding Your Roll Number Step by Step

To find your entry, start with your exact roll number as printed on your admit card, and check the gazette section for your group and examination, since gazettes are organised by group and paper rather than by name. In the BIEK Part I example, the gazette covers groups including Science Pre-Medical, Science General, Commerce Regular and Private, Arts Regular and Private, Home Economics, and special candidates, so confirming your group first prevents searching in the wrong section. Scan the roll numbers in order, slowly and carefully, because roll numbers that differ by one digit are easy to confuse when you are nervous.

Once you find a matching roll number, read the full row across before drawing any conclusion, because a gazette row contains marks or status for several papers, and reading only the first mark can mislead you. Write down every mark and status from your row in a notebook, double-check each digit against the gazette, and compare the result with your online result if one is available, since both should agree. If you cannot find your roll number, note the group and gazette you checked, and ask your school or college office for guidance, rather than assuming the worst. Understanding Marks in a Gazette Row

A gazette row typically shows, for each paper, either the marks obtained or a status indicating the outcome for that paper, and students should read each paper's entry separately rather than adding marks into a single judgement. A strong mark in one paper does not cancel a weak mark in another, and a weak mark in one paper does not erase strong marks elsewhere, so the honest picture comes from the full row. Some gazettes list marks for papers passed, while others use codes or statuses for particular outcomes, so students should look for any key, heading, or note printed at the start of the gazette that explains its format.

If a mark looks surprising, first verify that you are reading the correct row and the correct paper column, because column mistakes are the most common cause of false alarm when reading a gazette. Compare the gazette entry with your online result on the board website, such as biek.edu.pk for BIEK results, and with any result card issued by your school, since all official sources should agree. If a genuine difference remains after careful checking, raise it through your school or college office, which knows the board's correction procedure, rather than through social media. The blog offers further result guidance.

What Ungraded Means Under the New Rule

The most important new term for students reading recent Sindh gazettes is Ungraded, which comes from a new grading system decided by the Sindh Universities and Boards Department and the Inter Board Committee of Chairmen. Under this rule, a candidate scoring less than 40 percent in a paper is declared Ungraded and is not considered to have passed that paper, which means that paper must be addressed through the board's next procedure rather than counted as a pass. BIEK applied this rule in its Intermediate Part I Annual Examinations 2026 results, announced on 26 October 2026, and the same under-40 percent Ungraded rule applies to Class IX Part-I this year in the Benazirabad board context.

Students should understand Ungraded calmly and practically: it identifies the specific paper that needs another attempt or further action, and it does not erase passes in other papers. If your gazette row shows Ungraded for a paper, note that paper clearly, ask your school office promptly what the next step is, and begin revising that subject chapter by chapter from your textbook with solved practice. Do not hide an Ungraded status from teachers or family, because early guidance leads to a clear plan, while delay only shortens preparation time. You can revise with STBB books and solved notes for that paper.

Common Gazette Mistakes and What to Do Next

Common gazette mistakes include reading another student's row because of a single wrong digit, reading the wrong group section, mistaking one paper's column for another, and assuming that an absent entry automatically means failure without checking the gazette's format. Each of these mistakes can be avoided by the same simple habits: use the exact admit-card roll number, confirm the group section first, read the full row slowly, and check any heading or key that explains the gazette format. If you are still unsure after careful reading, ask a teacher to check the entry with you, since a second pair of eyes catches digit errors quickly.

After reading your gazette entry correctly, the next step depends on the row itself: if all papers are passed, plan your next class with revision of weak areas; if one paper is Ungraded or needs another attempt, ask your school office about the procedure and start targeted revision at once. Keep a written copy of your gazette row with your result card and admit card in one safe folder, so that you can refer to it for admissions or future procedures without searching again. A gazette read carefully is not a source of fear, but a clear, official map of exactly where you stand. For further help, visit the blog and about us.

5 key points you must know

  • A gazette is the board's official printed record, listing candidates by roll number with marks or status per paper.
  • BIEK published a 178-page Part I gazette for the Pre-Medical group, with roll numbers and marks of candidates passing papers.
  • Find your entry by confirming your group section first, then scanning roll numbers slowly using your admit-card number.
  • Read the full row across, since each paper has its own mark or status and one paper does not decide the whole row.
  • Under 40 percent in a paper means Ungraded, not passed, under the new rule applied in BIEK Part I and Benazirabad Class IX.
