English Essay: Role of Media in Modern Life

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-01 · 1515 words · 8 min read

The english essay role of media topic is highly relevant today, because television, newspapers, radio and the internet shape what millions of people know, think and discuss every day. Media is called the fourth pillar of the state because, after the legislature, executive and judiciary, it informs citizens and checks power. This article gives you an outline, a full model essay and exam tips. More study material is on the [Solved Notes](/solved.html) page and in the [books](/books.html) collection.

Media as Teacher, Watchdog and Entertainer

Media teaches us daily: newspapers explain government decisions, television shows documentaries about science and history, and the internet offers lessons, lectures and libraries to students in every village. During examinations, students use educational programmes and online material to revise difficult topics, and farmers learn about weather, crops and markets from radio and television. In this way, media has become a classroom without walls, open day and night to anyone willing to learn.

Media also acts as a watchdog: it reports corruption, injustice and negligence, and forces authorities to answer the public. When a road is broken, a hospital lacks medicine, or an official misuses power, media coverage brings the issue to public attention and often leads to action. At the same time, media entertains: dramas, films, music and sports programmes relax tired minds after work and study. The danger arises when media spreads fake news, exaggerates events for ratings, or fills young minds with useless content, so responsible use of media is as important as media itself.

  • Teacher: news, documentaries, online lessons
  • Watchdog: reports injustice and corruption
  • Entertainer: dramas, music, sports
  • Danger: fake news and exaggeration
  • Responsible use: verify before believing

Essay Outline: Role of Media in Modern Life

Organise this essay by the three roles: teacher, watchdog and entertainer, then a paragraph on dangers and responsible use, ending with a balanced conclusion. This three-roles structure is easy to remember and gives you three strong body paragraphs without repetition. Start by defining media and naming its main forms: newspapers, television, radio and the internet, including social media. Then devote one paragraph to each role with a concrete example, so the examiner sees understanding, not memorised praise.

The dangers paragraph is essential for a high score, because it shows balanced thinking. Mention fake news, the habit of believing every forwarded message, and the waste of time on useless content, then advise readers to verify news from reliable sources before sharing it. End by saying that media, used honestly and wisely, strengthens democracy, knowledge and national unity, while misuse harms all three. A balanced conclusion always scores higher than one that only praises or only criticises.

  • Introduction: meaning and forms of media
  • Body 1: media as teacher
  • Body 2: media as watchdog
  • Body 3: media as entertainer
  • Body 4: dangers and responsible use
  • Conclusion: honest media, wise readers

Model Essay: Role of Media in Modern Life

Media plays a very important role in modern life, and it includes newspapers, television, radio and the internet, which reach millions of people every day. Media is called the fourth pillar of the state, because it informs citizens, educates society and watches over those in power. In the morning, a newspaper tells us about national and world events; in the evening, television shows news, documentaries and discussions; and at any hour, the internet brings information to our mobile phones within seconds. Without media, people would live in ignorance of their own country and the world, and democracy itself would become weak.

First, media is a great teacher. Students learn from educational programmes, documentaries and online lessons, farmers learn about weather and crops, and citizens learn about their rights, laws and duties. Second, media is a faithful watchdog: it reports corruption, injustice and negligence, and brings public problems to the attention of the government, which often leads to quick action. Third, media is a source of entertainment: dramas, films, music and sports matches relax our minds after hard work and bring families together in the evening. In these three roles, media serves every age group and every section of society.

Model Essay: Conclusion and Useful Vocabulary

However, media also has dangers that we must recognise. Fake news spreads quickly on social media, and many people believe and forward messages without checking their truth, which creates fear, hatred and confusion. Some channels exaggerate events for ratings, and some programmes waste the precious time of students with useless content. The solution is responsible media and wise readers: journalists must report truthfully, and readers must verify news from reliable sources before believing or sharing it. In conclusion, media is a powerful force in modern life, and if it remains honest and its readers remain careful, it will continue to educate, protect and unite our nation.

Useful vocabulary includes: pillar for a main support, democracy for rule by the people, watchdog for one who guards against wrongdoing, and exaggerate for making something seem larger than it is. Use the phrase fourth pillar once, explained simply, rather than repeating it. When writing about social media, be specific: mention forwarded messages and the need to verify, because concrete detail always beats vague warnings in an essay.

  • pillar — a main support of a structure
  • democracy — rule by the people through voting
  • watchdog — one who guards against wrongdoing
  • exaggerate — make something seem larger than true
  • reliable — worthy of trust

Exam Technique for This Essay

Good essays are planned before they are written, and this topic is no exception. Spend the first five minutes making a short outline on the rough-work page: introduction, three or four body points in a sensible order, and a conclusion. This small habit prevents repetition, keeps your ideas in order, and helps you finish on time. In the examination hall, write neatly, leave a clear space between paragraphs, and check your spelling at the end if time allows. A clean, well-organised answer always scores better than a longer, confused one, even when both students know the same facts.

Practise by writing this essay once at home without looking at your notes, then compare your draft with the outline in this article and improve the weak paragraphs. Learn five or six useful words for this topic and use them naturally, not forced into every sentence. Avoid copying long sentences you do not understand, because examiners reward clear, original English. Read your essay aloud once before submitting it in practice: if a sentence sounds strange when spoken, rewrite it in simpler words. With one careful practice essay a week, your speed, confidence and marks will all improve steadily.

  • Use the three roles: teacher, watchdog, entertainer
  • Give one concrete example per role
  • Add a dangers paragraph for balanced thinking
  • Advise verifying news before sharing it
  • End balanced: honest media plus wise readers

Frequently asked questions

What does fourth pillar of the state mean?

It means media stands beside the legislature, executive and judiciary as a support of democracy. It informs citizens and checks power, so people can vote and act wisely. Explain it in one simple sentence, as in the model essay.

How do I write about social media without being negative only?

Give both sides: social media spreads lessons and news quickly, but also spreads fake news when users forward messages without checking. End with responsible use, which shows balanced and mature thinking.

How long should this essay be in the examination?

For Classes 9 and 10, about 250 to 300 words is a safe length. That is usually five to seven paragraphs: one introduction, three or four body paragraphs and one conclusion. Quality matters more than length, so do not pad your essay with repeated sentences just to make it longer.

Can I change the model essay in my own words?

Yes, and you should. Use the model essay as a guide for structure and ideas, then rewrite it in your own simple English. Examiners reward original writing, so keep the outline, change the examples where you can, and add one personal observation from your own life. You can find more study guides on the blog page.
