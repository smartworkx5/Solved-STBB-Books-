A November Revision Plan Using Your STBB Books and Solved Notes

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 1632 words · 8 min read

November is an ideal month for a structured revision plan, because the school year has covered enough chapters to revise meaningfully, supplementary examinations are still under way for some students, and a clear four-week plan can lift every subject before the next tests arrive. This plan is built entirely around your STBB books and solved notes, using no invented dates or board data, and it works whether you are revising for class tests, preparing for a supplementary paper, or simply strengthening weak chapters. Follow it week by week, adjust it to your own subjects, and let steady daily work do the heavy lifting.

Week One: Map Your Subjects and Weak Chapters

Week one begins not with heavy study, but with an honest map of where you stand, because revision without a map usually repeats strong chapters and avoids weak ones. For each subject, list every chapter you have studied so far, and mark each one as strong, fair, or weak, using your class tests, homework, and your own sense of understanding as evidence. Then list the chapters that link to future topics, especially in science and mathematics, because those chapters deserve priority even if they feel only fair rather than weak, since later work will build on them.

Once the map is complete, set a simple weekly timetable that gives extra blocks to weak chapters while keeping a short daily slot for strong ones, so that strong chapters stay fresh without consuming the week. Gather your STBB book, solved notes, and past class papers for each subject in one place, and keep a revision notebook where you will record what you complete each day. A map made carefully in week one saves hours of wasted effort later, and it turns revision from a vague worry into a visible plan. You can start with STBB books and solved notes for every subject.

Week Two: Deep Revision of Weak Chapters

Week two is the deepest revision week, devoted mainly to the weak chapters identified in week one, worked through one chapter at a time from your STBB book. For each weak chapter, read the lesson carefully, work through its solved exercises in writing, and then close the book and write the key points, formulas, or steps from memory, correcting what you missed at once. This active cycle of reading, writing, and correcting builds real understanding far faster than re-reading alone, and it reveals exactly which parts of a chapter still need a teacher's explanation.

End each study block by solving two or three additional questions from the chapter, including at least one longer answer in full, because board-style papers reward complete answers rather than short remembered points. Keep a mistake notebook for errors you make during week two, and review it briefly each evening, since those mistakes are the most valuable revision material you own. If a chapter remains unclear after two honest attempts, ask a teacher for help in week two itself, rather than carrying the confusion into later weeks. The blog offers revision methods, and about us explains our notes.

Week Three: Timed Practice and Past-Style Papers

Week three shifts the emphasis from learning chapters to performing under examination conditions, because knowing a chapter and answering it well in limited time are two different skills. Set aside timed sessions in which you attempt a full set of questions from one subject without notes, following the same time limit and answer format you would face in a real paper. After each timed session, mark your answers honestly, note where time ran short, and practise those question types again, since time management improves only through timed practice itself.

During week three, also practise presentation, including clear headings, numbered steps, labelled diagrams where needed, and neat handwriting, because presentation can earn marks that knowledge alone cannot. Review your mistake notebook daily, and revisit any chapter whose timed answers were weak, using your solved notes to correct the method rather than simply memorising the answer. By the end of week three, you should feel the difference in speed and confidence, and you should know exactly which two or three topics still need final attention in week four.

Week Four: Quick Review, Confidence and Rest

Week four is for consolidation, not for starting new material, and its motto should be short, sharp, and calm. Review your short notes, formulas, and mistake notebook each day, solve one or two questions per subject to keep your writing hand active, and revisit only the small list of topics that remained weak after week three. Avoid the temptation to open a brand-new chapter in week four, because new material at this stage usually adds anxiety without adding marks, while a confident review of known material protects the marks you have already earned.

Week four also needs proper rest, normal meals, and a regular sleep routine, because the benefits of three weeks of revision are protected by a rested mind and lost by exhaustion. Keep your study blocks shorter in week four, take proper breaks, and spend time with family without guilt, since a calm student recalls and organises answers better than a tired one. On the final day of the plan, list what you revised across all four weeks, note your remaining questions for a teacher, and begin the next month with a clear sense of progress. You can review with STBB books and solved notes in short daily sessions.

Making the Plan Fit Your Own Subjects

No single plan fits every student exactly, so adapt this four-week structure to your own subjects, class level, and examination situation without changing its core logic of mapping, deep revision, timed practice, and consolidation. A science student may need more time for numerical practice and diagrams, while a language student may need more time for reading, writing, and vocabulary, and both adaptations are correct as long as weak chapters get priority. If you are also sitting a supplementary paper during November, give that paper's chapters first priority within each week, while keeping short slots for other subjects so they do not fall behind.

Track your plan daily in a simple notebook, writing what you completed, what remains, and what you will do tomorrow, because a written record keeps the plan honest and shows progress that memory alone would miss. If you fall behind by a day, do not abandon the whole plan, but adjust the next day's blocks and continue, since a flexible plan that survives is worth more than a perfect plan that collapses. Share your plan with a teacher or a parent if that helps you stay accountable, and celebrate each completed week before starting the next. The blog and about us pages offer further planning help.

5 key points you must know

  • Week one maps every chapter as strong, fair, or weak, so revision targets weak and linked chapters first.
  • Week two revises weak chapters deeply through reading, written solved practice, and correction from memory.
  • Week three practises full answers under timed conditions, including presentation, headings, and diagrams.
  • Week four consolidates with short notes, the mistake notebook, proper rest, and no brand-new chapters.
  • Adapt the plan to your subjects and any supplementary paper, and track progress daily in a revision notebook.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start a November revision plan with STBB books?

Start in week one by mapping every chapter you have studied so far, marking each as strong, fair, or weak using class tests, homework, and your own understanding. Then build a timetable that gives extra time to weak chapters and chapters that link to future topics, while keeping a short daily slot for strong chapters. Gather your STBB book, solved notes, and a revision notebook in one place before the deep revision weeks begin.

What should week two of the revision plan focus on?

Week two should focus on deep revision of weak chapters, one chapter at a time, using your STBB book and solved notes. Read the lesson, work through its solved exercises in writing, close the book, and write key points from memory, correcting what you missed at once. End each block with two or three extra questions, keep a mistake notebook, and ask a teacher promptly about any chapter that stays unclear.

Why does week three use timed practice?

Timed practice matters because knowing a chapter and answering it well in limited time are different skills, and time management improves only by practising under time pressure. In week three, attempt full question sets without notes, mark them honestly, and practise the question types where time ran short. Also practise presentation, including headings, steps, labelled diagrams, and neat handwriting, since presentation itself can earn marks.

Should I study new chapters in the final week of the plan?

No, the final week should consolidate rather than introduce new material, because a brand-new chapter at that stage usually adds anxiety without adding marks. Review short notes, formulas, and your mistake notebook, solve one or two questions per subject daily, and revisit only the small list of topics that stayed weak after timed practice. Proper rest, normal meals, and regular sleep in week four protect everything you revised in the earlier weeks.

How do I adapt the plan if I have a supplementary paper in November?

Give the supplementary paper's chapters first priority within each week of the plan, while keeping short daily slots for other subjects so they do not fall behind. Map the supplementary chapters first, revise them deeply in week two, practise them under timed conditions in week three, and consolidate them with short notes in week four. Track progress daily, adjust the next day if you fall behind, and do not abandon the whole plan over one missed day.
