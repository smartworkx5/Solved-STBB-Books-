Shaheed Benazirabad Board 9th Class Result 2026 Announced — Check by Roll Number, SMS & Gazette

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 2405 words · 12 min read

Class 9 students of the Shaheed Benazirabad division have their result. The Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education Shaheed Benazirabad (BISE SBA), based in Nawabshah, announced the results of the Annual Examinations 2026 for Class IX (SSC Part-I) Science and General Groups on 31 October 2026. The announcement was made with the approval of Chairman Dr Asif Ali Memon and Controller of Examinations Shah Muhammad Farooq Rashdi, and was reported the same evening by the Associated Press of Pakistan (APP) through UrduPoint. This is the board's own Class 9 annual result: it is not the Karachi result, which was announced by a different board on 28 October, and it is not an intermediate result. Students can check their result on the board's official website, www.bisesba.edu.pk, and through the board's Android application, according to the board officials quoted in the announcement report. This guide explains exactly what was announced, how to check your result by roll number step by step, what the new grading rule means for your marks, and what to do next whether you passed all your papers, passed some of them, or need to appear again.

The announcement: BISE Shaheed Benazirabad Class 9 result, 31 October 2026

The Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education Shaheed Benazirabad announced the results of its Annual Examinations 2026 for Class IX Science and General Groups on 31 October 2026. The announcement covers the SSC Part-I (Class 9) annual examinations conducted by the board for students in its jurisdiction in Sindh, centred on Nawabshah. The board's chairman, Dr Asif Ali Memon, and its Controller of Examinations, Shah Muhammad Farooq Rashdi, approved the result, according to the APP report carried by UrduPoint on the evening of 31 October 2026.

This announcement arrived later than many students expected. Sindh's boards do not announce on a single shared date the way Punjab's boards do; each Sindh board declares its result when its own marking and tabulation are complete. Karachi's secondary board, BSEK, announced its Class 9 Science Group result on 28 October 2026, and Shaheed Benazirabad followed three days later with its own Class 9 result. Students should therefore check only the Shaheed Benazirabad board's own website and app, not another board's portal, because each board holds only its own students' records. Our earlier post on the Karachi Board 9th Class Result 2026 explains how a neighbouring Sindh board's announcement worked, but your roll number will only work on the BISE SBA system.

No position-holder names were given in the announcement report for this Class 9 result, so this post names none. If the board publishes a separate position-holder list, it will appear through the board's own channels. The headline figures, however, are clear and come straight from the board's released results: the Science Group pass percentage is 79.02 per cent, and the General Group pass percentage is 52 per cent. The sections below break those figures down, explain the new grading system that applies to this year's Class 9 examinations, and walk you through checking your own result.

How to check your result online by roll number

The simplest way to check your result is on the board's official website, www.bisesba.edu.pk. The board officials quoted in the announcement report confirmed that results can be checked through the official website and the board's Android application. Keep your roll number slip or admit card beside you before you start, because the portal asks for your roll number, and a single wrong digit will show no record or somebody else's result. If the website is slow in the first hours after the announcement, wait a few minutes and try again rather than re-entering different numbers; heavy traffic on result day is normal and does not mean your result is missing.

Follow these steps in order. First, open the board's official website, www.bisesba.edu.pk, on your phone or computer. Second, look for the results section on the homepage and choose the SSC Part-I (Class IX) Annual Examination 2026. Third, enter your roll number exactly as printed on your admit card. Fourth, submit the form and wait for your result card to load, then read every subject's grade carefully and note any paper marked Ungraded, which is explained in the grading section below. Fifth, save or print a copy of the online result for your own record, and collect your original result card from your school when the board issues it, because the online printout is for information while the school-issued card is your formal record.

  • Official website: www.bisesba.edu.pk, plus the board's Android application
  • You need your roll number exactly as printed on your admit card
  • Choose SSC Part-I (Class IX) Annual Examination 2026, not any other class or year
  • Save or print the online result, and collect your original result card from your school

SMS, app and gazette: the other ways to check

If you cannot open the website, the board's Android application is the second official route named in the announcement report. Install the board's app, open its results section, and enter your roll number in the same way as on the website. The app and the website draw on the same board record, so a result shown on one should match the other; if the two ever disagree, trust the board's website and ask your school to confirm, because a typing error in one entry is the usual cause of a mismatch.

Result portals for this board also list an SMS method: type your roll number and send it to 800299. That code is reported by result portals rather than by the APP announcement report, so confirm the code on the board's own website or app before you send any message, and never pay anyone who offers to fetch your result for a fee. The board also publishes a result gazette, which is the complete published list of results; your school can help you look up your roll number in the gazette if the online portal is unreachable. Whichever method you use, check the result once carefully, write your grades down, and keep the copy safe for admission and record purposes.

Key statistics: Science Group 79.02 per cent, General Group 52 per cent

The Science Group is by far the larger of the two groups in this announcement. A total of 52,260 candidates registered for the Science Group examinations, of whom 51,546 appeared. Of those who appeared, 41,425 students were declared successful, giving an overall pass percentage of 79.02 per cent. The board's breakdown shows that the 41,425 successful candidates passed all subjects, while other candidates passed some papers: 4,353 passed six subjects, 1,517 passed five subjects, 1,380 passed four subjects, 704 passed three subjects, 342 passed two subjects, and 286 passed only one subject. If you passed some papers but not all, you have not failed outright; your passed papers stand, and the remaining papers are the ones to plan for next, as explained in the final guidance section.

The General Group is very small in this board's Class 9 entry. A total of 50 candidates registered for the General Group examinations, and all 50 appeared. Of these, 26 students were declared successful, recording an overall pass percentage of 52 per cent. Because the General Group entry is so small, its percentage moves sharply with just a few students either way, so it should not be compared directly with the Science Group percentage as if the two groups were the same size. Taken together, the announcement shows a strong Science Group outcome and a much smaller General Group cohort, and both groups are covered by the same new grading rule described next.

  • Science Group: 52,260 registered, 51,546 appeared, 41,425 passed all subjects, 79.02% pass rate
  • General Group: 50 registered, 50 appeared, 26 successful, 52% pass rate
  • Science partial passes: 4,353 passed six subjects, 1,517 five, 1,380 four, 704 three, 342 two, 286 one

The new grading system and what your result means

This year's Class 9 examinations follow a new grading system introduced under a decision of the Government of Sindh's Universities and Boards Department. Under the new system, a candidate securing less than 40 per cent marks in a paper will be declared Ungraded in that paper and will not be considered successful in it. That is the key change to understand when you read your result: a paper marked Ungraded is a paper you have not passed, even if your overall total looks reasonable, and it is the paper you must deal with in your next step.

Read your result subject by subject, not just as a total. If every paper shows a grade, you have passed all subjects and can move on to Class 10 with your school's admission process. If one or more papers show Ungraded, note exactly which subjects they are, because your plan depends on those subjects alone; your passed papers do not need to be repeated. Students sometimes panic when they see a single Ungraded paper, but the system is designed so that you can address the remaining papers through the board's next examination opportunity rather than repeating the whole class. Your school's examination office can confirm which route applies to you and the exact dates and fees.

The bi-annual (supplementary) session is the route for supplementary and improvement papers, so a student with Ungraded papers should ask their school immediately about registration for it, rather than waiting until the session has started. What to do next: rechecking, result card and supplementary exams

First, collect your result card. The online result tells you your grades, but your school-issued result card is the document you will need for Class 10 admission and for your academic record, so ask your school when it will be available and check that your name, roll number, and subject grades on it match the online result. If you spot a spelling mistake in your name or a mismatch in a grade, report it to your school at once so the correction can be raised with the board; small errors are far easier to fix early than months later.

Second, decide about rechecking only after reading your result carefully. If a paper's grade looks far below what you expected, ask your teacher whether rechecking is open for this examination and what it costs and covers, because rechecking re-examines the marking of that paper rather than re-teaching the subject. If instead you have Ungraded papers, the practical route is usually the board's bi-annual (supplementary) session, confirm the registration deadline with your school, because missing it can cost you a whole session.

Third, plan your Class 10 year around the subjects that need work. Students who passed everything should start Class 10 reading early, while students retaking papers should split their time between keeping up with Class 10 and revising the Ungraded subjects, with a teacher's help to prioritise. Either way, keep your admit card, your printed online result, and your result card together in one folder, and do not share your roll number publicly or pay any agent who claims to change a result, because no genuine result change happens outside the board's own rechecking and examination process.

  • Collect your school-issued result card and check it against the online result
  • Ask your teacher about rechecking if a grade looks far below your expectation
  • Ask your school now about bi-annual registration for any Ungraded papers
  • Never pay an agent who claims to change a result; only the board's own process counts
