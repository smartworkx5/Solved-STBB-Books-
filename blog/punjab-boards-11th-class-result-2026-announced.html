Punjab Boards 11th Class Result 2026 Announced — Check by Roll Number, SMS & Gazette

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-23 · 1757 words · 9 min read

The wait is over for first-year students across Punjab. All nine Boards of Intermediate and Secondary Education in the province — Lahore, Gujranwala, Faisalabad, Multan, Rawalpindi, Sargodha, Sahiwal, Bahawalpur and Dera Ghazi Khan — announced the HSSC Part-I (11th class) First Annual Examination 2026 result on Thursday, 22 October 2026, at 10:00 AM, on a single shared date coordinated by the Punjab Boards Committee of Chairmen. The results cover every group — FA, FSc Pre-Medical, FSc Pre-Engineering, ICS, I.Com and others — for both regular and private candidates. This guide collects everything in one place: how to check your result by roll number online, the SMS method, the result gazette, every board's pass percentage that has been officially published, and what to do next if you passed, failed, or want your paper rechecked.

The announcement: all nine boards on one day

Every Punjab board declared the Class 11 result on the same morning — Thursday, 22 October 2026, at 10:00 AM Pakistan time. That shared date is not a coincidence: the Punjab Boards Committee of Chairmen (PBCC) sets one common result date each year so that students, colleges and admission offices across the province get results at the same moment. The same joint-date system was used earlier in 2026 for the 12th class result on 23 September.

The announcement covers the HSSC Part-I First Annual Examination 2026 — the first year of college for FA, FSc Pre-Medical, FSc Pre-Engineering, ICS, I.Com, General Science and Humanities students, regular and private. Students should keep their examination roll number ready, because every checking method — online, SMS or gazette — is built around it. The online copy of your result is provisional: your detailed marks card (DMC) arrives through your institution later.

There is no separate official position-holder list for Part-I. Punjab boards issue official medals and position lists at the composite HSSC-II stage, so first-year 'topper lists' circulating on social media are unofficial estimates. Treat only your board's gazette and result portal as authoritative.

How to check your 11th class result online

The fastest and most reliable way to see your result is your board's official result portal. On result day these portals slow down in the first hour because thousands of students arrive at once — if a page does not load at 10:01 AM, wait and try again around 11:00 AM instead of hammering refresh. The following steps work on all nine Punjab board websites.

Before you begin, note that some boards show results under the name 'HSSC Part-I' and others under '1st Year' or 'Intermediate Part-I' — they all mean the same examination. Never trust a result shown on an unofficial website or a WhatsApp forward unless it matches your board's own portal.

  • Step 1: Open your board's official website — for example, BISE Lahore's result portal at result.biselahore.com.
  • Step 2: Find the Results section and select the HSSC Part-I (11th class) Annual 2026 examination.
  • Step 3: Type your roll number exactly as printed on your roll number slip.
  • Step 4: Press Search or View Result and wait for your full marks sheet to appear.
  • Step 5: Check your name, roll number and subject-wise marks, then save a screenshot or print the page.

Check by SMS and result gazette

If the websites are overloaded, the SMS service is the quiet alternative. For BISE Lahore, type your roll number in a text message and send it to 800291 — a reply message comes back with your result, though there can be a delay when thousands of students use the service at once. Boards other than Lahore also run SMS services on result day, but their codes were not published on the boards' own websites this time, so do not trust random codes from unofficial posts — check your own board's official notice for its SMS number before using it.

The third method is the result gazette: the complete official PDF list of every candidate's result, which each board publishes on its own website after the announcement. Download the gazette PDF, open it, and use your reader's search function to find your roll number. The gazette is slower than the portal for one student, but it is the best option when the online portal is down, and it doubles as the official record you can cite for admission queries. Key statistics: pass percentages board by board

Pass percentages moved up in 2026 on several boards, and girls continued to outperform boys. BISE Lahore reported an overall pass percentage of 60.03 per cent — 99,191 passed out of 165,245 who appeared (up from 53.77 per cent in 2025). Among Lahore's female candidates, 68,509 of 100,409 passed (68.23 per cent), against 30,682 of 64,836 boys (47.32 per cent). Group-wise in Lahore, Pre-Engineering led at 79.23 per cent, followed by Pre-Medical at 74.16 per cent, General Science at 61.05 per cent, Commerce at 52.66 per cent and Humanities at 50.17 per cent.

BISE Rawalpindi declared 54.23 per cent overall — 30,280 passed out of 55,834 appeared, a clear recovery from 43.46 per cent in 2025 — with girls at 62.13 per cent and boys at 38.54 per cent. BISE Faisalabad recorded 61.68 per cent (65,178 passed of 105,663 appeared). BISE Multan posted 65.15 per cent (44,547 of 68,375), including 78.25 per cent in Pre-Medical and 76.45 per cent in Pre-Engineering. BISE Bahawalpur reported 53.46 per cent (26,548 of 49,658), with regular candidates at 54.33 per cent and private candidates at 36.62 per cent. BISE Gujranwala and BISE Dera Ghazi Khan were reported at 56.99 per cent and 66.06 per cent respectively, while BISE Sargodha and BISE Sahiwal had not published their official statistics when this article was written.

Remember that a percentage is a board-wide average, not your own story. Thousands of students passed with modest marks and still moved on to strong colleges; the decisive factor now is what you do in 12th class, because the composite HSSC-II aggregate carries the real weight for university and entry-test admissions.

What to do next: rechecking, result card, supplementary exams

First, check your details. Compare your name, father's name, roll number and subject-wise marks against your roll number slip, and flag any mismatch to your institution immediately — corrections get harder later. Your provisional online result is only the first copy; the detailed marks card (DMC) comes through your college or institution.

If you believe a paper was not marked fairly, you can apply for rechecking. Boards usually allow about 15 days from the result date, and applications go through each board's online portal — read your own board's rechecking notice for the exact last date and fee, and apply before the deadline passes. If you failed one or more subjects, you do not wait a full year: the Intermediate supplementary examinations begin on 3 November 2026, giving you a second chance within weeks. Start preparing for the supplementary papers now rather than waiting for the date sheet.

If you cleared everything, turn your full attention to 12th class. The Part-I marks count toward your composite HSSC total, so a strong 12th-class performance can still lift your overall aggregate — especially important for MDCAT and ECAT candidates, where the Part-II weightage dominates.

5 key points you must know

  • All nine Punjab boards announced the HSSC Part-I result on 22 October 2026 at 10:00 AM on a shared PBCC date.
  • Check online by roll number, by SMS (Lahore: 800291), or in the official result gazette PDF.
  • Pass percentages rose on several boards — Lahore 60.03%, Faisalabad 61.68%, Multan 65.15% — with girls outperforming boys.
  • Rechecking applications are usually open for about 15 days on each board's portal; supplementary exams begin 3 November 2026.
