BIEK Supplementary Centres and Reporting Rules Explained

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-02 · 1834 words · 9 min read

More than 21,000 students are sitting the BIEK supplementary examinations across 49 centres in Karachi, so the system depends on every student knowing where to go and when to arrive. The examinations run from 28 October to 21 November 2026, with one afternoon shift each day, and the rules are simple once you understand them: know your centre, arrive by reporting time, carry your admit card, and follow the instructions printed on it. This guide explains centres, reporting, admit cards, and exam-day behaviour in plain language, so you can travel with confidence instead of guessing on the morning of a paper. This guide keeps every fact to what the board has actually announced, and it explains each step in plain language, so you can plan with confidence, avoid common mistakes, and focus on the work that actually matters this week.

49 Centres Across Karachi: Find Yours Early

With 49 examination centres spread across Karachi, your centre is assigned to you: it is not a choice you make on the day. Your admit card and date sheet information tell you where to sit, so check them as soon as you can and do not rely on a friend's centre or a remembered room from a previous examination. Visit the route in advance if possible, or at least study it on a map, and note the journey time at afternoon traffic levels, because a route that looks short in the morning can take longer in the early afternoon.

On the day, aim to reach the centre area early, then enter in good time for reporting. Large centres can be busy at the gate, and finding the right room takes a few minutes even when you know the building. Keep your centre details written down separately from your phone as well, so a dead battery or a lost signal does not leave you stranded. The solved notes and the blog carry general exam guidance, but only your own admit card can confirm your centre for each paper. Use this idea in your own plan, and check it against your own date sheet and admit card, so that nothing important is left to guesswork on the day itself.

  • 49 examination centres across Karachi for the supplementary run
  • Your centre is assigned on your admit card, not chosen on the day
  • Check the route and afternoon journey time in advance

Reporting at 1:30 PM: Why Early Arrival Matters

Reporting time is 1:30 PM, a full half hour before the paper begins at 2:00 PM, and that half hour exists for a reason. It gives staff time to check students in, guide them to rooms, and start the paper on time for everyone. A student who arrives at 2:00 PM has already missed that process and starts under stress, while a student who arrives by 1:30 PM can settle, find the seat, and begin calmly. Treat 1:30 PM as your deadline, not as a suggestion. Remember this as you move to the next step, because steady preparation, clear documents, and enough rest together make a real difference on examination day.

Plan your departure so that normal delays still leave you on time. If you usually need forty minutes, allow an hour. Eat lunch before you leave, carry water if the centre allows it, and keep your documents ready in your hand rather than buried in a bag. Arriving early is one of the simplest ways to protect your marks, because a calm start helps you read questions properly and manage time through the full paper from 2:00 to 5:00 PM. Keep this point in mind as you plan your week, because a calm, organised, and steady approach usually protects both your time and your marks across every paper.

  • Reporting time: 1:30 PM, papers: 2:00 to 5:00 PM
  • Treat reporting time as the deadline, not the paper start
  • Allow extra travel time for afternoon traffic and gate checks

Admit Cards: Where to Get Them and What to Check

Admit cards are available through the board portal at admitcard.biek.edu.pk, using a 16-digit challan number and a matric roll number. Download or collect your card well before your first paper, and check every detail on it: your name, your centre, your subjects, and the instructions printed on the card. If any detail looks wrong, raise it through the proper board or school channel at once, with your documents ready, rather than waiting until examination day. Use this idea in your own plan, and check it against your own date sheet and admit card, so that nothing important is left to guesswork on the day itself.

Keep the admit card safe throughout the run, because you will need it for every paper, not just the first one. Store it in a folder or envelope, keep a note of your key details at home, and do not fold or damage it carelessly. On each paper day, place it with your stationery the night before, so the morning is calm. If the portal asks for details you do not recognise, stop and ask a teacher or parent for help instead of guessing or sharing personal details with unofficial websites.

Which Groups Are Covered in This Run

The supplementary examinations cover HSC Part-I and Part-II students across several groups, including Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, Science General, Commerce, Arts, Home Economics, and special-category candidates. That breadth is why centre arrangements involve so many students and so many rooms: different groups sit different subjects, but they share the same afternoon timing and the same reporting discipline. Your group does not change the reporting time or the need for an admit card. Remember this as you move to the next step, because steady preparation, clear documents, and enough rest together make a real difference on examination day.

Because groups differ, do not copy another student's timetable or centre plan. A Commerce student and a Pre-Medical student may sit on different days, at different centres, even within the same week. Follow only the information issued for you, and check your own date sheet for each subject. If a classmate's plan contradicts your card, trust your card and ask your school or the board channel to clarify, rather than following the classmate and arriving at the wrong place. Keep this point in mind as you plan your week, because a calm, organised, and steady approach usually protects both your time and your marks across every paper.

  • Groups include Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, Science General, Commerce, Arts, and Home Economics
  • Special-category candidates are also covered in this run
  • Different groups may sit different subjects, so follow your own sheet

Exam-Day Behaviour: Simple Rules That Protect You

Exam-day rules are mostly common sense, but they matter. Follow staff instructions, sit in your assigned place, and do not carry anything the hall does not allow. Keep your work your own, do not disturb other students, and raise a hand if you need help or if something in the paper is unclear. If you finish early, use the remaining time to check your answers carefully instead of leaving in a rush, because a second read often catches small mistakes. Use this idea in your own plan, and check it against your own date sheet and admit card, so that nothing important is left to guesswork on the day itself.

After the paper, keep your admit card safe for the next paper and note the next date calmly. Do not argue about the paper at the gate, and do not spread rumours about questions or results. The supplementary run continues until 21 November 2026, and no result date has been announced, so your focus after each paper should be the next one. A steady, respectful routine across the whole run protects your results far better than any shortcut. Remember this as you move to the next step, because steady preparation, clear documents, and enough rest together make a real difference on examination day.

  • Follow staff instructions and sit in your assigned place
  • Keep your work your own and check answers before finishing
  • Keep your admit card safe for every remaining paper

Frequently asked questions

How many BIEK supplementary centres are there?

There are 49 examination centres across Karachi for the supplementary examinations. Your own centre is assigned on your admit card, so check it early and plan your route in advance. Do not follow a friend's centre, because different students may be assigned to different centres.

What time must I report at the centre?

Reporting time is 1:30 PM, with papers running from 2:00 to 5:00 PM. Arrive by reporting time so staff can check you in and guide you to your room. Treat 1:30 PM as your deadline and allow extra travel time for afternoon traffic.

How do I get my admit card?

Admit cards are available at admitcard.biek.edu.pk, using a 16-digit challan number and your matric roll number. Download or collect your card well before your first paper, check every detail, and keep it safe for the whole run. Ask your teacher or school office if anything still looks unclear after checking the official source.

Which groups sit the supplementary exams?

The run covers HSC Part-I and Part-II students in Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, Science General, Commerce, Arts, Home Economics, and special-category candidates. Subject dates differ by group, so always follow your own date sheet rather than another student's timetable. Ask your teacher or school office if anything still looks unclear after checking the official source.

Has a supplementary result date been announced?

No. No supplementary result date has been announced, and a result can only come after the run ends on 21 November 2026. Ignore rumoured dates and check only official board sources for genuine updates after the examinations finish.
