English Word Families: One Root, Many Words

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-04 · 1794 words · 9 min read

One root can teach you many words: from teach come teacher and teaching, from kind come kindness and kindly, and from help come helper and helpful, so learning word families is the fastest honest way to grow English vocabulary. This guide explains how endings and prefixes change meaning, gives families from verbs, feelings, school and work, and shows a family-tree method for weekly practice. Study one family at a time, write a sentence for each new word, and revise through the [solved notes](/solved.html) and [books](/books.html) pages, because words learned as a connected family stay together in memory.

What Is a Word Family?

A word family is a group of words that grow from one root, like teach, teacher and teaching, and once you know the root, you can guess many related words, which is why families are a shortcut to a large vocabulary, as shown in the guides on blog. The family of teach includes teacher, teaching and taught. For example, our teacher uses simple teaching methods in every lesson.. The family of learn includes learner, learning and learned. For example, a good learner enjoys learning something new every day.. The family of write includes writer, writing and written. For example, the writer finished her writing before the evening.. The family of read includes reader, reading and readable. For example, every reader in our class loves reading story books..

Suffixes like er, or and ing change a verb into a person or an activity, so when you meet a new word, ask which root it comes from and which suffix it carries, because that habit lets you understand many words you have never seen before. The family of play includes player, playful and playing. For example, the young player showed playful energy during the match.. The family of help includes helper, helpful and helping. For example, a helpful helper assists the teacher after every lesson.. The family of paint includes painter, painting and painted. For example, the painter finished a beautiful painting of our school.. The family of sing includes singer, singing and sung. For example, the singer practises singing every morning before school..

Families From Everyday Verbs

Everyday verbs give the most useful families, because work, speak, grow and move appear in lessons, letters and stories, and their families cover people, actions and results with only a small change in ending. The family of work includes worker, working and worked. For example, the hard worker finished his working day with a smile.. The family of speak includes speaker, speech and speaking. For example, the speaker gave a clear speech at the school function.. The family of grow includes growth, growing and grown. For example, the growth of the plant shows in its growing leaves.. The family of move includes movement, mover and moving. For example, the movement of the class was quiet and orderly..

More verb families follow the same pattern, so collect them in your notebook under their roots, and revise a family by covering the derived words and trying to recall them from the root alone. The family of act includes action, active and acting. For example, quick action and active habits bring good results.. The family of create includes creation, creative and creating. For example, her creative creation won first prize in the exhibition.. The family of decide includes decision, decisive and deciding. For example, a firm decision helped him finish deciding quickly.. The family of collect includes collection, collector and collecting. For example, the collector showed his collection of old coins proudly..

Families From Feelings and Qualities

Feeling words grow into rich families too, because kind becomes kindness and kindly, happy becomes happiness and unhappy, and care becomes careful and careless, each with a clearly different meaning worth learning. The family of kind includes kindness, kindly and unkind. For example, her kindness showed when she spoke kindly to the new student.. The family of happy includes happiness, unhappy and happily. For example, their happiness grew when they worked happily together.. The family of care includes careful, careless and carefully. For example, a careful student writes carefully and avoids careless mistakes.. The family of friend includes friendly, friendship and unfriendly. For example, true friendship grows when friends behave friendly every day..

Prefixes like un and dis also build families by reversing meaning, so unhappy, disagree and disappear teach you that a small change at the start can turn a word into its opposite, which doubles your vocabulary quickly. The family of agree includes agreement, disagree and disagreement. For example, their agreement ended when they began to disagree politely.. The family of appear includes appearance, disappear and reappear. For example, the bird seemed to disappear and then reappear on the branch.. The family of cover includes uncover, recover and coverage. For example, after rest, the sick student began to recover fully.. The family of hope includes hopeful, hopeless and hopefully. For example, the hopeful student waited hopefully for good news..

Families With Useful and Useless Endings

Endings like ful, less and able change meaning in predictable ways, because useful means full of use, useless means without use, and powerful means full of power, so learning the ending teaches a whole group at once. The family of use includes useful, useless and used. For example, a useful book is better than a useless pile of old papers.. The family of power includes powerful, powerless and powerfully. For example, the powerful river flowed powerfully after the rain.. The family of colour includes colourful, colourless and coloured. For example, the colourful garden looked bright beside the colourless wall.. The family of success includes successful, unsuccessful and successfully. For example, the successful student completed the task successfully..

More families use ness, ity and tion to turn adjectives and verbs into nouns, so darkness, nationality and information name things, qualities and results, and they appear often in essays and formal writing. The family of dark includes darkness, darken and darkly. For example, darkness fell slowly as the sun set behind the hills.. The family of nation includes national, nationality and nationwide. For example, the national team won praise across the whole nation.. The family of inform includes information, informative and informed. For example, the informative book gave us useful information about health.. The family of differ includes difference, different and differently. For example, the difference between the two answers was small but different..

Families for School and Work

School and work families help in applications, essays and formal letters, because employ, govern, educate and manage grow into employer, government, education and manager, words that describe real roles in daily life, with examples on solved notes. The family of employ includes employment, employer and employee. For example, the employer offered employment to the skilled employee.. The family of govern includes government, governor and governing. For example, the government builds schools and the governor visits them.. The family of educate includes education, educator and educated. For example, good education needs dedicated educators and educated parents.. The family of manage includes manager, management and managing. For example, the manager improved management by managing time well..

Finish by building one family tree each week: write the root in the centre, add its family around it, and write one sentence for each new word, because a family tree shows connections that a plain list hides completely. The family of invent includes invention, inventor and inventive. For example, the inventor showed his new invention at the science fair.. The family of celebrate includes celebration, celebrated and celebrating. For example, the celebration marked a celebrated day for the whole school.. The family of quick includes quickly, quickness and quicker. For example, she finished quickly because quickness comes from daily practice.. The family of safe includes safety, safely and unsafe. For example, safety rules help students travel safely to school every day..

5 key points you must know

  • One root can teach many related words.
  • Endings like er, ing and ness change word jobs.
  • Prefixes like un and dis often reverse meaning.
  • Always learn a family member with a sentence.
  • Build one family tree in your notebook each week.

Frequently asked questions

What is a word family?

A word family is a group of words that share one root, such as teach, teacher and teaching, and learning the root plus its endings lets you understand and use many related words instead of memorising each one separately. Keep this answer beside your lesson notes, and read it once more before the next test so the idea stays fresh and ready.

What do endings like er and ness mean?

The ending er often names a person who does an action, as in teacher, while ness often turns an adjective into a noun, as in kindness, and learning these patterns helps you guess the meaning of new words correctly. Use the idea in one real sentence or task today, because using knowledge turns it from a fact into a skill you own.

How can word families help in examinations?

Word families help you understand unseen words in passages, choose the correct form in grammar questions, and write varied sentences, because knowing teach, teacher and teaching gives you three correct choices instead of one. Revise older answers together with new ones, so new learning connects with what you already know instead of replacing it.

Should I memorise every member of a family?

No, learn the root and the two or three most useful members first, use them in sentences, and add rarer members later, because a small well-used family helps more than a large list copied without practice. Stay patient with slow progress, because steady small steps in English build more lasting skill than rushed cramming before tests.

What is a family tree and how do I make one?

A family tree puts the root in the centre and its related words around it with a sentence for each, because seeing the connections on one page shows how words grow and makes weekly revision quick, visual and enjoyable. End each study session by recalling the main point aloud without looking, since recall practice strengthens memory far more than re-reading.
