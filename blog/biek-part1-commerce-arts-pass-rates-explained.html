BIEK Part-I Commerce and Arts Pass Rates Explained

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-03 · 1556 words · 8 min read

When the BIEK Part-I results were announced on 26 October 2026, the headlines naturally focused on the largest groups, but the Commerce and Arts figures tell their own important story. Commerce Regular and Arts Regular recorded the lowest pass-all-papers rates among the main groups, and understanding why those numbers look the way they do matters for students, parents and teachers alike. This article explains the verified figures, shows how the rates are calculated, and clarifies what passed all papers actually means, so that nobody mistakes a partial result for a failure or a pass-all rate for a whole-group failure rate.

The Verified Figures for Commerce and Arts

For Commerce Regular, the verified figures are 23,549 registered, 22,693 appeared, and 9,332 who passed all seven papers. For Arts Regular, the figures are 11,171 registered, 10,678 appeared, and 3,748 who passed all six papers. These are the official counts behind the rates discussed here, and they come from the same 26 October 2026 announcement that covered the Pre-Medical, Science General, Commerce, Arts, Special Candidates and Home Economics groups. Commerce students sit seven papers, which is why their passed-all count refers to seven papers, while Arts students sit six.

It is worth noting the gap between registered and appeared in both groups. In Commerce Regular, about 856 registered students did not appear, and in Arts Regular the gap is about 493. Those students are counted in neither the appeared base nor the passed count, which is why pass rates are calculated against appeared students rather than registered ones. A rate calculated against registered students would look lower and would mix two different things: students who did not sit the examination and students who sat it but did not pass all papers.

How the Rates Are Calculated

The pass-all-papers rate is simply the number of students who passed every paper divided by the number who appeared, multiplied by one hundred. For Commerce Regular, that is 9,332 divided by 22,693, which comes to about 41 percent. For Arts Regular, it is 3,748 divided by 10,678, which comes to about 35 percent. Both of these rates are calculated from the verified figures above, and they are labelled as calculated here because the board's announcement gave counts, and the percentages are arithmetic derived from those counts.

The same calculation for the larger science groups gives useful context. Pre-Medical recorded 11,966 passes out of 24,544 appeared, which is about 49 percent, and Science General recorded 15,036 passes out of 30,386 appeared, which is also about 49 percent. These calculated rates show that Commerce at about 41 percent and Arts at about 35 percent sit below the science groups, but the gap is not enormous, and it certainly does not mean that the remaining students in those groups failed everything. That misunderstanding is the most common one, and the next section explains why.

What Passed All Papers Means, and What It Does Not

Passed all papers means exactly what it says: the student passed every paper in the group, all seven for Commerce Regular and all six for Arts Regular. A student who passed five out of six papers is not counted in the passed-all figure, even though that student passed most of their papers and has only one paper to clear. This is why a pass-all rate of 35 percent does not mean that 65 percent of Arts students failed everything. It means that 65 percent did not pass every single paper, and within that group there is a wide range, from students who missed one paper to students who missed several.

The 40 percent rule sits behind this. Under the rule applied by IBCC and the Sindh Universities and Boards Department, a paper scored under 40 percent is marked Ungraded, meaning that paper is not passed. So a student with five passes and one Ungraded paper is a partial pass, not a full pass, and the supplementary route exists precisely for such students to clear the remaining paper. Parents reading a pass-all rate should therefore ask a second question: how many papers did my child pass, and which ones remain? That question is far more useful than the headline rate alone.

Why Commerce and Arts Rates Differ from Science Groups

There is no single verified explanation for the gap between the groups in the announcement itself, and this article does not invent one. What can be said honestly is that the groups differ in structure: Commerce students must pass seven papers rather than six, which gives one more paper in which to fall below the line, and Arts students come from a wide range of college backgrounds and subject combinations. Subject difficulty, preparation time and college support all vary, and any one of them can move a group's rate by several percentage points. Students should therefore treat the group rate as background context, not as a judgment on their own ability.

A more useful comparison for an individual student is with their own record. If you passed most of your papers, your position is strong and your task is narrow: clear the remaining paper or papers with focused revision. If you missed several papers, your task is broader and deserves a conversation with your teachers about which topics caused the most trouble. Either way, the group rate does not decide your next step. Your own marksheet does, paper by paper, and the solved notes section offers general study help for the subjects you need to strengthen.

Reading Results Responsibly: Advice for Students and Parents

First, read the marksheet, not just the headline. Count your passed papers, identify the Ungraded ones, and keep the marksheet safe with your admit card and result printouts. Second, ask your college what support is available for the papers you need to improve, and whether practice tests or revision classes are offered. Third, avoid comparing your result with group percentages in a way that discourages you, because a group rate describes thousands of students and says nothing about your next paper. Fourth, start revising the remaining papers now, while the material is fresh, rather than waiting for a supplementary notice, since no date for this cohort has been announced in the sources checked.

Finally, remember that the science-group context cuts both ways. Pre-Medical at about 49 percent and Science General at about 49 percent also mean that roughly half of appeared students in those groups did not pass all papers either. Partial passes are common across every group, and the system provides a route forward for them. A calm, paper-by-paper plan, built with your teachers and followed week by week, is the most reliable response to any result, whatever the group rate says. 5 key points you must know

  • Commerce Regular: 9,332 passed all seven papers out of 22,693 appeared, a calculated rate of about 41 percent
  • Arts Regular: 3,748 passed all six papers out of 10,678 appeared, a calculated rate of about 35 percent
  • Pre-Medical and Science General both recorded calculated pass-all rates of about 49 percent
  • Passed all papers excludes partial passes; a student missing one paper is not counted in the figure
  • Under 40 percent in a paper means Ungraded, which means that paper is not passed

Frequently asked questions

What is the Commerce Regular pass rate?

Commerce Regular recorded 9,332 passes out of 22,693 appeared students. Calculated from those verified figures, the pass-all-papers rate is about 41 percent. Commerce students sit seven papers.

What is the Arts Regular pass rate?

Arts Regular recorded 3,748 passes out of 10,678 appeared students. Calculated from those verified figures, the pass-all-papers rate is about 35 percent. Arts students sit six papers.

Does a 35 percent pass-all rate mean 65 percent failed everything?

No. Passed all papers counts only students who cleared every paper. Many of the remaining students passed most of their papers and missed one or a few, and they are partial passes, not total failures.

How do the science groups compare?

Pre-Medical recorded 11,966 passes out of 24,544 appeared, about 49 percent calculated, and Science General recorded 15,036 out of 30,386, also about 49 percent calculated. Both sit above Commerce and Arts, but roughly half their students also did not pass all papers.

What does Ungraded mean for my result?

Under the IBCC and Sindh Universities and Boards Department rule, a paper scored under 40 percent is marked Ungraded, meaning that paper is not passed. Check your marksheet paper by paper to see which papers are Ungraded and which are passed.
