How to Revise for Board Exams in the Last Month

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 802 words · 4 min read

492Study Tips

The last month before board exams is not the time to start new books — it is the time to turn what you already studied into marks. Students who revise in a plan — solved exercises first, then notes, then MCQs, then mixed timed practice — walk into the paper knowing exactly how each answer should look. This guide gives a week-by-week plan for the last month, for students of all four boards.

Week one: finish the syllabus

The first week of the last month has one job: no chapter left unstudied. Work the solved exercises of every chapter you have not yet covered, one chapter a day, in the book's order. Open the Books page, find your book, and work its solved exercises — answering in writing, then comparing with the solved answers.

Do not read new chapters passively. A chapter studied by working its exercises is finished; a chapter only read is a chapter you will not remember in the exam. By the end of week one, every chapter of every subject has been worked at least once.

Week two: notes, error notebook and MCQs

Week two is for depth. Re-read the chapter notes on the Notes page and re-work every question you once got wrong — the ones in your error notebook, if you kept one, and the exercise questions that took two tries. Then test each chapter on the MCQs practice page.

MCQs in week two are diagnostic: every wrong answer points at a fact or a chapter to revisit. Write each missed fact into your error notebook, one line each, and give the weakest chapters one more pass through their solved exercises.

Week three: mixed timed practice

In week three, switch from chapter sets to mixed sets across the whole book, timed. Mixed practice is the closest thing to the exam: it forces you to choose the method, not just apply the chapter you already know you are in. Practise in the exam time limit, with a clock.

After each mixed set, note which chapters still produce errors and give those chapters one more pass through their notes and solved exercises. By the end of week three, the weak chapters should be down to one or two — and those get the final week.

Week four: your own documents, and rest

The final week is for your own documents only: your error notebook, your formula sheets, and your one-page chapter summaries. Nothing new enters the plan — new material in the final week is panic, not preparation. Revise a little each day, with the weakest subjects getting the morning hours.

Stop studying at 9 pm every night and sleep well. A rested student remembers more than a tired one, and the last week is about recall, not new learning. On the exam morning, revise only the summaries — and walk in knowing the plan is finished.

5 key points you must know

  • Week one: finish the syllabus. Work the solved exercises of every chapter you have not yet covered, one chapter a day, in the book's order.
  • Week two: notes and error notebook. Re-read chapter notes, re-work every question you once got wrong, and test each chapter with MCQs.
  • Week three: mixed timed practice. Switch from chapter sets to mixed sets across the whole book, timed, and give weak chapters one more pass.
  • Week four: only your own documents. Revise your error notebook, your formula sheets and your one-page chapter summaries — nothing new.
  • In the final week, stop at 9 pm every night and sleep well. A rested student remembers more than a tired one, and the last week is about recall, not new learning.

Frequently asked questions

What should I study first in the last month of board exam revision?

The notes and solved exercises first: open the Books page for your book, work its solved exercises, and keep the chapter notes from the Notes page beside you. MCQs and past papers come after the concepts are clear.

When should I switch from chapter-wise practice to mixed sets?

Mixed sets in the last two weeks only. Until then, practise chapter by chapter so every error points at the chapter that needs another pass.

What is the single most useful document for last-month revision?

Your error notebook: the mistakes you made in solved exercises and MCQ practice, with the correct fact or method written beside each one. It is the only revision made from your own errors.

Should I start any new chapter in the final week?

Nothing new after the last week. Revise your own summaries, your error notebook and your formula sheets — and sleep well. A rested student remembers more than a tired one.
