Maths Word Problems: Percentage in Marks and Shops

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-04 · 1745 words · 9 min read

Percent means out of one hundred, and percentage problems appear everywhere in school maths: in exam marks, in shop discounts, in price increases, and in pass marks. Students often know that percent means per hundred but still struggle, because percentage problems ask for different things: sometimes the percentage itself, sometimes the amount it represents, and sometimes the original value before a change. The trick is to identify which of these three the question wants, and then use the right method. This guide works through marks, pass marks, increases and decreases with simple numbers you can follow step by step. Percent Means Out of One Hundred

A percentage is a fraction with a denominator of 100, so 25 percent means 25 out of every 100, which is the same as the fraction one quarter. To find a percentage of an amount, multiply the amount by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, 20 percent of 300 is 20 times 300 divided by 100, which is 60. To find what percentage one number is of another, divide the first number by the second and multiply by 100. For example, 15 out of 60 as a percentage is 15 divided by 60 times 100, which is 25 percent. Learning both directions of this skill covers most percentage questions.

It also helps to memorise the common percentage-fraction pairs: 50 percent is one half, 25 percent is one quarter, 75 percent is three quarters, 20 percent is one fifth, and 10 percent is one tenth. These pairs let you solve many problems mentally. Ten percent of any number is found by dividing by 10, so 10 percent of 450 is 45, and 20 percent is double that, which is 90. When a problem uses a friendly percentage like 10, 20 or 25, use the fraction shortcut; when it uses an awkward percentage like 37 percent, use the multiply-and-divide-by-100 method and show your working carefully.

Marks and Pass Marks

Marks problems usually ask for a percentage score: divide the marks obtained by the total marks and multiply by 100. If a student scores 72 out of 90, the percentage is 72 divided by 90 times 100, which is 80 percent. Simplify the fraction first when you can: 72 over 90 simplifies to 4 over 5, which is 80 percent, and simplifying before multiplying keeps the numbers small and avoids errors. If the total marks are 100, the marks themselves are the percentage, which is why many students find tests out of 100 the easiest to read.

Pass-mark problems work backwards: the pass percentage is given, and you must find the minimum marks needed. If a paper is out of 200 marks and the pass mark is 40 percent, the passing marks are 40 percent of 200, which is 80 marks. A student scoring 75 marks would fail by 5 marks, while a student scoring 95 marks would pass by 15 marks. A common trap is confusing the pass percentage with the pass marks themselves; always check whether the question asks for marks or for a percentage, and label your answer clearly so the examiner sees what you found.

Increase Problems: Prices Going Up

An increase problem gives an original value and a percentage increase, and asks for the new value. First find the increase amount, then add it to the original. For example, a book priced at 200 rupees rises by 10 percent. Ten percent of 200 is 20 rupees, so the new price is 220 rupees. A faster method is to multiply the original by 110 percent, which is 1.10 times 200, also giving 220 rupees. Both methods work, but the two-step method is safer for beginners because it shows the increase amount separately, which is often what the question asks for.

Increase problems also appear in marks and populations. If a class of 40 students grows by 25 percent, the increase is 10 students, so the new class size is 50. If a player's score rises from 60 to 75, the increase is 15, and the percentage increase is 15 divided by 60 times 100, which is 25 percent. Notice that the percentage increase is always calculated on the original value, never on the new value. This is the single most tested idea in increase problems, and remembering it will save you from the most common error in this topic.

Decrease Problems: Discounts and Falling Marks

A decrease problem works like an increase problem in reverse: find the decrease amount and subtract it from the original. If a jacket priced at 1000 rupees is reduced by 20 percent, the decrease is 200 rupees, so the new price is 800 rupees. The shortcut is to multiply by 80 percent, which is 0.80 times 1000, also giving 800 rupees. Decrease problems in shops are the same as discount problems, so the skills from money word problems transfer directly here. Always confirm whether the question asks for the decrease amount or the new price, because these are different answers.

A classic exam trap is the double change: a price rises by 10 percent and then falls by 10 percent, and students expect it to return to the original price. It does not. A price of 100 rupees rising by 10 percent becomes 110 rupees, and then falling by 10 percent means subtracting 11 rupees, giving 99 rupees. The second percentage is calculated on the new, higher value, so the fall is bigger than the rise. Remembering that each percentage change applies to the current value, not the original, protects you from this trap and from similar questions about marks and populations.

Common Mistakes in Percentage Problems

The first common mistake is calculating a percentage on the wrong base value, especially in increase and decrease problems where the base changes after the first step. The second is forgetting to multiply by 100 when finding a percentage, giving an answer like 0.8 instead of 80 percent. The third is confusing the percentage with the amount, for example answering 20 percent when the question asked how many rupees the discount was. The fourth is mishandling percentages over 100 percent, which are perfectly valid for increases but impossible for a share of a fixed total like exam marks.

Another frequent error is rounding too early in multi-step problems, which changes the final answer by a mark or two. Keep full numbers through the working and round only at the end, and only if the question asks for rounding. Finally, always write the percent sign when the answer is a percentage, and write rupees or marks when the answer is an amount. A bare number with no unit loses marks even when the calculation is correct. Practise a mix of marks, shop and increase problems every week, and percentages will become a reliable strength. 5 key points you must know

  • Percent means out of 100: find a percentage of an amount by multiplying and dividing by 100.
  • Marks percentage equals marks obtained divided by total marks, times 100.
  • Pass marks equal the pass percentage of the total marks; check marks versus percent carefully.
  • Percentage increase or decrease is always calculated on the original value, not the new one.
  • After a rise and a fall of the same percent, the value does not return to the original.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a percentage of a number?

Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, 20 percent of 300 is 20 times 300 divided by 100, which is 60. For friendly percentages, use fraction shortcuts instead: 10 percent means divide by 10, 25 percent means divide by 4, and 50 percent means divide by 2. Both routes give the same answer, so choose the one that keeps your numbers simplest and show your working.

How do I calculate my percentage score in an exam?

Divide the marks you obtained by the total marks and multiply by 100. If you scored 72 out of 90, the calculation is 72 divided by 90 times 100, which is 80 percent. Simplify the fraction before multiplying when possible, because smaller numbers reduce errors. If the paper is out of 100 marks, your marks are already your percentage, which makes reading the result straightforward.

How do I find pass marks from a pass percentage?

Multiply the total marks by the pass percentage and divide by 100. For a paper out of 200 marks with a pass mark of 40 percent, the passing marks are 40 percent of 200, which is 80 marks. A student scoring below 80 fails, and the shortfall is found by subtracting their marks from 80. Always check whether the question asks for the pass marks or the pass percentage, because these are different answers.

What is the difference between percentage increase and percentage decrease?

Both are calculated on the original value, but an increase is added to the original while a decrease is subtracted from it. A price of 200 rupees rising by 10 percent becomes 220 rupees, while the same price falling by 10 percent becomes 180 rupees. Remember that a rise followed by a fall of the same percent does not return to the original value, because the second change applies to the new, changed value.

Why do percentage answers go wrong so often?

Most errors come from calculating on the wrong base value, forgetting to multiply by 100, confusing a percentage with an amount, or rounding too early in multi-step problems. Students also mix up increase and decrease, or give the change amount when the question asked for the new value. Writing the method first, labelling the answer with percent, rupees or marks, and rounding only at the end will fix nearly all of these mistakes.
