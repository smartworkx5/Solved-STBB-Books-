KPK Board Class 10 Physics MCQs Practice Guide

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 709 words · 4 min read

490Subject Guides

Class 10 Physics is a numerical paper, and its objective section rewards the students who practise: every MCQ has one exact answer, and the same facts and formulas return across sets. KPK Board students who practise MCQs chapter by chapter — after studying the solved exercises — find the objective section the most predictable part of the paper. This guide shows how to practise Class 10 Physics MCQs properly.

The chapters your MCQs come from

The KPK Board Class 10 Physics book covers electrostatics, current electricity, electromagnetism, waves, light, and sound. Each chapter produces MCQs of a recognisable kind: electrostatics tests charges and fields, electricity tests circuits and formulas, and waves and light test diagrams and properties.

Practise a chapter's MCQs only after studying its solved exercises on the KPK Board Class 10 Physics book page. The written exercises teach the concepts and the working; the MCQs then measure whether the facts and formulas are exact.

How to practise on the MCQs page

Work chapter by chapter on the MCQs practice page. For every wrong answer, read the explanation and write the correct fact or formula in an error notebook — one line, in your own words. Physics MCQs repeat their facts across years and sets, so a fact fixed once is a mark earned many times.

For numerical MCQs, work the calculation in writing with units at every step. Most numerical mistakes are unit slips — forgetting to convert centimetres, or mixing up the symbol — and writing the units down catches them before they cost a mark.

Diagrams: waves and light

Waves and light MCQs are picture questions: a ray diagram, a wave sketch, a lens or a mirror. Draw the diagram yourself before choosing an answer — the path of a ray, the shape of a wave, the image formed by a lens. A drawn diagram turns a confusing question into a picture you can read.

Keep labelled sketches of the standard diagrams beside you while practising: reflection, refraction, the human eye, the convex lens. Most diagram-based MCQs ask you to name a part or state what happens next, and a sketch answers both.

Final weeks: mixed timed practice

In the last two weeks, switch from chapter sets to mixed sets across the whole book, timed. Note which chapters still produce errors and give those chapters one more pass through their solved exercises and notes on the Notes page.

On the final day, revise only your error notebook and your formula sheet. Those two documents contain exactly what you personally were getting wrong — the highest-value revision material you own.

5 key points you must know

  • Practise MCQs chapter by chapter — electrostatics, current electricity, magnetism, waves, light, sound — and only mix chapters in the final two weeks.
  • Read the explanation under every answer and write each missed fact as one line in an error notebook. Physics facts and formulas repeat across sets.
  • Keep a formula sheet with every formula, the meaning of each symbol and its unit. Most numerical MCQ mistakes are unit slips.
  • For waves and light, draw the diagram before choosing: ray diagrams and wave sketches turn a confusing question into a picture.
  • For numerical questions, work them in writing with units at every step. A formula practised on three examples stays; a formula only read collapses.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I practise KPK Board Class 10 Physics MCQs?

On the MCQs practice page. Choose the Class 10 Physics book and practise chapter by chapter; every MCQ shows the correct answer with an explanation.

Should I practise MCQs before or after the written exercises?

After. Study the chapter's solved exercises on the book page first so the concepts are clear, then use MCQs to test exactness.

How do I handle numerical MCQs in Physics?

Practise it in writing with units at every step, and keep a formula sheet with the meaning and unit of every symbol. Most numerical MCQ mistakes are unit slips, not concept errors.

What should I revise on the last day for Physics?

Your error notebook and your formula sheet. They hold precisely the facts and formulas you personally missed, which is the most efficient final revision.
