Maths Word Problems: Averages of Marks and Runs

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-04 · 1751 words · 9 min read

An average is a single number that represents a whole group fairly, as if the total had been shared equally among everyone. Average problems appear in exams through class marks, cricket scores, heights, weights and daily earnings, and they test two skills: finding an average from a list, and working backwards to find a missing value from a known average. Both skills use one formula: average equals total divided by how many, and total equals average times how many. This guide explains both directions with class and cricket examples, plus the missing-score problems that students find hardest. More practice is in our [solved notes](/solved.html) and on the [books page](/books.html).

Average Means Sharing Equally

The average of a set of numbers is found by adding all the numbers and dividing by how many numbers there are. If five students score 70, 80, 90, 60 and 100 marks, the total is 400 and the average is 400 divided by 5, which is 80 marks. Think of the average as sharing the total equally: if all five students had scored the same, each would have scored 80. This sharing idea explains why an average must always lie between the smallest and largest values in the set; an average outside that range is impossible and signals a calculation error.

The reverse formula is just as important: total equals average multiplied by how many. If the average of 6 numbers is 25, their total is 150. This reverse form is the key to missing-value problems, because it lets you find a total even when one value is unknown. For example, if the average of 4 numbers is 30, the total is 120, so if three of the numbers are 25, 35 and 30, the missing number is 120 minus 90, which is 30. Practise both directions until switching between them feels natural, because exams test both equally.

Class Averages: Marks of Many Students

Class average problems often combine two groups, such as boys and girls, and ask for the combined average. The correct method is to find each group's total first, add the totals, and divide by the total number of students. For example, 10 boys average 70 marks, giving a total of 700, and 15 girls average 80 marks, giving a total of 1200. The combined total is 1900 and the combined number of students is 25, so the combined average is 76 marks. Notice that 76 is not the simple average of 70 and 80, which would be 75, because the groups have different sizes and the larger group pulls the average towards itself.

A common trap is averaging the averages directly, which only works when the groups are exactly equal in size. Another trap gives the combined average and one group's average and asks for the other group's average. If a class of 30 students averages 72 marks, the total is 2160. If 18 of them average 70 marks, their total is 1260, so the remaining 12 students total 900 marks, averaging 75 marks. Working through totals at every step keeps these problems organised. A table with columns for group, count, average and total makes the method visible and checkable.

Cricket Averages and Runs

Cricket averages work the same way as class averages, but the context makes them more interesting. A batsman's average is the total runs divided by the number of times dismissed, and a bowler's average uses wickets instead. If a batsman scores 40, 60, 20 and 80 in four innings, the total is 200 and the average is 50 runs per innings. If the same batsman then scores 100 in the next innings, the total becomes 300 over 5 innings, and the new average is 60. Notice how one big score lifts the average, and one low score pulls it down, which is why averages change gradually over a long career.

Cricket problems often ask what score is needed to reach a target average. Suppose a batsman averages 45 runs over 10 innings, giving a total of 450 runs, and wants an average of 50 after 11 innings. The required total after 11 innings is 550 runs, so the batsman must score 100 in the next innings. This is the missing-value method applied to cricket: find the required total from the target average, subtract the current total, and the difference is the needed score. The same method answers how many runs a team needs in its last match to reach a tournament average.

Finding a Missing Score

Missing-score problems give an average and all but one value, and ask for the missing value. The method is: multiply the average by the count to get the total, add up the known values, and subtract to find the missing one. For example, the average of 5 test scores is 84, so the total is 420. If four scores are 78, 90, 85 and 82, their sum is 335, so the missing score is 420 minus 335, which is 85. Always verify by recomputing the average with the missing value included: 420 divided by 5 equals 84, confirming the answer.

A harder version asks what score is needed to raise an average to a target. If a student averages 70 over 4 tests, the total is 280, and to average 75 over 5 tests the total must be 375, so the fifth test score must be 95. Students sometimes try to average the averages or guess the difference, but the totals method is exact and reliable. Another variation removes a score and asks for the new average: subtract the removed score from the total, reduce the count by one, and divide again. Keeping totals at the centre of your working handles every variation of this problem type.

Common Mistakes in Average Problems

The first common mistake is averaging the averages of unequal groups directly, instead of combining through totals. The second is forgetting to change the count when a value is added or removed, for example dividing by 5 when there are now 6 values. The third is adding incorrectly when finding the total, a careless slip that ruins an otherwise perfect method; adding the numbers twice, in a different order, catches most slips. The fourth is giving an average that lies outside the range of the data, such as an average of 120 when all scores are below 100, which should trigger an immediate recheck.

Another error is confusing average with the middle value or the most common value, which are different ideas tested in later topics. For now, remember that average means total divided by how many, and that totals are your best tool for missing-value and combined-group problems. Always label your answer with its unit, such as marks or runs, and check that it falls between the smallest and largest values. With totals-based working, average problems become some of the most reliable marks in the paper. More guides are on our blog and about page.

5 key points you must know

  • Average equals total divided by how many; total equals average times how many.
  • Combine groups through their totals, never by averaging the averages directly.
  • A missing value equals the required total minus the sum of the known values.
  • When a value is added or removed, both the total and the count change.
  • An average must always lie between the smallest and largest values in the set.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the average of a set of numbers?

Add all the numbers together and divide by how many numbers there are. For example, the scores 70, 80, 90, 60 and 100 total 400, and dividing by 5 gives an average of 80. Think of the average as sharing the total equally among everyone in the group. Always check that your average lies between the smallest and largest values, because an average outside that range is impossible and signals an error.

How do I combine averages from two groups?

Find each group's total by multiplying its average by its count, add the totals together, add the counts together, and divide the combined total by the combined count. For example, 10 boys averaging 70 give 700, and 15 girls averaging 80 give 1200, so the combined average is 1900 divided by 25, which is 76. Averaging the two averages directly gives 75, which is wrong, because the groups are different sizes.

How do I find a missing score from an average?

Multiply the average by the count to find the total, add up all the known values, and subtract that sum from the total. If the average of 5 scores is 84, the total is 420, and if four scores sum to 335, the missing score is 85. Verify by recomputing the average with the missing value included. The same totals method finds the score needed to reach a target average in the next test or innings.

How does a cricket batting average work?

A batting average is total runs divided by the number of times the batsman has been dismissed. If a batsman scores 40, 60, 20 and 80 in four innings, the total is 200 and the average is 50. To find the score needed to reach a target average, multiply the target average by the new innings count to get the required total, then subtract the current total. One big innings lifts the average, while one low innings pulls it down.

What mistakes should I avoid in average problems?

The most common mistakes are averaging averages of unequal groups, forgetting to update the count when a value is added or removed, and adding the numbers incorrectly when finding the total. Students also sometimes give an average outside the data range or confuse average with the middle value. Working through totals at every step, re-adding in a different order, and checking that the average sits between the minimum and maximum will prevent nearly all errors.
