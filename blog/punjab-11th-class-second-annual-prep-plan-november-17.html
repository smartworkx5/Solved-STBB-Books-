Punjab 11th Class Second Annual: Two-Week Prep Plan Before 17 November

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-03 · 1487 words · 7 min read

While 12th class students sit their papers from 3 November, 11th class students across Punjab have a valuable two-week window before their own second annual theory papers begin on 17 November 2026. Those two weeks are the difference between entering the hall prepared and entering it hoping, but only if they are used with a plan rather than drifted through. This article builds a two-week preparation plan around the verified structure of the series, using BISE Multan date-sheet examples for subject order, and it ends with the practical examinations that follow in December.

The Shape of the 11th Class Series

The 11th class (Part-I) theory papers run from 17 to 28 November 2026 across all nine Punjab boards, Lahore, Rawalpindi, Gujranwala, Faisalabad, Multan, Sargodha, Sahiwal, Bahawalpur and DG Khan, with practical examinations following from 1 to 11 December 2026. Morning papers begin at 9:00 AM and evening papers at 2:00 PM, with Friday evening papers at 2:30 PM. Because the 12th class papers occupy the first half of November, 11th class students can watch the exam season begin around them while keeping their own focus on preparation, and that is an advantage if it is used calmly rather than anxiously.

In the BISE Multan examples, the 11th class order runs: English Compulsory on 18 November, Computer Science on 23 November, Mathematics or Biology on 25 November, and Physics on 27 November. Other subjects fill the days between and around these, and the exact order on your own board's sheet may differ, so treat these examples as a shape to plan around, not as your personal timetable. Write out your own board's full paper list first, then fit the two-week plan below to it.

  • Part-I theory: 17 to 28 November 2026
  • Practicals: 1 to 11 December 2026
  • Morning 9:00 AM, evening 2:00 PM, Friday evening 2:30 PM

Week One: Cover Weaknesses First (3 to 9 November)

The first week of preparation should attack weaknesses, not strengths. List every subject you are appearing in, and under each one write the two chapters or topics you find hardest. Those weak topics get the morning slots of week one, when concentration is highest, while stronger material gets the afternoon. For English, this means practising essays, precis work and grammar items you repeatedly get wrong. For Mathematics, it means working problems from the chapters where your steps break down. For Biology, it means drawing the diagrams you cannot yet reproduce from memory.

By the end of week one, you should be able to say honestly that nothing on your syllabus is completely untouched. That does not mean everything is mastered, only that there are no blank areas left to panic about later. Keep a short daily log of what you revised and which questions you still got wrong, because that log becomes the revision list for week two. Students who study without a log often revise the same comfortable chapters three times while the weak ones stay weak, and week one exists to prevent exactly that.

Week Two: Practice Papers and Exam Conditions (10 to 16 November)

Week two should feel like the exam itself. Sit down with past-paper style questions, a clock, and no notes, and practise full papers or full sections under timed conditions. For English, practise the essay and the comprehension passage within their time limits. For Mathematics and Physics, practise numerical sets and derivations by writing every step, since examiners award marks for method. For Computer Science, practise short questions and programming concepts until definitions come quickly. After each practice session, mark your work honestly and add every repeated mistake to a short error list.

The final two days of week two should taper, not intensify. Revise your error list, read through your formula sheets and definitions, and practise one more short paper for confidence, but do not start any brand-new chapter. The night before 17 November, prepare your roll number slip, stationery and centre route, and get a full night's sleep. Students who arrive rested and organised outperform students who arrive exhausted from a final all-nighter, even when the tired student studied more hours in total.

Subject Order: Using the Multan Examples Wisely

If your board follows the Multan example order, English on 18 November comes first and deserves the heaviest early attention, because it opens your series and sets your confidence. Computer Science on 23 November then gives a gap of several days after English, which is ideal for splitting between programming concepts and short-question revision. Mathematics or Biology on 25 November is the heavy paper of the second week of exams, and Physics on 27 November closes the series, so the days between them should be protected for numerical practice and derivations rather than spent on already-strong material.

Whatever your board's actual order, apply the same principle: give the first paper extra preparation because it sets your tone, give heavy numerical papers the longest practice blocks, and keep the last paper's revision going right to the end instead of relaxing early. Check your own board's sheet as soon as it is available, and if any date differs from the examples here, rebuild your plan around your own dates immediately. A plan that matches your real timetable is worth far more than a beautiful plan built on another board's dates. For general preparation help, see solved notes and the blog listing.

After Theory: Practicals from 1 to 11 December

Theory papers end on 28 November, but the series is not over. Practical examinations run from 1 to 11 December 2026, and students in science subjects should begin practical preparation during the theory weeks, not after them. Keep your practical notebooks complete and up to date, revise the experiments you performed during the year, and practise writing observations and results clearly. A practical notebook that is finished early removes a whole source of stress from the days after theory ends.

The gap between the last theory paper and the first practical is also the time to rest properly for a day or two before shifting into practical mode. Eat well, sleep properly, and then work through your practical syllabus subject by subject, confirming your practical centre and timing from your slip. Treat practicals with the same seriousness as theory, because their marks count just as much, and a well-prepared practical performance is one of the most reliable ways to lift an overall result.

5 key points you must know

  • 11th class theory papers run 17 to 28 November 2026, with practicals from 1 to 11 December 2026
  • Week one targets weak chapters; week two practises full papers under timed conditions
  • Multan examples: English 18 Nov, Computer Science 23 Nov, Mathematics or Biology 25 Nov, Physics 27 Nov
  • Always rebuild your plan around your own board's date sheet, not another board's examples
  • Taper revision in the final two days and sleep properly before the first paper

Frequently asked questions

When do Punjab 11th class second annual papers start?

Part-I (11th class) theory papers run from 17 to 28 November 2026 across all nine Punjab boards. Practical examinations follow from 1 to 11 December 2026.

What does the Multan example order look like for 11th class?

In the BISE Multan examples, English Compulsory falls on 18 November, Computer Science on 23 November, Mathematics or Biology on 25 November, and Physics on 27 November. Your own board's order may differ, so confirm your own date sheet.

How should I split the two weeks before 17 November?

Use the first week for your weakest chapters in morning slots and stronger material in the afternoon. Use the second week for timed practice papers, mark them honestly, and keep an error list for the final days.

When are the practical examinations?

Practical examinations run from 1 to 11 December 2026. Keep practical notebooks complete during the theory weeks so that the practical phase does not start with catch-up work.

Should I study all night before the first paper?

No. Taper revision in the final two days, prepare your slip and stationery the night before, and sleep properly. A rested student with a clear plan outperforms an exhausted student who crammed through the night.
