BSEK Karachi Matric Science Group Result 2026 Announced: How to Check

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-17 · 1797 words · 9 min read

The Board of Secondary Education Karachi (BSEK) has announced the SSC Part II (Class 10) Science Group Annual Examination 2026 result, and thousands of students across Karachi can now check their outcome by roll number. About 175,200 candidates appeared in the examination and about 141,688 passed, giving an overall pass percentage of about 80.87 percent. That is the headline number every student, parent, and teacher is talking about today. In this guide we explain exactly what the board announced, how to check your result step by step on the official website, how to read your result card, what the pass percentage really means, and what you should do next whether you passed, missed a paper, or want to improve your marks.

What BSEK Announced in the Matric Science Result 2026

BSEK announced the SSC Part II Class 10 Science Group Annual Examination 2026 result for students who appeared under the Karachi board. About 175,200 candidates appeared in the examination, and about 141,688 of them passed. Those two figures give an overall pass percentage of about 80.87 percent. If you are comparing your own result with the board average, remember that this average covers every Science Group student in Karachi, including regular and private candidates, and it says nothing about any single school, class, or subject. Your own result card is the only number that decides your next step.

The announcement ceremony was held at the BSEK Conference Hall, First Floor, Block B, where position holders were recognised with cash awards and certificates. Ceremonies like this matter because they show students that consistent work is publicly valued, but they are not the main event for most candidates. For the great majority of students, the real news is simpler: the result is out, it is checked by roll number on bsek.edu.pk, and the next decisions are about subjects, colleges, and improvement options. Keep your roll number and admit card safe until you have downloaded or printed your result, because you will need them again for verification and admission.

  • Board: Board of Secondary Education Karachi (BSEK)
  • Examination: SSC Part II (Class 10) Science Group Annual Examination 2026
  • Appeared: about 175,200 candidates
  • Passed: about 141,688 candidates, about 80.87 percent pass rate
  • Check: by roll number on the official website bsek.edu.pk

How to Check Your BSEK Matric Science Result by Roll Number

Checking your result is straightforward, but students often lose marks of patience when the website is slow on result day. Go to the official BSEK website, bsek.edu.pk, and look for the result section for SSC Part II Science Group Annual Examination 2026. Enter your roll number exactly as printed on your admit card, then submit the form and wait for your result card to load. Do not trust result screenshots forwarded on social media or unofficial websites that ask for extra personal details. The board website is the only source that counts, and any other page should be treated as a rumour until it matches the official result.

If the page does not load, wait a few minutes and try again instead of refreshing dozens of times, because heavy traffic on result day slows every board website. Keep a screenshot or printout of your result card once it appears, and note your marks in each subject along with your overall grade. If any detail looks wrong, such as a missing subject or a roll number mismatch, contact your school or the board office through the official channels listed on the board website. Do not pay any stranger who promises to change a result, because no genuine result can be changed through a private message or a payment link.

  • Open the official website bsek.edu.pk on your phone or computer
  • Find the SSC Part II Science Group Annual Examination 2026 result link
  • Enter your roll number exactly as printed on your admit card
  • Save or print your result card and note every subject mark

How to Read Your Result Card Without Confusion

Your result card shows your marks in each subject, your total, your grade, and whether you have passed, failed a paper, or need to reappear in any subject. Read it slowly, subject by subject, instead of jumping straight to the total. A student who scored well in Physics but weakly in Mathematics has a different next step from a student who scored evenly across all subjects. If you are unsure about any abbreviation or status word on the card, ask a teacher to explain it before you make a decision about improvement, rechecking, or college admission. A calm ten-minute review with a teacher can save weeks of confusion later.

Remember that a result card is a record of one examination, not a judgement on your ability for life. Students who pass with modest marks still have strong options, and students who fail one paper still have a clear path through reappearance or improvement as per board rules. What matters now is an honest look at where you gained marks and where you lost them. Check your Solved Notes and your STBB textbooks for the subjects you found hardest, and make a short list of the chapters that cost you the most marks so your next study plan starts from evidence, not guesswork.

  • Check marks subject by subject, not only the total
  • Note any paper you must reappear in, and the board rules that apply
  • Ask a teacher to explain any status word you do not understand
  • Keep the result card safe for college admission and verification

What the 80.87 Percent Pass Rate Really Means

An overall pass percentage of about 80.87 percent means that roughly four out of every five candidates who appeared passed the examination. That is useful background, but it does not tell you how hard any individual paper was, how your school performed, or whether a particular subject was marked strictly. Pass percentages move from year to year for many reasons, including changes in paper patterns, preparation levels, and checking standards. Treat the figure as context for the whole board, and keep your focus on your own subject-wise performance, because colleges and future choices respond to your marks, not to the board average.

Students sometimes worry when they hear that a large number of candidates secured high grades, and they wonder whether their own grade still counts. It does. A grade earned through your own preparation is a real achievement, and it opens the same doors it always did: college admission in your chosen stream, scholarship applications where they exist, and a stronger base for Class 11 and 12. If you want to understand how boards handle unusual grade patterns, read our explainer on why BSEK delayed the result for super checking and the BIEK result checking guide for how verification works across Sindh boards.

  • About 80.87 percent of about 175,200 candidates passed
  • The average covers the whole board, not your school or subject
  • Your subject-wise marks matter more than the board average
  • A pass earned by honest preparation is a real achievement

What to Do Next After the Matric Science Result

Your first job after the result is to decide your next step based on your marks and your interests, not on pressure from friends or relatives. If you passed, start researching college streams such as Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, Computer Science, Commerce, or Arts, and read our guide on what to do after the Matric Science result before you choose. If you failed or missed a paper, find out the board rules for reappearance and the deadlines that apply to you, and speak to your school about the fastest honest route back. If you believe a paper was marked unfairly, learn how scrutiny and rechecking work in Sindh before you apply, so you understand the process and its limits.

Whatever your result, give yourself one quiet evening to plan before you act. Write down your marks, your three strongest subjects, your two weakest subjects, and the stream or goal you want next. Then talk to a parent and a teacher about that short plan. Students who plan this way avoid the two most common mistakes after results: rushing into a stream they do not enjoy, or waiting so long that admission deadlines pass. Your result is a starting point, not a finish line, and the next three months of steady work will matter far more than the single day the result was announced.

  • Passed: research college streams and admission steps calmly
  • Missed a paper: check board rules for reappearance and deadlines
  • Unhappy with marking: read the scrutiny and rechecking process first
  • Write a short plan: marks, strengths, weaknesses, and your next goal
