How to Prepare Board Exams with Solved Exercises & Notes

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 777 words · 4 min read

478Study Tips

Most students prepare for board exams by reading — and then discover in the exam hall that reading is not the same as writing. Solved exercises, notes and MCQs each train a different skill, and a good preparation cycle uses all three in the right order. This guide gives you that cycle, for any subject and any of the four boards.

The four-step cycle: learn, model, write, test

Step one — learn from the textbook. Read the chapter properly once; the textbook is what the paper is set from. Step two — study the model: read the chapter's solved exercises from the books catalogue and notice how each answer is structured. Step three — write from memory: close everything and write the main answers yourself. Step four — test: take the chapter's objective set on the MCQs practice page and record every fact you missed.

One chapter through all four steps is worth more than three chapters read three times. The cycle feels slower on day one and much faster by week two, because each step makes the next one easier.

What notes are really for

Notes are not a second textbook — they are a compression of the chapter for revision. Good notes hold the definitions, the formulas, the dates and the one-line summaries you must recall quickly. Use the chapter notes on the Notes page during your revision rounds, and download the notes PDFs for the last weeks, when re-reading whole chapters is no longer possible.

Make your own one-page summary after studying each chapter: five key points, two references or formulas, one example. Writing that page is itself a study step — students who write summaries remember chapters longer than students who only highlight them.

A weekly rhythm that covers everything

Give each weekday two subjects: one solving subject (Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry numericals) and one reading subject (Islamiat, Urdu, English, Pakistan Studies). Solving in the morning, reading in the evening matches how the mind works — fresh for methods, calm for memory. Keep one day for weak chapters only: the chapters your error notebook says you keep failing.

Every week, take one full past-paper-style sitting for a single subject: objective and written together, timed, book closed. Weekly sittings train the two things daily study cannot — time control and exam nerves.

The mistakes that waste preparation

Mistake one: studying only from guides and never opening the textbook — the paper is set from the book. Mistake two: reading solved exercises without ever writing — the exam tests writing. Mistake three: practising MCQs before learning the chapter — guessing is not practice. Mistake four: revising everything equally in the last week — your weak chapters deserve the last week, not your strong ones.

Avoid those four and the same study hours produce visibly better results. Preparation is not about more hours; it is about the right order of the same hours.

5 key points you must know

  • Use the four-step cycle for every chapter: textbook reading, solved exercises as the model, writing from memory, then an MCQ test. Never skip the writing step.
  • Notes are for revision, not for first learning. Learn from the book, model from solved exercises, revise from notes.
  • Write a one-page summary per chapter: five key points, two references or formulas, one example. That page is your last-week material.
  • Study two subjects a day — one solving subject in the morning, one reading subject in the evening — and keep one weekly day for weak chapters only.
  • Take one timed, closed-book sitting every week. Time control and calm are skills, and only sittings train them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best order to use solved exercises and notes?

First read the chapter in the textbook, then study the solved exercises to learn the answer style, then write the answers from memory, and revise later from the notes. Notes come last in learning and first in revision.

Are solved exercises enough without the textbook?

No. Solved exercises teach you how to write answers, but the paper is set from the textbook. Always pair them: the book for content, the solved exercises for method.

How do MCQs fit into board exam preparation?

After finishing a chapter, take its set on the MCQs page. MCQs expose the small facts you half-know — exactly the facts the objective section tests.

How should I revise in the last week before exams?

Revise only your one-page summaries, your error notebook, and the notes PDFs. No new chapters in the last week — new material at that stage creates panic, not marks.
