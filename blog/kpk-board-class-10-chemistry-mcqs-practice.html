KPK Board Class 10 Chemistry MCQs Practice

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 660 words · 3 min read

495Subject Guides

Class 10 Chemistry has a large objective section, and it rewards the students who practise: every MCQ has one exact answer, and the same facts, formulas and reactions return across sets. KPK Board students who practise MCQs chapter by chapter — after studying the solved exercises — find the objective section the most predictable part of the paper. This guide shows how to practise Class 10 Chemistry MCQs properly.

Study first, then practise

Study the chapter's solved exercises on the Class 10 Chemistry book page first, so the concepts are clear, and only then open the MCQs. An MCQ practised before the chapter is studied is a guess — and guesses teach nothing.

Practise MCQs chapter by chapter — chemical equilibrium, acids and bases, salts, organic chemistry — and only mix chapters in the final two weeks, when you are ready for exam-style sets.

Read the explanation under every answer

On the MCQs practice page, read the explanation under every answer, right or wrong. A correct answer you cannot explain is luck, and luck does not repeat in the paper — the explanation is the lesson.

Write every missed fact into your error notebook in one line: the fact, the chapter, and the correct answer. That notebook is the most efficient final revision you own, because it holds precisely what you personally missed.

Reactions, formulas and equations

Most Chemistry MCQ mistakes are small slips: a wrong formula, a missed coefficient in a balanced equation, or a confused acid and base. Keep a sheet of formulas and key reactions per chapter and revise it daily in the last month.

Practise balancing equations in writing, with coefficients counted on both sides. An equation you can balance on paper is an equation you will not miss in the objective section.

Timed sets in the final weeks

In the last two weeks, switch to mixed timed sets across the whole book, with a clock. Timed practice teaches the one skill chapter sets cannot: choosing the right fact quickly, under pressure, without going back to the book.

After each timed set, note which chapters still produce errors and give those chapters one more pass through their solved exercises and notes on the Notes page. By the final week, the weak chapters should be down to one or two.

5 key points you must know

  • Study the chapter's solved exercises first, then practise its MCQs — an MCQ attempted before the chapter is studied is a guess.
  • Practise MCQs chapter by chapter — equilibrium, acids and bases, salts, organic chemistry — and mix chapters only in the final two weeks.
  • Read the explanation under every answer, right or wrong, and write each missed fact into your error notebook in one line.
  • Keep a sheet of formulas and key reactions per chapter, and practise balancing equations in writing with coefficients counted on both sides.
  • In the last two weeks, switch to mixed timed sets with a clock, then give the chapters that still produce errors one more pass.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I practise KPK Board Class 10 Chemistry MCQs?

On the MCQs practice page. Choose the Class 10 Chemistry book and practise chapter by chapter; every MCQ shows the correct answer with an explanation.

Should I practise MCQs before or after the written exercises?

After. Study the chapter's solved exercises on the book page first so the concepts are clear, then use MCQs to test exactness.

How do I stop making small mistakes in Chemistry MCQs?

Keep a formula and reaction sheet per chapter and revise it daily. Practise balancing equations in writing — most MCQ mistakes are formula slips and missed coefficients, not concept errors.

What should I revise on the last day for Chemistry?

Your error notebook and your formula and reaction sheets. They hold precisely the facts and reactions you personally missed, which is the most efficient final revision.
