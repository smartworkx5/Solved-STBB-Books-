Urdu Poetry: Hamd and Naat Explained

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-04 · 1658 words · 8 min read

Hamd and naat are two of the most beloved forms of Urdu poetry, because they express praise, love and devotion in beautiful words. A hamd praises Allah, while a naat praises the Holy Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), and both forms have been written by great poets for centuries. Students meet hamd and naat in school lessons, in assemblies, and in religious gatherings, yet many do not know how to explain their themes or answer questions about them. This article explains both forms simply, gives famous examples, and shows how students can understand and appreciate praise poetry with respect and confidence.

What Are Hamd and Naat?

A hamd is a poem written in praise of Allah, and it usually describes His greatness, mercy, power and kindness, often through images from nature such as the sky, the sea, the sun and the changing seasons. A naat is a poem written in praise of the Holy Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), and it usually describes his character, his mercy for humanity, his teachings, and the love that believers feel for him. Both forms are written with deep respect and sincerity, and their language is usually gentle, musical and full of beautiful images that even young readers can enjoy.

Hamd and naat differ from ordinary poetry because their purpose is devotion rather than entertainment, and poets take special care to use respectful and pure language in them. A hamd often begins with wonder at creation, asking who made the stars shine and the rivers flow, while a naat often begins with love and longing, describing the light and mercy the Prophet brought to the world. Students should remember this simple difference: hamd is praise of Allah, and naat is praise of the Prophet, and both are central to Urdu literary and religious culture. Themes of Hamd

The main themes of hamd are the oneness of Allah, His power over creation, His mercy towards human beings, and His wisdom in the order of the universe. Poets often point to nature as proof of greatness: the sun rises on time, the rain falls on dry fields, and the stars shine without support, and each of these signs becomes a verse of praise. Another common theme is human helplessness before Allah, because the poet admits that human knowledge is small while divine knowledge is limitless, and this humility gives hamd its gentle and sincere tone. Gratitude is also a major theme, as poets thank Allah for life, health, family and countless blessings.

Students can understand a hamd by asking three questions: what quality of Allah does this verse praise, what image from nature or life does it use, and what feeling does it create in the reader. For example, a verse about rain may praise Allah's mercy, use the image of dry land becoming green, and create a feeling of gratitude and wonder. This method turns a hamd from a poem to be memorised into a poem to be understood, and it helps students write answers that show real appreciation. Themes of Naat

The main themes of naat are love for the Prophet, his mercy for all humanity, his perfect character, and his role as a guide who brought light to a dark world. Poets describe his kindness to the poor, his patience in hardship, his truthfulness, and his love for his followers, and they often express a longing to visit Madinah or to follow his example. Another theme is gratitude, because believers thank Allah for sending such a merciful guide, and this gratitude gives naat its warm and emotional tone. Naat poetry is also famous for its respectful language, since poets avoid any word that could sound disrespectful.

Students should notice that naat often uses light as its central image: the Prophet is described as a light that removed darkness, a lamp in the night, or a sun that rose over Arabia. This image helps students explain naat verses in exams, because they can say what the light stands for, namely guidance, knowledge and mercy. When writing about naat, students should use respectful language, mention the Prophet with the proper salutation, and focus on character and teachings rather than only on emotional praise. Famous Examples Students Know

One of the most famous naats in Urdu is Sab se aula o aala hamara Nabi, written by Imam Ahmed Raza, which declares that our Prophet is the highest and most exalted of all. This naat is loved because its language is simple, its rhythm is strong, and its message of love and honour is clear even to young listeners. Another widely loved naat is Mustafa jaan-e-rehmat pe lakhon salam, which sends countless greetings upon the Prophet who is described as the soul of mercy. Students often hear these naats in gatherings and assemblies, and knowing their themes helps them answer questions with confidence.

Famous hamds include poems that praise Allah as the creator and sustainer of the universe, often beginning with wonder at nature and ending with gratitude and humility. One well-known hamd theme is Wohi Khuda hai, which praises Allah alone as the one God who controls all creation, and students can recognise this theme in many hamds they hear. When studying a famous hamd or naat, students should learn its poet, its central theme, and one or two famous lines with their meanings, because this combination is exactly what exam questions usually ask. How to Write About Hamd and Naat

A good exam answer on hamd or naat begins by naming the form correctly and stating its theme in one sentence, for example that the poem praises Allah's mercy or the Prophet's character. Then the student should explain one or two verses in plain words, mention the images used, and describe the feeling the poem creates, such as wonder, love or gratitude. The answer should end with a short sentence about the poem's message for daily life, such as gratitude for blessings or following the Prophet's example of kindness. This structure is simple, respectful and complete, and it works for both hamd and naat questions.

Students should also remember to use respectful language throughout, especially when writing about the Prophet, and to avoid casual or joking tones in answers about religious poetry. Quoting one correct verse with its meaning is better than quoting many verses without explanation, because examiners value understanding over memorisation. 5 key points you must know

  • A hamd praises Allah, while a naat praises the Holy Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him).
  • Hamd themes include oneness, power, mercy, wisdom and gratitude for creation.
  • Naat themes include love, mercy, perfect character and guidance for humanity.
  • Light is the central image of naat, standing for guidance, knowledge and mercy.
  • Good answers name the form, explain verses simply, and end with the poem's message.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between hamd and naat?

A hamd is a poem in praise of Allah, describing His greatness, mercy, power and wisdom, often through images from nature. A naat is a poem in praise of the Holy Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), describing his character, mercy and guidance for humanity. The difference is simply the subject of praise: hamd praises Allah, and naat praises the Prophet.

What themes appear in hamd poetry?

The main themes of hamd are the oneness of Allah, His power over creation, His mercy towards human beings, His wisdom in the order of the universe, and gratitude for blessings. Poets often use nature images like the sun, rain, stars and seas to show greatness, and they express humility by admitting that human knowledge is small before divine knowledge.

What is a famous naat that students should know?

One of the most famous naats is Sab se aula o aala hamara Nabi by Imam Ahmed Raza, which declares that our Prophet is the highest and most exalted. Another widely loved naat is Mustafa jaan-e-rehmat pe lakhon salam, which sends countless greetings upon the Prophet as the soul of mercy. Both are loved for simple language, strong rhythm and clear messages of love.

How do I explain a hamd verse in an exam?

First name the quality of Allah the verse praises, such as mercy or power, then explain the image it uses, such as rain making dry land green, and then name the feeling it creates, such as gratitude or wonder. Finish with a short sentence about the verse's message. This method shows real understanding and is much stronger than copying the verse without explanation.

What language should I use when writing about naat?

Use respectful and sincere language when writing about naat, and mention the Prophet with the proper salutation. Focus on his character, mercy and teachings rather than casual praise, and avoid joking or careless tones. Quoting one correct verse with its plain meaning is better than quoting many verses without explanation, because examiners value understanding and respect.
