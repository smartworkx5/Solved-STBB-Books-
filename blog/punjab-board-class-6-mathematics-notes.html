Punjab Class 6 Mathematics Notes Guide

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 658 words · 3 min read

511Solved Notes

Class 6 Mathematics is where the habits that decide later maths are formed: showing the working, checking the answer, and practising a little every day. The Punjab Board book builds from numbers and fractions to geometry and the first steps of algebra. These notes and solved exercises help a young student learn each idea properly the first time.

Show every step, even for easy questions

Open the Class 6 Mathematics book page and work one exercise at a time. Write every step, even when the answer is obvious — the habit of showing working is what earns full marks in later classes, and it is easiest to learn now.

Check your answer against the solved exercise step by step. If your answer is right but a step is missing, add the step and do the question again. Right answers with full working are the goal, not just right answers.

Learn fractions and decimals by doing, not reading

Fractions, decimals and percentages are the chapters where Class 6 students struggle most — and they are learned by practice. Solve five questions a day from these chapters, using the solved exercises to check each one. Short daily practice beats a long session once a week.

When a question goes wrong, do it again from the start, in writing, until you can reach the answer without looking. The questions you get wrong twice go into a small error notebook to practise again before the test.

Draw geometry figures with instruments

Geometry in Class 6 is about lines, angles and simple shapes — and about drawing them properly. Use a ruler and a protractor, label every point and angle, and write the name of each figure beside it. A neat, labelled figure is part of the answer.

Learn the names first: what is a right angle, an acute angle, a triangle, a rectangle. Then practise drawing each one. The solved exercises show the figures — copy them until you can draw them on your own.

Revise little and often, then test with MCQs

Revise one chapter a day from the notes on the Notes page — a little every day is better than a lot once a week for young learners. After each chapter, test yourself with the solved exercises and the book closed.

Finish with the MCQs practice page. Choose the Class 6 Mathematics book and practise with the correct answer and explanation shown for every question — quick questions on numbers and shapes are exactly what the objective part tests.

5 key points you must know

  • Write every step, even for easy questions — showing working is a habit to learn now.
  • Practise five fraction or decimal questions every day and check each against the solved exercise.
  • Draw geometry figures with a ruler and protractor, labelled, with the name of each figure.
  • Re-work every question you get wrong until you can solve it without looking.
  • Revise one chapter a day, a little at a time, and test yourself with the book closed.
