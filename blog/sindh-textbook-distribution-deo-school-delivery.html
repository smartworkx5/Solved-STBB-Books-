How Free STBB Textbooks Reach Your School: The Delivery Chain

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-19 · 1337 words · 7 min read

The Sindh textbook distribution DEO school delivery chain is the reason free textbooks arrive in classrooms across Sindh, and it is worth understanding because it explains both the scale of the operation and what to do when books are late. For the academic year 2026-27, the Sindh Textbook Board (STBB) prepared 52 million textbook sets for distribution to 5.2 million pupils in 41,158 schools across Sindh, with formal distribution to be completed by 1 April and District Education Officers (DEOs) directed to ensure timely delivery to schools. This article follows a textbook from the printing press to the pupil's desk, explains the numbers behind the operation, and gives general advice for pupils whose books have not yet arrived.

The delivery chain at a glance

A free textbook reaches a pupil through a chain with four main links. First, STBB prepares and publishes the textbooks. Second, the books are supplied onward through the education administration. Third, District Education Officers are directed to ensure timely delivery to schools in their districts. Fourth, the school receives the books and hands them to pupils. If any link is slow, the pupil feels it as a late book, even though the books may already exist further up the chain.

Understanding the chain helps a pupil ask the right question. A pupil whose books have not arrived does not need to contact the board first. The right first step is to ask the school, because the school is the link that receives the books for that pupil. The school, in turn, deals with the district administration that handles delivery.

The scale of the 2026-27 operation

The numbers for 2026-27 show why textbook delivery is one of the largest logistical operations in Sindh's education system. STBB prepared 52 million textbook sets for the year. Those sets are meant for 5.2 million pupils studying in 41,158 schools across the province. Moving that volume of books, on time, to tens of thousands of schools, is a task where small delays at one level can show up as empty desks at another.

Scale also explains why distribution is organised by district. No single office can deliver to 41,158 schools directly, so the work is divided among districts, with DEOs responsible for ensuring that schools in their own district receive their books on time. When the system works, the pupil never notices the machinery. When it does not, the pupil notices immediately.

The 1 April formal distribution deadline

Formal distribution of the free textbooks is to be completed by 1 April. A formal deadline like this matters because it gives every level of the chain a target: the books should be in schools, not merely in transit, by that date. It also gives schools and parents a fair point at which to ask questions if books have still not arrived.

Pupils should read the deadline as a promise about the system, not as a guarantee for a single school on a single day. Deliveries reach different schools on different days. A school that has not received its books a week before the deadline is in a different position from a school that still has nothing after it. In both cases, the school is the right place to ask, because the school can check with the district administration.

The 195-title series behind the books

The books being distributed for 2026-27 belong to a series with its own identity. STBB completed publication of textbooks from Early Childhood Education up to Grade 10 under the title Shaheed Mohtarma Benazir Bhutto Textbook Series, covering 195 titles in Sindhi, Urdu and English. The three-medium coverage matters: a pupil should receive the book in the medium their school uses, and a mismatch of medium is worth reporting to the school as promptly as a missing book.

Knowing the series name helps pupils and parents check that they have received the current books rather than an old edition. If a book looks different from the one classmates received, or if the title page shows a different series, the school can confirm whether it is the right book. For a fuller account of the series, see our guide to the STBB 195-title textbook series.

What to do if your books have not arrived

If your books have not arrived, start with the school. Ask your class teacher or head teacher whether the school has received its consignment, and whether your class set has been handed out yet. Sometimes books have arrived at the school but have not yet reached every class, and a single question clears up the delay. Keep the question polite and specific: which books are missing, and for which class and medium?

While waiting, do not let the missing book stop all study. Borrow a classmate's book for the lesson, copy the key pages with permission, use class notes, and keep up with homework as far as possible. If the delay continues, the school can raise it with the district administration, since DEOs are directed to ensure timely delivery.
