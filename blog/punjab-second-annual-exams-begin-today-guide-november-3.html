Punjab Second Annual Exams Begin Today: First-Day Guide for 3 November 2026

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-03 · 1576 words · 8 min read

Today is the day thousands of Punjab students have been preparing for. The HSSC second annual examinations begin across the province on Tuesday 3 November 2026, with Part-II (12th class) theory papers running from 3 to 16 November 2026 under all nine Punjab boards. If you are appearing today, your job is simple: reach the right centre on time, carry the right documents, and attempt your paper with a clear head. This guide walks you through the first day step by step, using only verified board information, so that nothing about the morning surprises you.

What Begins Today Across Punjab

The second annual examinations are held province-wide through the Punjab Boards Committee of Chairpersons, covering all nine boards: Lahore, Rawalpindi, Gujranwala, Faisalabad, Multan, Sargodha, Sahiwal, Bahawalpur and DG Khan. Part-II theory papers run from 3 to 16 November 2026, and Part-I (11th class) theory papers follow from 17 to 28 November 2026. Practical examinations are scheduled later, from 1 to 11 December 2026. Knowing this overall shape matters because it tells you that today is the start of a two-week theory window, not a single-day event, and that your preparation should pace itself across the whole series.

Morning papers begin at 9:00 AM and evening papers at 2:00 PM, while on Fridays the evening paper starts at 2:30 PM. Check your own roll number slip for whether your paper is a morning or evening session, because the two shifts have very different travel and revision plans. A morning-paper student should plan to be up early, revised and travelling well before 9:00 AM, while an evening-paper student gets extra morning revision time but must still reach the centre comfortably before 2:00 PM. Never guess your session from a friend's slip, since centres and shifts can differ even within the same subject.

Before You Leave Home: The First-Day Checklist

Your roll number slip is the most important item of the day. It tells you your centre, your seat and your paper timing, and examiners will ask for it before you enter the hall. Pack it the night before, along with your original identity documents if your board requires them, and the stationery your paper needs: pens, pencils, a ruler, a geometry box or calculator only where the paper allows them. Keep everything in one clear folder so you are not searching through a bag at the centre gate.

Check your centre address the night before and plan your route with extra time for traffic. If your centre is in an unfamiliar part of town, confirm the location with a family member or a classmate who has been there before, and keep a charged phone for navigation and contact. Eat a proper breakfast, drink water, and avoid starting any brand-new topic on the morning of the exam. First-day nerves are normal, and a calm, prepared start does more for your marks than a panicked last-minute cram session ever will.

  • Roll number slip, checked for centre name, paper and session timing
  • Pens, pencils, ruler and any paper-specific stationery allowed by the board
  • A water bottle and a light snack for the wait before the paper
  • Travel planned with extra time so you arrive before the gate closes

At the Centre: Seating, Instructions and First Minutes

Arrive early and head straight to the seating plan or notice board, where your roll number will show your room and seat. Sit down, settle your stationery, and listen carefully to the invigilator's instructions about attendance, answer-book details and the use of extra sheets. Fill in your roll number and other required details on the answer book slowly and exactly as they appear on your slip, because a wrongly written roll number can cause real trouble later even when the answers themselves are good.

When the question paper is handed out, use the first few minutes to read the whole paper before writing anything. Mark the questions you know best, note the ones that carry the most marks, and decide your attempt order. Most students do best when they start with a question they are confident about, since an early success steadies the nerves and builds momentum for the harder questions that follow. Keep an eye on the clock from the start, and divide your time roughly in proportion to the marks each section carries.

A Calm Plan for the First Paper

The first paper sets the tone for the whole series, so treat it as a warm-up as well as an exam. Write in a clear hand, keep your answers organised under the right question numbers, and show your working in numerical papers, because examiners award marks for method as well as final answers. If a question stalls you, move on and come back to it later rather than losing twenty minutes on one part while easier marks wait elsewhere in the paper.

In the last fifteen minutes, stop writing new answers and start reviewing. Check that every attempted question is numbered correctly, that no page is left blank by accident, and that your roll number appears wherever the answer book requires it. A short review at the end catches small slips that cost marks for no reason. After the paper, avoid long post-paper discussions about answers you cannot change, and instead rest briefly, eat well, and turn your attention to the next paper on your date sheet.

What Comes Next, and What Is Already Closed

After today, the Part-II theory series continues until 16 November 2026, followed by Part-I theory from 17 to 28 November 2026 and practicals from 1 to 11 December 2026. Keep your date sheet somewhere visible at home, and tick off each paper as it passes, so that your revision always focuses on the next paper rather than the one already done. If you are a 12th class student, remember that the 11th class papers starting on 17 November do not concern you, and if you are in 11th class, your series has not started yet, so use these two weeks for focused revision rather than exam-day stress.

Two things are worth stating plainly so that no student is misled. First, the admission windows for these examinations, which ran from 24 September to 17 October, are now closed, so nobody should expect to apply fresh at this stage. Second, no supplementary result date has been announced by an individual board in the sources checked for today. A preliminary schedule reported in Dawn on 18 January 2026 puts a supplementary intermediate result around 12 January 2027, but that is only a reported schedule expectation, not a confirmed board announcement, and students should wait for their own board's notice before treating any date as final. 5 key points you must know

  • Punjab HSSC second annual Part-II theory papers run 3 to 16 November 2026 across all nine boards
  • Morning papers start 9:00 AM, evening papers 2:00 PM, and Friday evening papers 2:30 PM
  • Carry your roll number slip and allowed stationery, and arrive at the centre early

Frequently asked questions

When do Punjab second annual exams begin?

Part-II (12th class) theory papers begin on Tuesday 3 November 2026 and run to 16 November 2026. Part-I (11th class) theory papers follow from 17 to 28 November 2026, and practicals run from 1 to 11 December 2026.

What time do morning and evening papers start?

Morning papers begin at 9:00 AM and evening papers at 2:00 PM. On Fridays, the evening paper starts at 2:30 PM. Always confirm your own session from your roll number slip, since it can differ by paper and centre.

Can I still apply for the second annual exams?

No. The admission windows ran from 24 September to 17 October and are now closed. Students appearing now should already hold a roll number slip for their centre.

Has a supplementary result date been announced?

No individual board has announced a supplementary result date in the sources checked. A preliminary schedule reported in Dawn on 18 January 2026 puts a supplementary intermediate result around 12 January 2027, but that is only a reported expectation, not a confirmed announcement.

What should I do in the first minutes of the paper?

Read the whole question paper before writing, mark the questions you know best, and decide your attempt order. Starting with a confident question steadies your nerves and builds momentum for the rest of the paper.
