From Ethics to Their Own Faith: A Big Change for Minority Pupils

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-19 · 1206 words · 6 min read

The Sindh minority pupils ethics own faith textbooks change is one of the most meaningful textbook decisions of 2026-27, because it concerns what children learn about their own religion at school. The Sindh School Education and Literacy Department decided in April 2026 to introduce religious textbooks for Hindu pupils in Grades 3-5 in government schools for the academic year 2026-27, with STBB handling distribution. Previously, minority-religion pupils were taught Ethics instead of education related to their own faith. The Education Minister said: We cannot deprive any religious community of its fundamental rights. This article explains what changed, who it covers, how the books are provided, and why the change matters, in a factual and respectful tone.

What changed in 2026-27

The change is straightforward. From the academic year 2026-27, Hindu pupils in Grades 3-5 in government schools in Sindh study religious textbooks of their own faith, rather than the Ethics subject that minority-religion pupils were previously taught instead of education related to their own faith. The decision was taken by the Sindh School Education and Literacy Department in April 2026.

The point of the change is that pupils learn about their own religion from a proper textbook, in school, as part of their ordinary education. It places the religious education of minority pupils on the same footing as that of other pupils: a subject with a textbook, a teacher and a place in the school day, rather than a substitute subject chosen because no textbook existed.

Who the change covers

The change covers Hindu pupils in Grades 3-5 in government schools, for the academic year 2026-27. The grade range matters: it is the early primary years, when children are forming their first organised understanding of faith, values and community. A textbook at this stage gives children accurate material to learn from, instead of leaving religious learning entirely to what they happen to hear outside school.

Over 129,000 Hindu pupils study in Sindh public schools. That is the community the decision serves, and it is a reminder that this is not a small administrative adjustment. It is a change that touches a large number of children and their families across the province.

Where the pupils are: Tharparkar and Umerkot

Two districts stand out in the figures. Tharparkar has the highest number of Hindu pupils in public schools, at 26,642, followed by Umerkot with 21,584. These are districts where the change will be felt in a large number of classrooms, and where the new textbooks will reach many children at once.

The district figures also explain why distribution matters as much as the decision itself. A decision in Karachi helps a child in Tharparkar only when the textbook actually reaches that child's school. That is why STBB's role in distribution is part of the story, and why schools and district officials have a practical part to play in making the change real.

From Ethics to their own faith: what it means in class

In class, the change means that a Hindu pupil in Grade 3, 4 or 5 now studies a textbook about their own faith where they previously studied Ethics. The lesson has a textbook to open, passages to read, and ideas to discuss with the teacher and classmates. For the pupil, the difference is simple and personal: school now teaches them about what they believe, rather than giving them a different subject in its place.

For the classroom as a whole, the change is a lesson in respect. Pupils of different faiths learning side by side, each with textbooks for their own faith, see that the school treats every community's beliefs with the same seriousness. That is the practical meaning of the minister's words: no religious community should be deprived of its fundamental rights, and schools should reflect that principle in what they teach.

Who prints and distributes the books

STBB handles distribution of the new religious textbooks. The publication cost for the current year is borne by Prem Sagar Sanstha Karachi, a community organisation, and STBB is to budget for the cost from the next academic year. This arrangement means the books could reach schools in the current year without waiting for a new budget cycle, while the regular funding is put in place for the years that follow.

Pupils and parents who want to know whether the books have reached their school should ask the school, as with any textbook delivery. Teachers can confirm whether the Grade 3-5 religious textbooks have arrived and when they will be handed out.
