STBB Class 9 Islamiat Solved Exercises — Sindhi Medium (Complete Notes)

By Solved STBB Books Team · Updated 2026-10-16 · 650 words · 4 min read

01Solved Notes

Class 9 Islamiat is the subject where most Sindh Board students lose easy marks — not because it is hard, but because they write incomplete answers. This guide gives you the complete STBB Class 9 Islamiat solved exercises in Sindhi medium, chapter by chapter, in the exact style examiners expect. Every answer follows the textbook, so you can learn it, write it in the exam, and move on with confidence.

What this guide covers

Students in Sindh study from STBB textbooks, but the textbook alone does not show how an answer should look in the exam. That is the gap this guide fills. Below you will find the key ideas in exam order, the mistakes to avoid, and a simple revision method you can use tonight. Keep your STBB book open beside this page while you read — every section below maps to a chapter or exercise in the book.

If you are in a hurry, read the five key points first, then the FAQ at the end. If you have a full study session, work through the whole page and write the answers from memory at the end. Both ways work; the second way works better.

5 key points you must know

  • Start with the Quran section: learn the translation of the selected Ayaat first, then the tashreeh in your own words. Examiners give full marks when the translation is correct and the explanation is in the student's own clear language.
  • For Hadith questions, always write the Arabic text, then the Sindhi translation, then two lines of tashreeh. This three-step format is the safest way to get full marks in the Hadith section.
  • Tauheed and Risalat answers must include one Quranic daleel and one Hadith reference. A belief answer without a daleel reads like an opinion; with a daleel it reads like Islamiat.
  • For Seerat questions, answer in sequence: event, year, reason, result. For example, Hijrat-e-Madina: reason (persecution in Makkah), year (13 Nabvi), result (first Islamic state). Sequence is what earns marks.
  • In Akhlaq answers, always finish with one practical example from daily life. If the question is about Iffat aur Haya, end with how a student shows haya at school. That one line separates A-grade answers from average ones.

How to use this guide (step by step)

  1. Read with the book open. Match each point above to the matching chapter or exercise in your STBB textbook. If a point does not match, re-read the chapter — the book is always the final source.
  2. Write one answer from memory. Close the book and write the most important answer in your own words. Writing reveals what you actually know.
  3. Check and fix. Compare your answer with the book and this guide. Add the one line you missed. That missing line is your real lesson.
  4. Solve a past paper question. Find one past board question on this topic and answer it timed. Past paper practice turns knowledge into marks.
  5. Revise after 2 days. Re-read your written answer after two days. Spaced revision — reading again after a gap — is what moves answers from short-term to long-term memory.

Common mistakes to avoid

Most students lose marks on the same few mistakes: starting without reading the question twice, writing one long paragraph instead of points, skipping the reference or example, and leaving questions blank. Avoid those four and your marks rise even if your knowledge stays the same. A second common mistake is studying only from guides and never opening the textbook. Guides help you understand; the textbook is what the paper is set from. Always study both together.

Finally, do not leave revision to the last night. The night before the exam is for re-reading your notes, not for learning new chapters. Plan your revision so the last night is calm — that calm is worth marks on its own.
