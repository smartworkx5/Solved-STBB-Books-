How to Write Answers in Board Exams for Marks

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 572 words · 3 min read

533Study Tips

Two students can know the same answer and score very differently — the difference is how the answer is written. Board examiners mark what they can see: a clear heading, the steps in order, the diagram labelled, the final answer boxed. This guide shows how to write every answer so the marks you know become the marks you get.

Start with the heading, then the points

Every long answer starts with a heading that names the topic, then the points in order — definition first, explanation next, example last. An examiner who sees the structure gives marks even before reading every word.

Write short paragraphs, one idea each, and underline the key term in each paragraph. Long blocks of text hide your knowledge; short paragraphs show it.

Show every step in numericals

In Maths, Physics and Chemistry, write the given data, the formula, the substitution and the answer with units — four lines for every numerical. A correct answer with no working earns little, and a wrong answer with full working still earns step marks.

Box the final answer. An examiner marking a hundred papers finds a boxed answer in seconds — and a found answer is a marked answer.

Draw the diagram, label it fully

Where the question allows a diagram, draw it — Biology, Physics and Chemistry all reward labelled diagrams with marks. A diagram drawn well can carry half the answer's marks on its own.

Label every part, give the diagram a title, and refer to it in the text: 'as shown in the diagram'. An unlabelled diagram is decoration; a labelled one is marks.

Plan your time before you write

Spend the first five minutes reading the paper and marking the questions you know best. Answer those first — early marks build confidence and leave time for the hard ones.

Keep the last ten minutes for checking: units, signs, spellings and the question numbers. Most lost marks are small mistakes a final check would have caught. Find solved examples to model your answers on in the solved exercises guides and practise the method on your own subject's book page.

5 key points you must know

  • Start every long answer with a heading, then short paragraphs — one idea each, key term underlined.
  • Write every numerical as given data, formula, substitution, answer with units — and box the final answer.
  • Draw and fully label the diagram wherever the question allows it.
  • Answer the questions you know best first, then return to the hard ones.
  • Keep the last ten minutes for checking units, signs, spellings and question numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write answers to score full marks in board exams?

Use a heading, short paragraphs with underlined key terms, full working for numericals, and a labelled diagram wherever the question allows it.

How much working should I show in numericals?

All of it: given data, formula, substitution and answer with units. A wrong answer with full working still earns step marks.

Should I draw diagrams in board exams?

Yes, wherever the question allows it. A labelled diagram with a title can carry half the answer's marks in Biology, Physics and Chemistry.

How do I manage time in the board exam paper?

Spend five minutes reading and marking the questions you know best, answer those first, and keep the last ten minutes for checking.
