BISE Hyderabad Inquiry: 30-Day Report and What Students Should Do

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-30 · 1905 words · 10 min read

On 23 October 2026, the Sindh government suspended the BISE Hyderabad Deputy Controller of the Top Secret Branch and formed a three-member inquiry committee to examine alleged irregularities in the Class XII annual examination 2026 results, and students have asked what it means for their results. The short answer, based on the verified facts, is that the announced results have not been cancelled, the allegations are allegations and not proven findings, and no student has been named as a beneficiary of manipulation. This guide explains what the committee was asked to do, what its 30-day reporting window means, what students should and should not do while the inquiry runs, and how to protect your own record through formal channels rather than rumour.

What Happened on 23 October 2026

The action began with two linked decisions by the Sindh government. First, the Deputy Controller of the Top Secret Branch at the Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education Hyderabad, Ijaz Ali Kaka, was suspended on the orders of the Provincial Minister for Universities and Boards, Muhammad Ismail Rahoo. Second, a three-member inquiry committee was formed through a notification issued by Universities Secretary Abbas Baloch, headed by Professor Dr Arbeela Bhutto, with members Additional Secretary Amir, also reported as Ameer, Fazal Owaisi and Mehran University Registrar Dr Nasrullah Pirzada. These are the verified facts of the order, and they should be reported exactly as stated, without adding motives, outcomes or names that were not published.

The scope of the inquiry is also defined, and students should understand its limits. The committee is examining alleged irregularities in the Class XII, or Intermediate Part-II, annual examination 2026 results, specifically in the pre-medical and pre-engineering groups, looking at position holders, top-ten candidates and A-1 grade students, and at the checking, tabulation and announcement processes behind those results. The allegations themselves circulated through social media and public complaints, which is how they reached the authorities, but circulation is not proof, and this guide treats them strictly as allegations until the committee reports. No student has been named publicly as a beneficiary of manipulation, and no article should name any student in that connection.

The 30-Day Report: What It Means and What It Does Not

The committee's report is due within 30 days of 23 October 2026, and that wording matters. This guide presents the window as within 30 days, not as a confirmed calendar date, because the verified material states the window in those terms and converting it into a specific date would add a claim the source does not make. Students should therefore watch for the report during that window without treating any particular day as a deadline, and should be suspicious of social media posts that announce a specific reporting date, a leaked outcome, or a decision that the committee has not yet published. The report, when it arrives, will go to the authorities that ordered the inquiry, and any action that follows will be announced through official channels, not through rumour.

It is equally important to understand what the 30-day window does not mean. It does not mean results are suspended while the committee works, it does not mean every Class XII result is under question, and it does not mean students must take any action before the report appears. The inquiry's scope is the pre-medical and pre-engineering groups' position holders, top-ten candidates and A-1 grade students and the processes behind those results, which is a defined subset, not the entire result. Students outside that scope have no reason to worry about their own results on the basis of this inquiry, and students inside it should still wait for an official outcome rather than acting on speculation about what the committee might find.

Your Result Stands: Why There Is No Need to Panic

The single most reassuring verified fact in this story is that the announced results have not been cancelled. Students who passed, students who hold positions, and students now in college or preparing for the next stage can continue on the basis of their announced results, because no cancellation has been ordered and no official notice has withdrawn any result. Panic helps no one, and it actively harms students who let rumour disrupt their studies or who pay agents promising to protect a result that is not actually under threat. If your result was announced and you hold an official transcript, that transcript remains your record unless and until an official notice says otherwise, and no social media post can change that.

Students should also resist the temptation to treat the inquiry as a judgement on every high-achieving student. Position holders and A-1 grade students are part of the inquiry's scope because the allegations concerned the processes behind top results, not because every top result is suspect, and the committee's job is to examine processes, including checking, tabulation and announcement, rather than to presume guilt. High achievers should continue their studies normally, keep their documents safe, and respond only to official communications, while other students should avoid speculating about named classmates, since no student has been named and speculation about individuals is both unfair and unverified. If You Suspect a Problem With Your Own Mark Sheet

The inquiry does not replace the normal, individual route for a student who suspects a discrepancy in their own mark sheet, and that route is formal rechecking. If your marks in a paper look inconsistent with your class performance, or if you believe a totalling or recording error has occurred, apply for rechecking through your board's official process, following the notice published by the board, and keep the receipt and any reference number safe. Rechecking is an individual remedy for an individual mark sheet, and it works whether or not a wider inquiry is running, so students should not wait for the committee's report before using it if they have a genuine concern about their own result. Check your board's official website for the current rechecking notice, fee and last date, and apply only through the official channel.

At the same time, keep your paperwork in order, because every formal process asks for the same documents. Keep your official transcript, mark sheet, admit card, registration details and any rechecking receipt together, in both original and photocopy or photograph form, so that nothing is lost while the inquiry runs or afterwards. If your college or institution asks for documents for admission or verification, provide copies and keep the originals safe, and note down the dates and reference numbers of anything you submit. Students who keep clean records never have to reconstruct their case from memory, and that is the best protection available while any inquiry is unresolved. Rumours, Social Media and Responsible Reading

The Hyderabad allegations reached the authorities partly through social media and public complaints, which shows both the power and the danger of online discussion about board results. Social media can surface genuine concerns, but it can also amplify unverified claims, name students without evidence, and invent outcomes that no authority has announced, and students should read every post about this inquiry with that distinction in mind. A post that cites an official notification, names the committee and its reporting window, and states that results have not been cancelled is reporting verified facts, while a post that names a student, claims a result has been changed, or announces a leaked committee finding is speculation and should be ignored and not shared. Sharing an unverified claim about a named student can harm that student and can spread confusion that no later correction fully undoes.

The responsible habit for the coming weeks is simple: check one official source before believing or sharing any claim about the inquiry, whether that source is the board's official website, the Universities and Boards Department, or your institution's notice board. If a claim cannot be confirmed officially, treat it as unverified and do not act on it, whether it concerns your own result, a classmate's result, or the committee's findings. Continue your studies normally, keep your documents safe, and use formal rechecking for any individual concern, because those three habits protect you regardless of what the committee eventually reports. What is the inquiry committee, and when does it report?

A three-member committee headed by Professor Dr Arbeela Bhutto, with members Additional Secretary Amir/Ameer Fazal Owaisi and Mehran University Registrar Dr Nasrullah Pirzada, was formed on 23 October 2026. Its report is due within 30 days of that date, presented here as a window rather than a confirmed calendar date.
