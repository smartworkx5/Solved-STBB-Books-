Balochistan Board Class 10 Chemistry MCQs Practice Guide

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 705 words · 4 min read

481Subject Guides

Class 10 Chemistry decides a large part of the Matric science result, and its objective section is built from exact facts: formulas, names, conditions and definitions. Balochistan Board students who practise MCQs chapter by chapter walk into that section prepared. This guide shows how to practise Class 10 Chemistry MCQs properly, chapter by chapter, with the book's solved exercises beside you.

The chapters your MCQs come from

The Balochistan Board Class 10 Chemistry book covers Chemical Equilibrium, Acids, Bases and Salts, Organic Chemistry, Hydrocarbons, the Atmosphere, Water, and Chemical Industries. Each chapter contributes MCQs of a recognisable kind: equilibrium questions test direction of shift, acids and salts test pH and reactions, organic chapters test names, formulas and functional groups.

Practise a chapter's MCQs only after studying its solved exercises on the Balochistan Board Class 10 Chemistry book page. The written exercises teach the concepts; the MCQs then measure whether the concepts are exact.

How to practise on the MCQs page

Work chapter by chapter on the MCQs practice page. For every wrong answer, read the explanation and then write the correct fact in an error notebook — one line, in your own words. Chemistry MCQs repeat their facts across years and sets, so a fact fixed once is a mark earned many times.

For organic chemistry, keep a names-and-formulas table beside you while practising: the alkanes, alkenes and alkynes with their general formulas, and the common functional groups. Most organic MCQs are table questions in disguise — they ask what the table already holds.

Reasoning MCQs: equilibrium and acids

Equilibrium MCQs are reasoning questions: they describe a change — pressure, temperature, concentration — and ask which way the equilibrium shifts. Practise them by saying the reason aloud before choosing: 'pressure increased, so the side with fewer gas molecules is favoured'. A spoken reason turns a guess into a method.

Acids, bases and salts MCQs test pH values, indicators, and which salt forms from which acid. Learn the three acid families and their salts as one table — hydrochloric gives chlorides, sulphuric gives sulphates, nitric gives nitrates — and a whole family of MCQs becomes one fact.

Final weeks: mixed timed practice

In the last two weeks, switch from chapter sets to mixed sets across the whole book, timed. Note which chapters still produce errors and give those chapters one more pass through their solved exercises and notes on the Notes page.

On the final day, revise only your error notebook and your organic table. Those two documents contain exactly what you personally were getting wrong — the highest-value revision material you own.

5 key points you must know

  • Practise MCQs chapter by chapter — equilibrium, acids bases and salts, organic chemistry, hydrocarbons, atmosphere, water, chemical industries — and only mix chapters in the final two weeks.
  • Read the explanation under every answer and write each missed fact as one line in an error notebook. Chemistry facts repeat across sets; a fact fixed once pays many times.
  • Keep a names-and-formulas table for organic chemistry: homologous series, general formulas and functional groups. Most organic MCQs are table questions in disguise.
  • For equilibrium questions, speak the reason before choosing: state the change, then the shift. Reasoned answers beat remembered guesses.
  • Learn the acid-to-salt families as one table: hydrochloric–chlorides, sulphuric–sulphates, nitric–nitrates. One table answers a whole family of MCQs.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I practise Balochistan Board Class 10 Chemistry MCQs?

On the MCQs practice page. Choose the Class 10 Chemistry book and practise chapter by chapter; every MCQ shows the correct answer with an explanation.

Should I practise MCQs before or after the written exercises?

After. Study the chapter's solved exercises on the book page first so the concepts are clear, then use MCQs to test exactness.

How do I handle equilibrium MCQs?

Treat them as reasoning, not memory: name the change (pressure, temperature or concentration), recall which side it favours, then choose. Practise saying the reason aloud until it becomes automatic.

What should I revise on the last day for Chemistry?

Your error notebook and your organic names-and-formulas table. They hold precisely the facts you personally missed, which is the most efficient final revision.
