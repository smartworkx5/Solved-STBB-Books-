Verified Board Certificates on the Pak ID App: What Sindh Students Should Know

By Solved STBB Books Team · Updated 2026-10-16 · 1335 words · 7 min read

One of the most practical parts of Sindh's new agreement with NADRA is this: verified academic certificates will be available through the Pak ID mobile app. For students, that could mean carrying a digital, verifiable copy of your board certificate on your phone instead of relying only on a paper document. This article explains what the Pak ID app verified certificates announcement means, what is confirmed, and what students should — and should not — expect right now.

What Was Announced

Under the Academic Record Integration Project, the Sindh government signed an agreement with NADRA to connect all educational boards in Sindh with NADRA's secure digital system. As part of that pact, verified academic certificates will be available through the Pak ID mobile app. Sindh is the first province in Pakistan to sign such an agreement.

The announcement was made by the Universities and Boards Minister, Ismail Rahoo, and NADRA officials confirmed that NADRA will provide the integration facility to all educational boards free of charge. The purpose of the whole project is to prevent fake academic certificates and impersonation in examinations, and to make students' educational records digitally secured and verifiable.   • Verified academic certificates will be available through the Pak ID mobile app.
  • All educational boards in Sindh are covered by the NADRA agreement.
  • Sindh is the first province to sign such a pact.
  • NADRA provides the integration facility to boards free of charge.

What a Verified Certificate Means

A verified certificate is one that can be checked against an official digital record. Today, a paper certificate is usually checked by eye, by letter, or by a slow manual process. A verified digital certificate can be matched against the board's own record, so anyone who needs to confirm it — a college, a university, or an employer — can be much more confident that it is genuine.

This matters because a board certificate is the key that opens the next door: admission to the next class, entry to college, or a job application. When that key can be verified quickly, honest students face fewer delays and fewer doubts. It also protects the value of your result, because a system that catches fakes keeps genuine certificates trustworthy. The Unique Student ID Behind It

The Pak ID certificate feature sits on top of another part of the pact: a mechanism will be developed to assign a unique identification number to every student for accurate identification and verification of academic records. Think of it as a single, reliable key that ties your record to you and only you, so that two students with the same name are never mixed up.

Students do not need to guess how this number will be issued or what form it will take. Those details will come through official channels as the project is implemented. What you can do now is make sure the details on your school and board records — your name, your father's name, your date of birth, and your roll number history — are correct and consistent, because a verification system is only as good as the data it holds.

  • A unique identification number will be assigned to every student.
  • The number is for accurate identification and verification of academic records.
  • It reduces mix-ups between students with similar names.
  • Exact issuing details will come through official channels.

What This Does Not Mean Yet

It is important to be careful about what has actually been announced. The pact says verified academic certificates will be available through the Pak ID app. It does not say that paper certificates are abolished, that every old certificate is already on the app, or that any particular app menu or button exists today. Do not follow social media posts that claim to show step-by-step app instructions for a feature that has only just been announced.

It also does not change how you study or sit exams. Your syllabus, your paper pattern, and your preparation remain the same. The Pak ID feature is about proving a result you have already earned, not about earning it. Keep your focus on managing your time well in exams and revising well the night before an exam, and treat any app-related update as something to confirm through official announcements.

  • Paper certificates are not abolished by this announcement.
  • No specific app steps or menus have been announced.
  • Do not trust social media how-to posts about the new feature.
  • Exams, syllabus, and preparation are unchanged.

What Students Should Do Now

The practical steps are simple. First, keep your paper certificates, result cards, and roll number slips safe — they remain your primary proof until any digital feature is fully rolled out. Second, keep your personal details consistent across your school record, your board record, and your identity documents, and raise any mismatch with your school early.

Third, wait for official instructions. If your board or school announces how verified certificates will appear on the Pak ID app, follow those instructions exactly. Never share your personal login details with anyone, and never pay anyone who claims they can add, change, or speed up a certificate on the app. A genuine verification system never needs a middleman.   • Keep paper certificates and result cards safe.
  • Keep your name and details consistent across all records.
  • Follow only official board or school instructions about the app.
  • Never share login details or pay anyone for certificate changes.
