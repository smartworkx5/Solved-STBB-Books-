Morning and Evening Azkar: Short Daily Duas

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-03 · 1074 words · 5 min read

Azkar are short remembrances of Allah said in the morning and evening. They are like daily vitamins for faith: small, regular, and protective over time. For students, morning and evening azkar provide a calm start and a peaceful end to the day, without taking much time. This article presents five short azkar that are easy to memorise, explains each one simply, and suggests a routine that children and adults can follow. Always check exact wording with your Islamiat book and your teacher.

What Azkar Are and Why They Matter

The word azkar means remembrances. Morning azkar are said after Fajr, and evening azkar after Asr or before Maghrib, depending on teaching. They include praise of Allah, seeking protection, and affirming faith. Their purpose is simple: start and end the day with Allah in mind, so the hours between are shaped by that remembrance. A student who begins with azkar is less likely to start the day carelessly.

Azkar are not long rituals. Even a few short phrases, said with understanding, count as a good habit. The Prophet taught many azkar, and scholars collected them in books for daily use. Students should start with a small set they can sustain, rather than a long list they abandon. Regularity matters more than quantity, especially for children learning the habit for the first time.

Five Short Azkar to Memorise

First, say SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, and Allahu Akbar, each 33 times after prayers or as part of daily remembrance. They mean Glory be to Allah, All praise belongs to Allah, and Allah is Greatest. Second, recite Ayat al-Kursi in the morning and evening for protection and affirmation of Allah's power. Third, recite Surah Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, and An-Nas three times each in the morning and evening, as taught for protection.

Fourth, say La ilaha illallah wahdahu la sharika lah, lahul mulku wa lahul hamdu wa huwa ala kulli shayin qadeer, which affirms Allah alone, His kingdom, His praise, and His power over all things. Fifth, say Bismillahilladhi la yadurru maasmihi shayun fil ardi wa la fis sama, wa huwas sami ul aleem, three times, meaning in the name of Allah, with whose name nothing on earth or in heaven can cause harm, and He is All-Hearing, All-Knowing. Learn these one at a time with meaning, and check wording with your teacher.

A Simple Morning Routine for Students

After Fajr, sit for a few minutes and say your chosen azkar slowly. Then plan the day: classes, homework, and one good deed. This short pause prevents a rushed start. A student who rushes from bed to school without remembrance often carries stress all day. A calm morning with azkar, breakfast, and a tidy bag creates a better foundation for learning.

Parents can help by waking children in time, not at the last minute. A family that says a short azkar together, even for two minutes, builds a shared habit. At school, a teacher can remind students of one azkar per week, so the set grows gradually. The goal is understanding and consistency, not speed or competition in counting.

A Simple Evening Routine for Students

In the evening, after homework or play, pause again for azkar. Review the day briefly: what went well, what needs apology or improvement, and what to prepare for tomorrow. Say the evening azkar calmly, then spend time with family without screens where possible. This routine separates school stress from night rest and prepares the mind for sleep.

Evening azkar also protect against worry. Many students worry about exams, friendships, or family problems at night. Remembrance reminds them that Allah is in control and that tomorrow is a new chance. If worry is strong, students should also talk to a parent or teacher. Azkar support mental calm, but they do not replace asking for help when needed.

Building the Habit Without Overload

Start with two azkar, such as the tasbeeh phrases and the three short surahs, and add one more each week. Use a small card with translations near the prayer place. Tick off days on a simple chart for younger children, and praise steady effort. If a day is missed, simply resume the next day without guilt. The habit should feel like a gift, not a punishment.

Teachers can support this by teaching one azkar at a time with its meaning and a short story or context. Students can then write the azkar and its translation in their own notebooks for revision. For more daily guides, see the solved notes, the book lists, and the blog listing.

5 key points you must know

  • Azkar are short morning and evening remembrances of Allah.
  • Start with a small set and add one azkar at a time.
  • Morning azkar give a calm start; evening azkar give a peaceful close.
  • Ayat al-Kursi and the three short surahs are common daily azkar.
  • Regularity with understanding matters more than a long rushed list.

Frequently asked questions

What are morning and evening azkar?

They are short remembrances of Allah, including praise, protection prayers, and affirmations of faith, said after Fajr and in the evening.

Which five azkar should beginners learn?

Tasbeeh phrases, Ayat al-Kursi, Surah Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, and An-Nas, the declaration of Allah's oneness and power, and the Bismillah protection dua.

How long do morning and evening azkar take?

A small set takes only a few minutes when said slowly with understanding. Start small and build gradually.

What if I miss a day of azkar?

Simply resume the next day. The habit should be steady and sincere, not a source of guilt for one missed day.

Can children learn azkar?

Yes, start with one short azkar at a time, learn its meaning in simple language, and practise it together as a family.
