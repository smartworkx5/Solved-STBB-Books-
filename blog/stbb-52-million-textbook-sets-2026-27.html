Sindh Textbook Board's 52 Million Textbook Sets for 2026–27: What Students and Parents Should Know

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-10-16 · 1358 words · 7 min read

In February 2026, the Sindh Textbook Board completed publication of 52 million textbook sets for the academic year 2026-27 — one of the largest textbook operations in the province's history. The sets cover 195 titles up to Grade 10, in Sindhi, Urdu, and English. This article explains what was published, what was promised, and what students and parents should do if their school has not received its books.

What Was Published

The Sindh Textbook Board completed publication of 52 million textbook sets for the academic year 2026-27. The sets cover 195 titles printed up to Grade 10, in Sindhi, Urdu, and English, under the title Shaheed Mohtarma Benazir Bhutto Textbook Series, covering ECE classes to Grade 10. That range matters: it means the programme covers the youngest early-childhood pupils as well as students approaching their board exams.

Fifty-two million is a very large number, and it is worth understanding what it represents: sets, not single books. A set is the collection of textbooks a student needs for their class. When the programme works, every student in a government school starts the year with a full set in hand. If your child has received a full set, that is the system working as intended. If not, the steps later in this article explain what to do. You can also browse the Sindh Textbook Board books available on this site to see which titles your class uses.

  • 52 million textbook sets published for academic year 2026-27.
  • 195 titles printed up to Grade 10.
  • In Sindhi, Urdu, and English mediums.
  • Under the title Shaheed Mohtarma Benazir Bhutto Textbook Series, ECE to Grade 10.

How Distribution Was Launched

Distribution was inaugurated by Education Minister Syed Sardar Ali Shah at the Government Girls Lower Secondary School, Neelam Colony, Clifton, Karachi, on 19 February 2026. The launch event matters because it set the public promise for the programme: the minister said every student would have new textbooks in hand on the first day of the new academic year.

A launch is only the start, of course. After inauguration, sets travel through the province's distribution chain to districts, then to schools, then to classrooms. Delays can happen at any step — transport, counting, or school-level handover — which is why parents should check with their own school rather than assuming a provincial announcement means books have arrived locally. Our guide on how to get Sindh Textbook Board free textbooks explains the normal route in more detail.

  • Distribution inaugurated on 19 February 2026 in Karachi.
  • Launched by Education Minister Syed Sardar Ali Shah.
  • Venue: Government Girls Lower Secondary School, Neelam Colony, Clifton, Karachi.
  • Promise: every student with new textbooks on the first day of the new academic year.

What Was Promised for the Future

At the launch, the minister also said that curriculum changes would be introduced from the next academic year, focusing on research, scientific thinking, and creativity, plus modern trends in science, technology, mathematics, languages, and social sciences. It is important to read that promise correctly: it was announced as a future change, not delivered as part of this textbook publication. No verified new syllabus or curriculum list for 2026-27 beyond this announcement has been confirmed.

Parents and students should therefore treat the curriculum promise as something to watch for, not something already in their books. The books your child received for 2026-27 are the current curriculum. If and when new curriculum changes are officially published, schools will be told through official channels — and this site will explain them in plain language. Until then, the best use of the books is the usual one: steady study, good notes, and honest preparation.

What to Do If Your School Has Not Received Books

If your school has not received its books, act early and politely. First, ask your child's teacher or head teacher whether the school's delivery has arrived, and whether your child's class set is complete. Sometimes a delivery has arrived but has not yet been handed out, or a class set is short by a few titles. A calm question at the school is the fastest way to find out.

Second, if the school says the delivery has not arrived, ask the school to follow up with the district education office, and check back after a few days. Third, while you wait, do not let study stop: borrow a classmate's book for reading, use solved notes and class notes to keep up, and ask your teacher which chapters to prioritise. Missing books are a real problem, but a few weeks of shared reading is far better than a few weeks of no study.

  • Ask the teacher or head teacher whether the delivery has arrived.
  • Ask the school to follow up with the district education office if it has not.
  • Check back after a few days — do not assume silence means nothing is happening.
  • While waiting, share books, use solved notes, and keep studying.

Making the Most of a Full Set

When the full set does arrive, make it count. Check that every title for your class is present, write your name on each book, and keep them safe — free textbooks are a shared resource, and the next student needs them in good condition too. Then build a simple study habit around them: read each chapter before the teacher covers it, make short notes in your own words, and revise those notes weekly.

Students who treat textbooks as tools — reading, questioning, and practising from them — learn far more than students who only open them before exams.
