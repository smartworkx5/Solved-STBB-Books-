Sindh Boards on 2 November 2026: What Is Happening This Week

By Solved Sindh Textbook Board Books Team · Updated 2026-11-02 · 1886 words · 9 min read

Monday 2 November 2026 is a useful checkpoint in a busy Sindh board season, because several stories are running at once and students can easily mix them up. BIEK supplementary examinations continue in Karachi, Benazirabad Class IX students are still working through result follow-up, the Hyderabad inquiry continues, and Punjab second annual examinations begin tomorrow, which matters for comparison but must not be confused with Sindh dates. No new STBB announcement was verified on 2 November itself, so this roundup separates what is actually happening in Sindh from what is merely rumoured, and gives you a calm week-ahead plan. This guide keeps every fact to what the board has actually announced, and it explains each step in plain language, so you can plan with confidence, avoid common mistakes, and focus on the work that actually matters this week.

BIEK Supplementary Exams Continue to 21 November

The largest ongoing Sindh story is the BIEK supplementary run in Karachi, which runs from 28 October to 21 November 2026 with one afternoon shift, papers from 2:00 to 5:00 PM, and reporting at 1:30 PM. More than 21,000 students sit across 49 centres, covering Pre-Medical, Pre-Engineering, Science General, Commerce, Arts, Home Economics, and special-category candidates. If you are sitting these papers, your week is defined by your own date sheet, your centre, and your admit card, not by headlines about other boards or other classes. Keep this point in mind as you plan your week, because a calm, organised, and steady approach usually protects both your time and your marks across every paper.

No supplementary result date has been announced, and a result can only come after the run ends on 21 November 2026, so this week is for papers, not for result watching. Revise in short, active blocks, arrive by reporting time, and keep your admit card safe for every paper. The solved notes and the blog offer steady revision support, but your own remaining papers should set your daily priorities through this week and the weeks that follow. Use this idea in your own plan, and check it against your own date sheet and admit card, so that nothing important is left to guesswork on the day itself.

  • BIEK supplementary run: 28 October to 21 November 2026
  • Papers 2:00 to 5:00 PM, reporting 1:30 PM, 49 Karachi centres
  • No supplementary result date announced yet

Punjab Papers Start Tomorrow: Do Not Apply Them to Sindh

Punjab boards begin HSSC second annual examinations tomorrow, Tuesday 3 November 2026, with Part-II theory from 3 to 16 November and Part-I theory from 17 to 28 November 2026. Those dates are important for Punjab students, and they are useful background for Sindh readers who follow national board news, but they are not Sindh dates. A Sindh student must never copy a Punjab date into a Sindh plan, just as a Punjab student must never copy a Sindh date. Boards issue their own sheets, and only your own board sheet decides your papers.

The confusion risk is real because Punjab and Sindh stories appear side by side online, often without a clear board label. When you read any examination news this week, check the board name first, then the class, then the date. If the story says Punjab, PBCC, or a Punjab board city, it does not set your Sindh timetable. Keep Sindh and Punjab plans in separate notes, and ask a teacher if a forwarded message does not name its board clearly. Remember this as you move to the next step, because steady preparation, clear documents, and enough rest together make a real difference on examination day.

  • Punjab Part-II theory: 3 to 16 November 2026; Part-I: 17 to 28 November
  • Punjab dates are NOT Sindh dates and must not be copied across boards
  • Always check the board name first when reading examination news

Benazirabad Result Follow-Up Continues

BISE Shaheed Benazirabad announced Class IX results on 31 October 2026, so this week is follow-up week for those students: checking individual results, reading subjects carefully, and asking schools about next steps. The Science Group reported 52,260 registered, 51,546 appeared, 41,425 successful, and 79.02 percent overall, while the General Group reported 52 percent overall, without published registered or appeared counts. Students should check only at www.bisesba.edu.pk or through the board Android application, and should note any paper marked Ungraded, meaning under 40 percent and not passed in that paper. Keep this point in mind as you plan your week, because a calm, organised, and steady approach usually protects both your time and your marks across every paper.

Follow-up is often more important than the announcement day itself, because it turns a result into a plan. Passed students can confirm Class X steps with their schools, while students with an Ungraded paper can ask about the proper route for that subject. Avoid unofficial pages that mix groups or invent counts, keep your official record safe, and make a short written plan for the next term. A calm follow-up week sets up a stronger Class X far better than a week spent comparing group averages. Use this idea in your own plan, and check it against your own date sheet and admit card, so that nothing important is left to guesswork on the day itself.
