How to Attempt a Board Exam Paper: Pattern Guide

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 728 words · 4 min read

502Study Tips

Most students lose marks in board exams not because they do not know the answers, but because they attempt the paper badly: the wrong question first, too long on one section, messy presentation, and no time left to check. The paper pattern is the same across Sindh, Punjab, KPK and Balochistan boards — a mix of objective, short and long questions — and a student who walks in with a plan for it scores more from the same knowledge. This guide gives that plan.

Use the reading time first

When the paper is handed out, spend the first few minutes reading the whole paper before writing anything. Mark the questions you know well, the ones you are unsure of, and the long questions that will need the most time — then decide your order before your pen moves.

Start with the questions you know best. Early marks build confidence, and confidence keeps your handwriting and thinking steady for the harder questions that follow.

Divide your time by the sections

Split the paper's time by its sections before you start: the objective section first and fast, then short questions, then long questions, with the last minutes kept for checking. A section that eats the next section's time is the most common way to lose marks.

For the objective section, answer what you know immediately and mark the doubtful ones — come back to them after the short questions, not before. Never leave an objective question blank: a guess has a chance, a blank has none.

Write answers the examiner can mark

Write the question number clearly, leave a line between answers, and underline the key terms in each answer. An examiner marks dozens of papers a day — an answer that is easy to find and easy to read is an answer that gets its full marks.

For long answers, plan first: a definition, the points in order, and a labelled diagram where the subject uses one. Practise this structure at home with the solved exercises on the Books page — a long answer with a clear structure earns marks even when a fact or two is short.

The last minutes belong to checking

Keep the last minutes for checking, not for starting a new long answer. Re-read every question number against your answers, check the numericals' units, and make sure no question is left blank by accident.

In the weeks before the paper, rehearse the whole pattern with timed practice: solved exercises first, then chapter notes on the Notes page, then mixed sets on the MCQs practice page. A paper attempted in practice is a paper attempted with confidence.

5 key points you must know

  • Read the whole paper in the first few minutes, mark what you know well, and decide your order before writing anything.
  • Start with the questions you know best — early marks build the confidence that steadies the rest of the paper.
  • Split the paper's time by its sections, keep the last minutes for checking, and never let one section eat the next section's time.
  • Answer the objective section fast, mark the doubtful ones for later, and never leave an objective question blank.
  • Write the question number clearly, leave a line between answers, underline key terms, and plan every long answer before writing it.
