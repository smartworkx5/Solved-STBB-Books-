Urdu Poetry: Ghalib Couplets in Simple Words

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 1507 words · 8 min read

Mirza Ghalib is one of the most famous poets of Urdu, but many students find his poetry difficult because his words are deep and his ideas are clever. The good news is that Ghalib becomes easy once a few famous couplets are understood in simple words, because his themes are familiar: wishes, love, pain, hope and the strange ways of the human heart. This article explains five famous Ghalib couplets in plain language, shows what image each couplet uses, and teaches a simple method for reading any Ghalib couplet without fear.

Who Was Mirza Ghalib?

Mirza Asadullah Khan Ghalib lived in Delhi during the nineteenth century, a time when the Mughal empire was fading and the city was losing its old glory. He wrote in both Urdu and Persian, and his Urdu ghazals made him the most quoted poet of the language, with lines that people still use in daily conversation today. Ghalib's poetry is famous for its wit, its wordplay and its deep thoughts about life, and he often turned a simple feeling into a clever, surprising idea that makes the reader stop and think twice.

Students meet Ghalib in school lessons and in general knowledge, but his couplets can feel like riddles because he uses old words and indirect meanings. The trick is to read slowly, find the main feeling in the couplet, and ask what picture the poet is drawing with his words. Once the picture is clear, the meaning usually follows, and the couplet stops feeling difficult. Five Famous Couplets in Simple Words

The first famous couplet says: Hazaaron khwahishen aisi ke har khwahish pe dam nikle, bahut nikle mere armaan lekin phir bhi kam nikle. In simple words, the poet says he had thousands of wishes, each so strong that it felt like life itself could leave him, and yet even after many wishes came true, his desires still felt less than enough. The lesson is about human nature: wishes never end, and a person who chases every wish without satisfaction will always feel incomplete, so contentment is also a kind of wisdom.

The second couplet says: Dil-e-nadan tujhe hua kya hai, aakhir is dard ki dawa kya hai. Here the poet speaks to his own innocent heart and asks what has happened to it, and what medicine can cure this pain. It is a picture of a person talking to himself about his own sadness, and it shows Ghalib's habit of turning feelings into a conversation. The third couplet says: Humko maloom hai jannat ki haqeeqat lekin, dil ke khush rakhne ko Ghalib khayal achha hai, which means the poet knows the truth about paradise, but keeps a pleasant thought just to comfort his heart, showing how people use hopeful ideas to bear difficult truths.

Two More Couplets and Their Meanings

The fourth couplet says: Koi umeed bar nahin aati, koi soorat nazar nahin aati. In simple words, no hope seems to come from anywhere, and no way out is visible, which paints a picture of complete disappointment. Students should notice that Ghalib does not just say he is sad; he describes a world where every door looks closed, and that image makes the sadness feel real and deep. This couplet is useful in exams because it shows how Ghalib uses short, plain words to express a heavy feeling without long explanation.

The fifth couplet says: Ishrate qatra hai dariya mein fana ho jana, dard ka hadd se guzarna hai dawa ho jana. This means that the drop's pleasure lies in disappearing into the river, and pain becomes its own cure when it crosses its limit. In simple words, small happiness grows when it joins something greater, and extreme pain sometimes ends itself by changing the person completely. These two ideas show Ghalib's philosophical side: he thinks about life's puzzles and gives answers that sound strange at first but feel true after thinking. Why Ghalib Feels Difficult, and How to Make Him Easy

Ghalib feels difficult for three reasons: he uses some old and Persian words, he often says the opposite of what he seems to say, and he packs a big idea into just two lines. A student who reads him quickly will miss the point, but a student who reads slowly and asks simple questions will find him enjoyable. The best habit is to keep a small dictionary habit: whenever a word is unknown, find its plain meaning first, because one unknown word can hide the whole couplet. Reading a couplet three times, once for sound, once for words, and once for meaning, also makes a big difference.

Another helpful trick is to compare Ghalib's idea with daily life, because his themes are universal even when his words are old. The endless wishes couplet, for example, matches the feeling of a student who wants a new phone, then a better phone, and still feels unsatisfied. When students connect a couplet to their own experience, they remember it longer and can explain it better in exams. Teachers also advise students to learn a few couplets by heart, since quoting one correct couplet in an answer shows real understanding and confidence.

How to Write Answers on Ghalib

A strong exam answer on Ghalib begins with the couplet written correctly, followed by its plain meaning in the student's own words. Then the student should explain the image or idea in the couplet, such as endless wishes or a heart talking to itself, and finish with the lesson or message the poet gives. This three-part answer, meaning plus image plus lesson, is short, clear and complete, and it works for almost every couplet question. Students should avoid writing long, memorised paragraphs that do not match the couplet asked in the paper.

Marks also improve when the answer uses simple, correct language and mentions Ghalib's style briefly, such as his wit or his deep thinking about life. If the question asks for tashreeh, the student should explain both lines separately and then give the combined meaning, because that shows careful reading. Practising one couplet every day, writing its meaning in a notebook, and revising it weekly is enough to build confidence over a term. 5 key points you must know

  • Ghalib is Delhi's most quoted Urdu poet, famous for wit, wordplay and deep thoughts about life.
  • His endless-wishes couplet teaches that desires never end, so contentment is also wisdom.
  • The heart couplet shows Ghalib talking to himself, turning sadness into a conversation.
  • Read a couplet three times: once for sound, once for words, and once for meaning.
  • A good answer gives the plain meaning, the image, and the lesson in the student's own words.

Frequently asked questions

Why do students find Ghalib difficult?

Students find Ghalib difficult because he uses some old and Persian words, says things indirectly, and packs a big idea into just two lines. His couplets often work like riddles, so a quick reading misses the meaning. Reading slowly, learning unknown words first, and finding the image in the couplet makes him much easier and even enjoyable.

What is the meaning of Hazaaron khwahishen aisi?

This famous couplet means the poet had thousands of wishes, each so intense that it felt like life could leave him, and yet even after many wishes were fulfilled, his desires still seemed too few. It teaches a truth about human nature: wishes never end, and chasing every wish without contentment leaves a person feeling incomplete, so satisfaction is a form of wisdom.

What does Dil-e-nadan tujhe hua kya hai mean?

In this couplet the poet speaks to his own innocent heart and asks what has happened to it, and what medicine can cure its pain. It is a picture of a person talking to himself about sadness, and it shows Ghalib's style of turning a feeling into a conversation. The couplet is famous because it expresses inner pain in a simple, direct and memorable way.

How can I memorise Ghalib couplets easily?

Memorising becomes easy when you first understand the meaning, because a couplet with a clear picture stays in memory longer than one learnt by repetition alone. Read the couplet aloud, write its plain meaning beside it, and connect it with a daily-life example. Revising one couplet a day and repeating it after a few days builds lasting memory without stress.

What should a tashreeh answer on Ghalib include?

A tashreeh answer should include the couplet written correctly, its plain meaning in your own words, an explanation of both lines separately, and the combined message or lesson. Mentioning the image, such as the heart or endless wishes, and a brief note on Ghalib's style makes the answer stronger. Keep the language simple and clear rather than copying long memorised paragraphs.
