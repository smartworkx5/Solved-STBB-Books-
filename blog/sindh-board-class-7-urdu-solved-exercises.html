Sindh Class 7 Urdu Solved Exercises

By Tasleem Sanjrani · SolvedBooks.pk · 656 words · 3 min read

507Solved Exercises

Class 7 Urdu builds the reading and writing skills that every later Urdu paper depends on. The Sindh Board book mixes lessons, poetry, grammar and composition — and the students who score well are the ones who practise all four, not just the lessons. These solved exercises cover the whole book, and this guide shows how to work through it.

Read the lesson, then write the answers yourself

Open the Class 7 Urdu book page and read the lesson once slowly, for meaning. Then try every exercise question in writing before checking the solution — an answer you attempt yourself stays in memory far longer than one you only read.

For poetry, learn the central idea of each poem first, then the difficult words, then the explanation. A student who knows what the poet is saying can answer any question on the poem; a student who only memorised lines cannot.

Keep grammar and vocabulary notebooks

Urdu grammar — ism, fail, harf, jumlay, and the rules of writing — is tested in short questions that reward exact knowledge. Keep a grammar notebook with one rule per page and three of your own examples under each rule, and revise it every week.

For vocabulary, note every difficult word in the lesson with its meaning and one sentence of your own. Words learned from the book are the words the paper asks about — and using them in your own sentences fixes them in memory.

Practise mazmoon, khat and darkhwast regularly

Writing — mazmoon, khat, darkhwast — is the part of the paper students leave until the last week, and it shows in the result. Write one piece every week from the solved exercises, in full, by hand. Learn the format first: heading, address, salutation, body in paragraphs, closing.

After writing, compare your piece with the solved version for ideas and order, then correct your own spelling and grammar. Neat handwriting and clear paragraphs matter: an examiner marks what is easy to read.

Revise with the notes and MCQs

In the final weeks, revise the chapter notes on the Notes page — one chapter a day, in order — and test yourself on the solved exercises with the book closed. Whatever you cannot answer goes into a short revision list for the last days.

Finish with the MCQs practice page. Choose the Class 7 Urdu book and practise with the correct answer and explanation shown for every question — it catches the small grammar and vocabulary facts that long-answer revision misses.

5 key points you must know

  • Attempt every exercise question in writing before checking the solved answer.
  • Learn the central idea of each poem before memorising its lines.
  • Keep a grammar notebook — one rule per page, three of your own examples under each.
  • Write one mazmoon, khat or darkhwast every week, in full and by hand.
  • Revise one chapter a day from the notes in the final weeks and test yourself with the book closed.
