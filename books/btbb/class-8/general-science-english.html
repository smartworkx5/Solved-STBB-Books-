Sample solved questions

Ecology is the study of the relationships between living organisms and their environment. The word ecology was coined by Ernst Haeckel . The term ecosystem was coined by Arthur George Tansley in 1935 . Biogeochemical Cycles A biogeochemical cycle is the pathway through which elements (such as carbon, oxygen, nitrogen and water) circulate between living organisms and the non-living environment. Through these cycles the essential elements are used again and again by organisms. The Oxygen Cycle In the oxygen cycle, oxygen circulates between the atmosphere, living organisms and the earth. Plants release oxygen into the atmosphere during photosynthesis . Animals and plants use this oxygen for respiration and release carbon dioxide, which plants again use for photosynthesis. In this way the amount of oxygen in the atmosphere stays balanced. Resources of an Ecosystem Biotic resources — the living organisms: producers, consumers and decomposers. Abiotic resources — the non-living components such as sunlight, air, water, soil, minerals and temperature, which organisms need to live and grow.

Sample MCQs with answers

  1. Who coined the term 'ecosystem' and in which year?

    Answer: Arthur George Tansley in 1935

  2. A biogeochemical cycle is the pathway through which elements circulate between:

    Answer: Living organisms and the non-living environment

  3. Which elements are given as examples of elements moved by biogeochemical cycles?

    Answer: Carbon, oxygen, nitrogen and water

  4. During photosynthesis, green plants release which gas into the atmosphere?

    Answer: Oxygen

  5. In the oxygen cycle, the carbon dioxide released by respiration is used again by plants for:

    Answer: Photosynthesis
