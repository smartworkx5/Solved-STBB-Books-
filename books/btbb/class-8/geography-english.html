Sample solved questions

There are five oceans on the Earth's surface: the Pacific Ocean, the Atlantic Ocean, the Indian Ocean, the Antarctic (Southern) Ocean and the Arctic Ocean. An ocean is an excessively vast and deep body of saltish water. The oceans and seas hold 97 percent of the world's total water. The Three Largest Oceans Pacific Ocean — the largest ocean, with an area of 168 million sq. km, covering 45% of the world's oceanic surface. It is also the deepest ocean, with an average depth of 4,200 metres. The Mariana Trench in it, about 11 km deep, is the deepest place on the ocean floor. Atlantic Ocean — the second largest ocean, covering 29% of the world's oceanic surface, with an area of 85 million sq. km and an average depth of 3,650 metres. Its deepest point is the Puerto Rico Trench, 8,648 metres deep, and it lies in the earthquake-active zones of the world. Indian Ocean — the third largest ocean, with an area of 70 million sq. km, which is 20% of the world's oceanic surface. Its average depth is 3,900 metres, it extends mostly in the Southern Hemisphere, and its deepest point is the Java Trench, 7,400 metres deep. The Arctic Ocean is the smallest ocean of the world; in this book its area is given as 15 million sq. km. The Suez Canal connects the Red Sea with the Mediterranean Sea.
