Sample solved questions

Muslims and Hindus had jointly taken part in the War of Independence of 1857 , but the British placed the entire blame on the Muslims and took revengeful action against them. As a result, the Muslims of India were devastated politically, socially and economically ; their political dominance came to an end and they were reduced to the status of a defeated nation. Their educational condition also collapsed. According to William Hunter's report , of 2,141 government recruits in 1871 only 92 were Muslims , and in 1870 only 17 of the 517 graduates of the Bombay, Madras and Calcutta universities were Muslims. The East India Company monopolised trade and destroyed local industries, and Muslim agricultural estates were confiscated on a large scale — in Bombay province alone 20,000 estates were seized. In his pamphlet Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind (1858), Sir Syed Ahmed Khan argued that the real causes of the revolt were the British government's ignorance of the conditions of its subjects and laws that did not match Indian traditions. He stressed that Hindus and Muslims had both taken part in the war, so blaming Muslims alone was unjust.

How many Muslim agricultural estates were seized in Bombay province alone after 1857?
