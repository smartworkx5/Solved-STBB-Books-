Sample solved questions

The history of computers can be divided into five generations . The idea of the computer began with people who wanted an easier way to calculate numbers. With time, computers changed from huge, slow machines into the fast, intelligent machines we use today. First and Second Generations First generation (1942) — computers were huge in size, slow, unreliable and expensive. They used vacuum tubes . The ENIAC was 30 x 50 feet in size and 30 tons in weight. Second generation (1955) — came with the invention of the transistor . Transistors were faster, more reliable, smaller and cheaper than vacuum tubes. Example: IBM 1401 . Third and Fourth Generations Third generation (1964) — used Integrated Circuits (ICs) , made of interconnected electronic components. They gave higher processing speeds, greater storage capacity and lower prices. Example: IBM 360 series . Fourth generation — the modern computers we use nowadays. The microprocessor was introduced in this generation; these computers use very complex circuits on a microprocessor chip. Example: Apple I . Fifth Generation Fifth-generation computers are highly complex and are based on Artificial Intelligence (AI) technology. They are reliable, intelligent machines and include expert systems, game playing, robotics, natural language understanding and neural networks.
