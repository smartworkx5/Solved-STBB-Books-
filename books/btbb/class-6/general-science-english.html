Sample solved questions

A cell is the basic unit of structure and function of living things. All living things are made up of cells. Cells are very small and cannot be seen with the naked eye, so we study them with a microscope . The word cell means a small room or a bounded space. Both plant and animal cells have a cell membrane, cytoplasm and a nucleus. A group of similar cells together makes a tissue. Who Gave the Name Cell? The term cell was introduced in 1665 by the British scientist Robert Hooke . He saw a thin slice of cork under a microscope. He observed honeycomb-like structures and called them cells. Key Points A cell is the smallest unit of structure and function in living things. Cells are seen with a microscope, not with the naked eye. Robert Hooke introduced the term cell in 1665.

Sample MCQs with answers

  1. What is a cell?

    Answer: The basic unit of structure and function of living things

  2. Why do we need a microscope to study cells?

    Answer: Cells are very small and cannot be seen with the naked eye

  3. The word 'cell' means:

    Answer: A small room or a bounded space

  4. Which three parts are present in both plant and animal cells?

    Answer: Cell membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus

  5. A group of similar cells together makes a:

    Answer: Tissue
