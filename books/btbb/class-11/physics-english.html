Sample solved questions

A physical quantity is anything that can be measured: length, mass, time, temperature, force and so on. Every measurement of a physical quantity is written as a number together with a unit , for example 3.51 m. Estimation means finding the approximate value (the order of magnitude ) of a quantity without an exact measurement, using known reference values and simple reasoning. Estimates are rounded to one significant figure and are useful for checking whether a calculated result is reasonable. Estimating the Base Quantities Length is estimated by comparing with a metre rule or with the known height of a person. Mass is estimated by comparing with standard masses we handle daily, such as a 1 kg packet. Time is estimated by counting a regular event, such as a pendulum swing or a heartbeat. Worked Example: Your Age in Seconds A pupil of age 17 years: convert this age into seconds. Step 1: 17 years x 365 = 6205 days. Step 2: 6205 days x 24 = 148,920 hours. Step 3: 148,920 x 3600 = 536,112,000 s. Answer: about 5.36 x 10 8 s (leap years add roughly one extra day per four years). Worked Example: Distance Covered by Light in One Year Light travels at 3 x 10 8 m s -1 . How far does it travel in one year? This distance is called one light year . Step 1: Time in one year = 365 x 24 x 3600 = 3.15 x 10 7 s. Step 2: Distance = speed x time = 3 x 10 8 x 3.15 x 10 7 . Answer: about 9.5 x 10 15 m. A light year is a unit of distance, not of time.
