Sample solved questions

The cell is the structural and functional unit of living organisms. Some organisms are made of only one cell ( unicellular ), such as Euglena , Amoeba , Paramecium and Plasmodium , while others are made of many cells ( multicellular ), such as plants and animals. The study of the cell is called cell biology or cytology . An adult human contains more than 37.2 trillion cells. 1.1 Properties of Life Because cells are the basic unit of life, they carry out all the fundamental functions of life: Movement — Change in shape and position of a body part. Cells use cilia, flagella, pseudopodia and contractile proteins, with energy from mitochondria. Respiration — Breakdown of glucose and other nutrients to release energy in the form of ATP . Homeostasis — Maintaining a stable internal environment, such as pH, temperature and ion concentration, helped by transport proteins in the plasma membrane. Growth — Increase in size and number of cells by cell division; DNA controls growth and ribosomes synthesize protein for growth. Reproduction — Producing offspring of their own kind and passing on genetic material; binary fission, mitosis and meiosis make genetic continuity possible. Excretion — Removal of metabolic wastes such as CO₂ and urea. Nutrition — Obtaining food: plant cells absorb minerals and make food by photosynthesis; animal cells absorb digested nutrients from the blood stream. 1.2 Cell Theory All organisms are composed of one or many cells. The cell is the structural and functional unit of life. All cells come from parent (pre-existing) cells. The cell contains hereditary information, transferred from parental cells to daughter cells.     Answer: Cell biology or cytology

    Answer: 37.2 trillion cells

  4. Which structures do cells use for movement, with energy supplied by mitochondria?

    Answer: Cilia, flagella, pseudopodia and contractile proteins

    Answer: Glucose and other nutrients are broken down to release energy in the form of ATP
