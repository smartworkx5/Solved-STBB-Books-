How We Know About the Past Historians learn about ancient times from two kinds of sources. Primary sources are the original materials from the time of the event itself, such as inscriptions , coins , tools , weapons , buildings and official records; they give first-hand evidence. Secondary sources are written later by people who were not present, such as history books based on primary sources. Historians first study primary sources and then use secondary sources to understand and explain the past. The Land and the Beginning Mesopotamian Civilization grew between the rivers Tigris and Euphrates in present-day Iraq. It started in about 4500 BCE . The earliest people there were the Sumerians , who built city-states. Before the Babylonian Empire, the Akkadian Empire existed in Mesopotamia; founded by Sargon , it is considered the first empire of Mesopotamia. Later Hammurabi built the Babylonian Empire. The Code of Hammurabi The Code of Hammurabi was a set of 282 laws made by Hammurabi, the famous king of Babylon. The laws were carved on a large stone pillar so that everyone could see them. They covered trade, property, family, wages and punishments. Their main purpose was that people may respect each other and justice may prevail in society. It is one of the earliest written law codes of the world. Trade, Architecture and Occupations Trade: Mesopotamians exported grain, cloth and handicrafts and imported wood, stone, metals and ivory, using rivers and land routes. Architecture: They built houses and palaces of mud-bricks and huge temple towers called ziggurats ; their cities had planned streets. Occupations: Most people were farmers who grew wheat, barley and dates with the help of irrigation canals; others were craftsmen, weavers, potters, metal workers, traders, priests…
