Sample solved questions

Producers and the Sun The Sun is the main (ultimate) source of energy for life on the Earth. Green plants, called producers , trap the Sun's energy as chemical energy by photosynthesis and make food. All consumers — herbivores, carnivores and decomposers — depend directly or indirectly on this food. Trophic Levels The feeding level of an organism in an ecosystem is called its trophic level . Producers form the first trophic level, primary consumers (herbivores) the second, secondary consumers the third, and so on. Energy flows from producers to consumers through food chains and food webs. Food Chain and Food Web A food chain is a single, straight sequence showing who eats whom, e.g. Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake. A food web is the network formed when all the food chains of an ecosystem are joined together; in a food web one organism may be eaten by several others and may eat several kinds of food, so the feeding relationships are interlinked and more stable. Ecological Pyramids An ecological pyramid is the graphical representation of the trophic relationship of organisms in an ecosystem. It shows the number of organisms, biomass or energy at each trophic level. Producers occupy the base of the pyramid because they are the first trophic level: they make their own food by trapping the Sun's energy and all other organisms depend on them. They are the most numerous and contain the greatest amount of energy and biomass, while only a part of the energy passes to each next level, so the pyramid is broad at the base and narrows towards the apex. Key Points The Sun is the ultimate source of energy; producers trap it as chemical energy. A trophic level is the feeding level of an organism in an ecosystem.
