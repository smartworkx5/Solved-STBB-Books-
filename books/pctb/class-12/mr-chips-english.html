Note on the Book's Exercises The book contains no end-of-chapter question sets. Its only question set is the end-of-book EXERCISES (book p. 50), solved here in full (13 questions; Q. 13 has 6 brief notes). Earlier comprehension selections (Chapters 1-18) are retained. Who was Mr. Chips? Where did he teach? Mr. Chips , whose real name was Arthur Chipping , was a schoolmaster. He taught Latin and Greek at Brookfield , an English public school. He joined Brookfield in 1870 as a young man of twenty-two. He remained connected with the school for the rest of his long life. Why was Chips called 'Mr. Chips'? The boys shortened his name 'Chipping' to ' Chips ' as a nickname. At first it was used teasingly, but later it became a title of affection. Old boys, colleagues and even the Head called him Chips. The name stuck to him for more than sixty years. What kind of teacher was Chips in his early years? In his early years Chips was a strict but dull teacher. He taught by old methods and the boys played tricks on him. He was shy and lacked confidence in dealing with boys. Yet he was kind at heart, and the boys gradually grew fond of him.
