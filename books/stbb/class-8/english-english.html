Story Summary Raheel and Basit were close friends who studied in the same school. They played outside together - cricket, fishing and long walks in the forest. On weekends they went to the nearby canal for fishing. Basit usually caught more fish than Raheel and sometimes gave fish to Raheel because Raheel often failed to catch any. One day the two nature lovers wandered deep into the dense, dangerous forest. They followed whatever caught their attention, lost their way, and worried because their parents and the villagers had warned them about dangerous beasts living there. Then a huge black bear came towards them. Raheel, who was good at climbing trees, climbed a tree to save himself and told Basit to run. Basit did not know how to climb. He was paralyzed with fear and shocked that his friend would abandon him. He lay flat on the ground and stopped breathing so the bear would think he was dead. The bear smelled Basit, took him for dead and walked away without harming him. Raheel came down and asked what the bear had done. Basit said the bear had whispered in his ear: do not trust a friend who leaves his friend at the time of danger. In fact bears cannot speak - the bear had only smelled Basit. Basit invented the reply to teach Raheel a lesson. Raheel had seen the way out from the top of the tree, so both friends reached home safely.
