Sample solved questions

What is gaseous exchange? Gaseous exchange means the exchange of gases like oxygen and carbon dioxide between an organism and the environment. This exchange takes place in two life processes: in photosynthesis as well as in respiration. How do plants exchange gases? Plants carry out gaseous exchange through the root, the stomata and the stem; all these parts take part in gaseous exchange. Leaves: mainly take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen through the stomata. Stem: exchanges gases through lenticels. Root: the root also exchanges gases. Stomata and guard cells A stoma is a small pore in a leaf. Each stoma is surrounded by two guard cells. The guard cells help in opening and closing the stoma. Why are stomata open during the daytime? During the day carbon dioxide is needed for photosynthesis, so the stomata stay open so that carbon dioxide can enter and oxygen can leave. Other organisms and the body surface Some organisms exchange gases through the whole body surface without special organs, e.g. the earthworm, the frog (through its skin), and single-celled organisms like Amoeba. Experiment: carbon dioxide in respiration Germinating seeds are placed in a flask and the air passing from it is passed through lime water → the lime water turns milky → this proves that carbon dioxide is released during respiration.

Sample MCQs with answers

  1. What does gaseous exchange mean?

    Answer: The exchange of gases like oxygen and carbon dioxide between an organism and the environment

  2. In which two life processes does gaseous exchange take place?

    Answer: In photosynthesis as well as in respiration

  3. Through which parts do plants carry out gaseous exchange?

    Answer: The root, the stomata and the stem — all of these

  4. What do leaves mainly do during gaseous exchange?

    Answer: They take in carbon dioxide and release oxygen through the stomata

  5. Through which structures does the stem exchange gases?

    Answer: Through lenticels
