Sample solved questions

The representative elements are the s-block and p-block elements of groups IA to VIIIA. Their properties change in regular trends down a group and across a period, and these trends can be explained from atomic size, shielding and effective nuclear charge. Atomic Radii Atomic radii of the alkali metals increase regularly from Li to Cs, because a new electron shell is added at each step and shielding increases. A famous exception in group IIIA is gallium: gallium has a smaller atomic radius than aluminium although it lies below it, because gallium comes after the first transition series and its ten 3d electrons shield the nuclear charge poorly. The increased effective nuclear charge pulls the outer electrons closer (d-block contraction), so gallium is slightly smaller than aluminium. Ionization Energy Ionization energy decreases from top to bottom in the s-block elements. Down the group a new shell is added at each step, so the valence electron is farther from the nucleus and is better shielded by the inner electrons. The effective nuclear attraction on it therefore decreases, and less energy is needed to remove it. In group IIIA the trend is irregular: ionization energy falls from B to Al, then rises slightly at Ga, falls at In and rises again at Tl. The rises at Ga and Tl are due to the poor shielding of the filled d and f electrons (d-block and f-block contractions), which increases the effective nuclear charge. The overall trend is a decrease with irregular increases at gallium and thallium. Electronegativity Electronegativities of the alkali metals decrease from Li to Cs (Li 1.0 to Cs 0.7). Down the group the atomic size increases and the valence electron is held less tightly because of greater distance and shielding, so the atom attracts a shared pair of electrons…

Sample MCQs with answers

Atomic radii of the alkali metals change from Li to Cs in which way?

Answer: They increase regularly

Gallium has a smaller atomic radius than aluminium although gallium lies below it. Why?

Answer: The ten 3d electrons of gallium shield the nuclear charge poorly, so effective nuclear charge increases

Ionization energy in the s-block elements generally changes down a group by:

Answer: Decreasing from top to bottom

In group IIIA, the ionization energy trend is irregular because it rises slightly at:

Answer: Gallium and thallium

Electronegativity of the alkali metals changes from Li (1.0) to Cs (0.7) by:

Answer: Decreasing
