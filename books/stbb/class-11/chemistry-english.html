Sample solved questions

Stoichiometry is the study of the quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical reaction using a balanced chemical equation. The central counting unit of stoichiometry is the mole, which links the mass of a substance with the number of its particles. The Mole A mole is the gram atomic mass, gram molecular mass or gram formula mass of a substance, and it contains 6.02 x 10^23 particles (atoms, molecules or ions). For example, one mole of carbon atoms weighs 12g and contains 6.02 x 10^23 atoms, while one mole of water weighs 18g and contains 6.02 x 10^23 molecules. Avogadro's Number Avogadro's number is the number of particles present in one mole of any substance, equal to 6.02 x 10^23 and denoted by NA. It is a constant: it does not change with temperature. With its help the mole becomes a counting unit like a dozen: just as one dozen means 12 objects, one mole means 6.02 x 10^23 particles. The mole and Avogadro's number are interrelated because the mole is defined through it: one mole of every substance, whatever its mass in grams, always contains Avogadro's number of particles. Number of moles = Number of particles / 6.02 x 10^23 Molar Volume Molar volume is the volume occupied by one mole of any gas at STP (273K and 1 atm pressure); for all ideal gases its value is 22.4 dm3. So 44.8 dm3 of O2 at STP = 44.8/22.4 = 2 moles = 2 x 6.02 x 10^23 = 12.04 x 10^23 molecules. Sample MCQs with answers

  1. What does one mole of carbon atoms weigh and how many atoms does it contain?

    Answer: 6.02 x 10^23 atoms and 12 g

  2. A mole is best defined as:

    Answer: The gram atomic mass, gram molecular mass or gram formula mass of a substance, containing 6.02 x 10^23 particles

  3. Avogadro's number, denoted by NA, is equal to:

    Answer: 6.02 x 10^23

  4. Avogadro's number is a constant because it does not change with:

    Answer: Temperature

  5. One mole of water weighs 18 g and contains:

    Answer: 6.02 x 10^23 molecules
