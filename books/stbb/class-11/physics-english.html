Physical Quantities A physical quantity is a quantity that can be measured. Some quantities have their own base units and are called fundamental quantities. Mass, time and luminous intensity (luminosity) are fundamental quantities in this sense. Supplementary Quantities Besides the fundamental quantities there are supplementary quantities. The plane angle and the solid angle are supplementary quantities. Plane angle — a supplementary quantity; its unit is the radian Solid angle — the three-dimensional angle subtended at a point; a supplementary quantity SI Units Named in This Unit Quantity SI Unit Solid angle steradian (sr) Time second (s) Mass kilogram (kg) Luminous intensity candela (cd) The second, kilogram and candela are the units of time, mass and luminous intensity respectively, while the steradian belongs to the solid angle alone. Time — the Same Unit in All Systems Length is measured in metre, centimetre or foot and mass in kilogram, gram or pound in different systems, so their units change from system to system. Time is different: the second is used as the unit of time in the CGS, MKS and FPS systems alike, so time is the quantity having the same unit in all systems of units. Sample MCQs with answers

  1. Which of the following is a supplementary quantity?

    Answer: Solid angle

  2. What is the SI unit of solid angle?

    Answer: Steradian

  3. The candela (cd) is the SI unit of which quantity?

    Answer: Luminous intensity

  4. Which quantity has the same unit in the CGS, MKS and FPS systems alike?

    Answer: Time

  5. Which quantity has a unit but no dimensions?

    Answer: Plane angle
