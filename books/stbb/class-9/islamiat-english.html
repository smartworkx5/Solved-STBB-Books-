This is a textbook lesson of the book. There is no written exercise for it in the book; these notes give the facts printed in the book's own lesson. Introduction to the Holy Quran The literal meaning of the word "Quran" is: to read, and one of its meanings is also "a book that is read". Its terminological meaning is: the Word of Allah Almighty which was revealed to Hazrat Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, the Seal of the Prophets (peace and blessings of Allah be upon him and his family and companions) through Hazrat Jibreel (peace be upon him) and was written in the Mushafs (bound copies). The Holy Quran has 30 Paras, 114 Surahs, 558 Ruku and 6236 Ayats. The first Surah is "Al-Fatiha" and the last Surah is "An-Nas". The largest Surah is "Al-Baqarah" and the smallest Surah is "Al-Kawthar". The great Ayat that describes the greatness and majesty of Allah Almighty is "Ayat-ul-Kursi". Every Surah consists of at least three Ayats, and every Surah has a special content and subject. Except for Surah At-Tawbah, Bismillah-ir-Rahman-ir-Raheem is written at the beginning of every Surah. The literal meaning of "Ayat" is: a sign or mark. In terminology, a part of the Holy Quran is called an Ayat, and at the end of that part there is a sign of a circle. Names of the Holy Quran The real name of the Holy Quran is "Al-Quran" itself, but the scholars have counted many other names besides it, some of the famous names among which are: Al-Quran: This is the original and personal name of the Holy Word. Al-Furqan: The book that distinguishes between truth and falsehood. Az-Zikr: The book of advice. Al-Kitab: Specifically the Book of Allah Almighty, that is, the Holy Quran.
