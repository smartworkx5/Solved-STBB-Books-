Sample solved questions

Stoichiometry is the study of the quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a chemical reaction, using a balanced chemical equation. The central counting unit of stoichiometry is the mole, which links the mass of a substance with the number of its particles. The Mole A mole is the gram atomic mass, gram molecular mass or gram formula mass of a substance, and it contains 6.023 x 10^23 particles (atoms, molecules or ions). For example, one mole of carbon atoms weighs 12 g and contains 6.023 x 10^23 atoms, while one mole of water weighs 18 g and contains 6.023 x 10^23 molecules. Avogadro's Number Avogadro's number is the number of particles present in one mole of any substance, equal to 6.023 x 10^23 and written as NA. It is a constant: it does not change with temperature. With its help the mole becomes a counting unit like a dozen: just as one dozen means 12 objects, one mole means 6.023 x 10^23 particles. Number of moles = Number of particles / 6.023 x 10^23 Number of moles = Mass in grams / Molar mass Gram Atom A gram atom is the amount of an element whose mass in grams equals its atomic mass, and it contains Avogadro's number of atoms. The concept is useful because it lets chemists weigh out a known number of atoms on an ordinary balance and use that fixed number in stoichiometric calculations. Molar Volume Molar volume is the volume occupied by one mole of a gas at STP (273 K and 760 mm Hg), equal to 22.4 dm^3 for every gas. For example, the molar volume of SO2 gas at STP is 22.4 dm^3, and 67.2 dm^3 of CO2 at STP contains 3 moles, more than 11.2 dm^3 of H2, 44.8 dm^3 of N2 or 22.4 dm^3 of O2. Sample MCQs with answers

  1. Stoichiometry is the study of:

    Answer: The quantitative relationships between reactants and products in a chemical reaction

  2. One mole of any substance always contains:

    Answer: 6.023 x 10^23 particles

  3. The mass of one mole of water (H2O) is:

    Answer: 18 g

  4. Avogadro's number (NA):

    Answer: Is a constant, 6.023 x 10^23

  5. How many moles are present in 75 g of calcium (atomic mass of Ca = 40)?

    Answer: 1.875 mol
